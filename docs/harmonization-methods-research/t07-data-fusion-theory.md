# Multi-sensor data fusion: the general theory and what applies to detections

## Summary

Multi-sensor data fusion is the general theory of combining several imperfect sensors that observe the same thing. It covers weighting redundant measurements, tracking a hidden state through time, combining evidence or votes, estimating each sensor's error without a reference, and estimating how many events all sensors missed. Most of it assumes continuous measurements with known, independent errors. The team has none of these: it has point detections ("fire here"), already thresholded by each agency's algorithm, from two families of near-identical instruments (MODIS on Terra and Aqua; VIIRS on Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21), with no record of overpasses that saw nothing. The closest fire uses found were triple collocation of burned-area products (Brennan et al. 2019) and an ensemble Kalman filter in a fire-spread model (Mandel et al. 2008); no use on hotspot counts was found. The family's main value here is vocabulary and caveats; only capture-recapture and simple averaging are cheap enough to try, and neither removes the measured MODIS-VIIRS problems.

## How each would map onto MODIS and VIIRS hotspots

**Taxonomy.** Hotspots are decision-level outputs plus a few features (brightness temperature, fire radiative power, pixel size, confidence). Data-level fusion of radiances is impossible without swath data. The five sensors are competitive (same quantity) with a complementary part (different overpass times, 1 km against 375 m pixels). This costs nothing and frames the write-up.

**Inverse-variance weighting.** Steps: scale each sensor to one target, then average the sensors that have data in a month with weights 1/(relative scatter)^2. The VIIRS satellites agree within a few percent (NOAA-20 0.99, NOAA-21 0.97 of Suomi NPP), so weights barely differ from equal, and the team's daily-mean rule is already an equal-weight version. Their errors are surely correlated, so the fused uncertainty must not be reported below that of one satellite (the covariance-intersection lesson, a sentence, not code). For MODIS against VIIRS the result is dominated by the scale-factor error (monthly scatter 14-30%). It fixes nothing measured; it justifies the averaging. Hours.

**Kalman filter or state-space model.** Steps: latent monthly activity per area; each sensor observes it through its own gain and noise; Terra's daytime gain drifts (0.46 of expected in 2026); sensors enter and leave (NOAA-21 in 2023, Suomi NPP lost 2 November 2026, MODIS ending 2027). This is the principled form of "different sensors, different periods". But counts are heavy-tailed and mostly zero in small areas (91% of fire-season months empty at 10 km radius), so the Gaussian model fails, and gains and variances would be fitted from the same data. It cannot restore months where MODIS saw nothing. Days or more.

**Union of cell-days (voting with k = 1).** Steps: an active cell-day counts if any sensor flagged it. The team measured that the union steps up 1.24 and 1.37 times when the second and third VIIRS satellites join, and more in quiet years. So a union is only stable over time if the set of sensors is held fixed; otherwise it creates steps at April 2018 and March 2023. The optimal weighted vote is unusable: no false-alarm or detection rates exist. Hours.

**Capture-recapture on cell-days.** This is the one truth-free method here that needs only detections. Steps: for an area-month, count cell-days flagged by sensor A only, B only and both; compute Lincoln-Petersen with a bootstrap interval. Illustration (my arithmetic on the team's 1.24 union, assuming equal catches and independence): overlap 0.76n, total 1.32n, each VIIRS satellite about 76% complete. The independence assumption is doubtful: identical instruments miss the same small, cool fires, so the true missing share is larger. Overpasses about 50 minutes apart also mean the "population" of burning cells is not closed. For MODIS against VIIRS, Terra's different overpass time should give MODIS-only cell-days; the team has not measured that overlap, so how nested the detections are is unknown. At areas under about 50 km the overlaps are too few for an estimate (Tsay & Chao: only a bound at low overlap). It estimates "what we cannot see"; it does not remove the MODIS-VIIRS gap. Hours for two lists; a day for log-linear models with three or more.

## Not applicable, and why

- Dempster-Shafer: the belief masses would need omission rates the data cannot give, and the output is a per-event belief, not a count.
- Triple collocation and extended collocation: needs three systems with independent errors, but the five sensors form two correlated families; extended collocation requires each correlated pair to sit in "at least one data set triplet with fully independent errors" (Gruber 2016), which two families cannot satisfy; and zero months must be dropped, as Brennan et al. did, which drops most small-area months.
- Categorical triple collocation and Hui-Walter: need negatives; a cell-day without a hotspot cannot be told apart from one hidden by cloud or not overpassed; conditional independence fails.
- Occupancy models: need repeated visits with recorded non-detections.
- Particle and unscented Kalman filters: heavier versions of the Kalman approach.

## Unconfirmed

- Chair & Varshney 1986, McColl et al. 2014, Dawid & Skene 1979, MacKenzie et al. 2002, Gruber et al. 2016 (journal review): metadata confirmed through Crossref only; content not read. The description of the optimal vote is from secondary text.
- Chao et al. 2001 and Tsay & Chao 2001: abstracts only. Mandel et al. 2008: arXiv abstract only.
- Pierdicca et al.: read as the 2016 preprint; the published 2018 version was not opened.
- Not opened, and not relied on: Stoffelen 1998, Durrant-Whyte 1988 (metadata confirmed), Hui & Walter 1980, Dempster, Shafer, the Kalman and Rauch-Tung-Striebel smoother originals, Julier & Uhlmann (covariance intersection), Kam et al. 1992, the Chapman estimator, Chen et al. 2023 (land-cover triple collocation), and a 1981 aerial-survey capture-recapture paper (link returned 404).
- No search was possible for Dempster-Shafer or capture-recapture applied to satellite fire detections after the search budget ran out; a Crossref query found only a tunnel-fire sensor paper. "No hotspot application found" is therefore incomplete.
