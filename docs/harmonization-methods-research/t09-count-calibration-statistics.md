# Statistical calibration of count records when the measuring instrument changes

## Summary

This family of methods estimates a conversion between two instruments that count the same thing with different sensitivity, using a period when both ran. The conversion is then applied to the years in which only one instrument ran. The methods range from a single ratio of totals to generalised linear models (GLMs, regression models for non-normal data such as counts) and mixed models with random effects. The most developed practice comes from fisheries surveys that change vessel or gear, with smaller analogues in bird surveys, air-quality monitors and disease surveillance. No source opened for this topic applies these statistical methods to satellite fire hotspot counts. Fire systems do use overlap-fitted factors split by day and night (the team's r02 file), but those are documented as fitted factors rather than as count models with stated uncertainty. The fit to the team's problem is good for the core step (a multiplicative factor with honest uncertainty, split by day and night). It is weak for small areas, where MODIS records nothing in a third or more of the months in which VIIRS saw fire. No method in this family can fix the 2022–2026 orbit drift, because the drift years lie outside the stable overlap used for fitting.

## Methods

| Method | How it works | Where used | Data needed | Main assumption | Known failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ratio of sums (ratio estimator) | Factor = total of one instrument divided by total of the other, over the overlap. | Survey sampling; [Knottnerus and Scholtus 2019](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-001-x/2019003/article/00003-eng.htm) (abstract only) | Paired totals per month | One proportional factor | The standard variance formulas "may lead to serious underestimates when the sample size is small" (abstract). |
| Ordinary least squares (OLS) regression of target on source | Fits Y on X and minimises vertical errors only. | Allometry review, [Warton et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1464793106007007) | Paired values | X has no error | The slope is biased (flattened) when X is noisy. This matters for estimating the relationship, but not when the aim is to predict Y from a noisy X (Warton et al. 2006). |
| Major axis (MA), reduced or standardised major axis (RMA/SMA), Deming and York regression | Symmetric line fits that attribute error to both variables. Deming regression needs the ratio of the two error variances (lambda). | Instrument intercomparison, [Wu and Yu 2018](https://doi.org/10.5194/amt-11-1233-2018); PM2.5 monitor replacement, [Hsu et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1080/10962247.2015.1136362) | Paired values, and lambda for Deming | Error structure known or assumed | "An improper λ in DR can lead to a bias in both the slope and intercept" (Wu and Yu 2018). The MA/SMA slope is biased if X is sampled to have a large range (Warton et al. 2006). RMA is often misused where OLS fits the question better ([Smith 2009](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajpa.21090), abstract only). |
| Poisson, quasi-Poisson or negative binomial (NB) GLM with an offset | Models the count of one sensor with log(other sensor's count) as a fixed offset, so the intercept is the log factor. Quasi-Poisson and NB allow overdispersion (variance larger than the mean). | General count regression, [Zeileis et al. 2008](https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v027i08); harbor seal surveys, [Ver Hoef and Boveng 2007](https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/usdeptcommercepub/142/) (abstract only) | Monthly counts | The offset count is error-free | Quasi-Poisson and NB weight large and small counts differently and can give "striking differences" (Ver Hoef and Boveng 2007). In paired-tow data a quasi-likelihood dispersion term "did not completely account for the true variability" ([Cadigan and Dowden 2010](https://spo.nmfs.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/pdf-content/2010/1081/cadigan.pdf), p. 17, reporting an observation by Benoît). |
| Conditional binomial or beta-binomial GLMM (generalised linear mixed model) | Conditional on the pair total, one instrument's share is binomial with p = ρ/(1+ρ), where ρ is the relative efficiency. A random effect per pair, or a beta-binomial distribution, absorbs extra variation. | Fisheries paired tows: Cadigan and Dowden 2010; [Cadigan and Power 2011](https://waves-vagues.dfo-mpo.gc.ca/library-bibliotheque/343451.pdf); [Miller 2013](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjfas-2013-0136) (abstract only) | Paired counts per sampling unit | Both counts are taken of the same patch at the same time | The "commonly used over-dispersed binomial logistic regression model did not provide accurate statistical inferences" when density differed within pairs (Cadigan and Dowden 2010). Penalised quasi-likelihood fitting can bias ρ (same paper). |
| Covariate-dependent factor | log ρ is a linear function of a covariate (size class, time of day, season). | Length effects: Cadigan and Power 2011; [Thygesen et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/icesjms/fsy191). Diel (day versus night) effects: [Miller et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.fishres.2022.106565). Seasonal equations: Hsu et al. 2016 | The covariate in both periods | The covariate is observable when only one instrument runs | A pooled factor hid opposite size effects in redfish (Cadigan and Power 2011). |
| Instrument or protocol term in a hierarchical trend model | Adds an indicator for the new protocol inside a log-linear Poisson model of all counts. | Breeding Bird Survey (BBS), [Sauer et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1093/condor/duz009) | Many sites surveyed under both protocols | Protocol effect is constant | Users "will ignore the protocol ID" flag. Undocumented method changes bias trends. |
| Adoption-covariate counterfactual model | A mixed quasi-Poisson model with covariates for the share of new tests, checked against a stable reference series. | Foodborne disease surveillance, [Healy et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/ije/dyad133) (abstract only) | Adoption covariate and a reference series | The reference series is unaffected by the change | No parallel run was available, so the result rests on the model. |
| Hurdle and zero-inflated models | A hurdle model has two parts: whether the count is above zero, and how large it is if so. A zero-inflated model mixes a count distribution with extra zeros. | Zeileis et al. 2008 | Counts with many zeros | The zeros arise from a separable process | Many zeros do not by themselves mean zero inflation ([Warton 2005](https://doi.org/10.1002/env.702), title and metadata only). |
| Simulation prediction interval | Redraws the coefficients by parametric bootstrap, then draws a new count from the fitted NB distribution, and takes quantiles of the draws. | R package ciTools [add_pi.negbin](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/ciTools/html/add_pi.negbin.html) | A fitted GLM | The model is correct | The interval does not include uncertainty about which model is correct. |

## How each would map onto MODIS and VIIRS hotspots

All of these methods need only monthly counts by sensor and by day or night, which the team has. None needs cloud masks, swath coverage or records of overpasses that saw nothing. Any fit should use the stable-orbit overlap (February 2012 to December 2021, 116 months) after the Kamloops static source is removed, because a factor cannot remove a constant.

**Ratio of sums with year-block resampling.** Take Terra-plus-Aqua total ÷ Suomi NPP total, separately for day (0.34) and night (0.18), and get the interval by resampling whole years. The team has already done this. Effective independence is about ten years, not 116 months, so formulas that treat months as independent would understate the uncertainty. This method fixes the level difference. It does not fix the 15–30% single-month scatter, missing detections in small areas, or Terra drift. Effort: hours (already largely done).

**Poisson or NB GLM with an offset.** Fit MODIS_t ~ offset(log VIIRS_t) + day/night. With Poisson errors and only an intercept, the estimate equals the ratio of sums (a standard result, not taken from a source). The GLM adds a dispersion estimate and model-based intervals. NB may move the factor toward the quiet months (Ver Hoef and Boveng 2007), which matters because the team's quiet fire-season months run 25–50% above busy ones. Using VIIRS as the offset treats it as exact, while two identical VIIRS satellites differ by ±15–20% in a month. Effort: hours.

**Conditional binomial or beta-binomial share.** Model the MODIS share of the monthly MODIS+VIIRS total, with a random effect per month or a beta-binomial distribution, and day/night as a fixed effect. The share is bounded and keeps zero-MODIS months in the fit. The fisheries justification needs simultaneous pairs, and the team's "pair" is a month of overpasses at different hours (about 11:15 and 13:30 for MODIS against 12:40 and 02:10 for VIIRS), so its main advantage weakens. Effort: about a day including checks.

**Covariate-dependent factor.** Day/night is the covariate with direct precedent (Miller et al. 2023 found diel effects on relative efficiency for all but one of ten species). An activity covariate (the VIIRS count) is observable in both directions but had weak out-of-sample support in the team's tests. A covariate seen only by MODIS cannot be used after 2027. Effort: hours on top of a GLM.

**Hurdle model for small areas.** Model P(MODIS > 0 | VIIRS count) from the overlap, by radius class. This does not restore detections. It quantifies how often a converted value would be zero, which matters below about 50 km, where MODIS misses 33–50% of VIIRS fire months. Effort: a day.

**Prediction intervals.** Combine (1) the factor uncertainty, (2) month-level scatter (14–30%, depending on month size) and (3) Poisson noise of the new count, which dominates at small counts. Miller et al. 2023 note that one estimated factor applied to every year "induces correlation" between the calibrated values. The factor error therefore does not average out in a multi-year baseline median: the interval of 0.237–0.28 carries through to the whole converted history. Effort: hours.

**Pitfalls common to all.**
- *Extrapolation.* Factors fitted on the large-area box at 2012–2021 activity levels are being applied to small areas, quiet months and drift years. Hsu et al. 2016 found their adjusted monitor over-estimated above 10 µg/m³, although on only 15 points.
- *Selection effects.* Fitting only on months where one sensor exceeds a threshold distorts the fitted relation. The SMA analogue is in Warton et al. 2006. The count case is the team's own reasoning, not a sourced finding.

## Not applicable, and why

- **Deming or York regression on raw monthly counts.** These need lambda, which cannot be known for hotspot counts. They answer a symmetric "true relationship" question rather than a prediction question, and the team's data show a near-proportional relation that a ratio already captures.
- **Full log-Gaussian Cox process intercalibration (Thygesen et al. 2019).** It is built for size-class spectra from simultaneous hauls, and the team has no equivalent structure. It would take days or more.
- **Hierarchical Bayesian protocol-term model (Sauer et al. 2019).** It needs many sites under both protocols and Markov chain Monte Carlo fitting. It is days of work for little gain over a GLM.
- **Adoption-covariate counterfactual (Healy et al. 2024).** It is designed for cases with no overlap, but the team has ten overlap years.
- **Zero-inflated mixture.** Its "structural zero" class has no clear physical meaning separate from the MODIS detection floor. A hurdle model states the question more directly.

## Unconfirmed

- Read as abstract only: Knottnerus and Scholtus 2019; Ver Hoef and Boveng 2007; Healy et al. 2024; Miller 2013; Smith 2009. Warton 2005 was confirmed by title and metadata only.
- Warton et al. 2006 was read as the 2005 author manuscript. Volume 81 and the DOI were confirmed, but the page numbers were not.
- The Cadigan and Dowden (2010) report of overdispersion failing is second-hand: it is Benoît's observation cited in that paper.
- These leads were not opened: Pelletier (1998) paired-tow review; Sauer, Peterjohn and Link (1994); Link and Sauer (1999); Yin and Benoit (2022) DFO simulation; Cochran (1977); a syndromic-surveillance relative-risk use case; Atmosphere 9(5):191 (2018) on TEOM correction; arXiv 2404.05282; the FHWA counter-calibration guidance.
- Traffic counters: nothing usable was found.
- Fire applications: no statistical count-calibration study on hotspots was found. The web-search quota ran out during verification, so this rests on one researcher's searching plus the team's r01 and r02 files. It is not an exhaustive negative.
- All statements about how each method applies to MODIS and VIIRS are the reviewers' reasoning. No source tests these methods on fire data.
