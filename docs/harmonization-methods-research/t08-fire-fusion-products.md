# Multi-sensor fusion as practised for active fire

## Summary

Multi-sensor fusion for active fire means combining fire detections or fire radiative power (FRP, the heat a fire radiates, in megawatts) from several satellites into one product, usually by choosing a preferred sensor where it has data, rescaling a second sensor to it, or modelling the daily fire cycle between overpasses. It is well established for fire: emissions systems, fire-progression products and burned-area products all do it, and the main review of the field says that inter-comparisons of sensors aim "to derive transfer functions enabling data from multiple sensors to be combined into single time-series" (Wooster et al. 2021). Almost every published fusion outputs FRP, energy or emissions on a grid, not monthly hotspot counts, and no study found fuses MODIS (Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer) and VIIRS (Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite) hotspot counts. For the team's problem the literature helps most with combining the three VIIRS satellites that fly at the same time, and with the principle that a series must not change level when satellites are added or removed (Remy and Kaiser 2014). It does not solve the change of sensor mix over time by fusion alone; the people who make the products say that needs calibration over an overlap period (Giglio et al. 2025), and a maintained operational fusion system warns that adding or removing satellites "can introduce large variation in system performance" (NOAA Hazard Mapping System).

## Methods

| Method | How it works | Where used | Data needed | Main assumption | Known failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Priority switch | Use the better sensor's value where it has one; otherwise use a second sensor rescaled to it (weights 1 and 0). | GOES plus MODIS FRP (Li et al. 2019); GOES-R ABI plus VIIRS FRP (Li et al. 2022); Aqua MODIS plus Suomi NPP VIIRS FRP (Zheng et al. 2026) | Coincident observations to fit the rescaling; knowledge of when the better sensor looked | Rescaled second sensor is unbiased where the first is absent | Series changes character wherever the preferred sensor appears or drops out; none of the three papers tests continuity across the switch |
| Correction before fusion | Remove same-sensor duplicates (under 8 s apart and over 30% footprint overlap), sum VIIRS pixels inside each MODIS footprint, correct MODIS FRP for view angle with a quadratic, correct VIIRS for cloud, then priority-switch. | Zheng et al. 2026 | Second-level time stamps, footprints, cloud fraction | Coefficients fitted on Aqua apply to the period studied | Authors say Aqua coefficients should not be transferred to Terra without recalibration; view-angle step "does not alter the location or number of MODIS fire detections" |
| Observed-area weighting plus Kalman filter | Grid detections weighted by observed pixel area; plain daily mean of observations; a scalar Kalman filter (a recursive weighted average) carries the last estimate forward through cloud gaps, with its error inflated threefold. | GFAS version 1.0 (Kaiser et al. 2012) | Every observation including clear "no fire" pixels; cloud mask | Fire persists from day to day | Needs observed area, which point hotspot rows do not record |
| Single-satellite to constellation regression | Per region, regress daily FRP from one satellite on the merged all-satellite value over years when all flew; apply it when only one flies. | Terra-only 2000 to 2002 and single-satellite days in GFAS (Remy and Kaiser 2014) | Overlap years with all sensors; 400 or more positive grid cells per region | Single-to-merged relation is stable in time | Weaker for Terra (R² 0.5 to 0.8) than Aqua (0.85 to 1); boreal fires "much more irregular" |
| Diurnal-cycle correction | Fit the daily fire cycle from geostationary data, then scale polar-orbit snapshots by where their overpass falls on that cycle (Gaussian adjustment factor). | Himawari-8 plus VIIRS, eastern China (Zhang et al. 2020); Himawari-8 plus VIIRS, southeast Australia (Zheng et al. 2021); SEVIRI sampled at MODIS times (Andela et al. 2015) | Hourly geostationary fire data for the region | The cycle's shape is regionally stable | Andela et al. found good agreement only after aggregating to coarser space or time; Zhang et al. had no independent validation |
| Two simultaneous sensors combined per pixel | Per satellite, take the maximum detection confidence over time; combine GOES-East and GOES-West on a common grid, then smooth and threshold. | GOFER fire progression (Liu et al. 2024) | Gridded confidence from both sensors | Both sensors see the same fire | The paper describes the combination as the minimum in one passage and the average in another |
| Object-based tracking | Cluster detections each 12 hours into fire objects and draw perimeters (alpha shapes). | FEDS, California, Suomi NPP only (Chen et al. 2022) | Hotspot points | One sensor; adding others left as future work | Not designed for multi-sensor counts |
| Analyst-reviewed merge | Human analysts review GOES, VIIRS, MODIS and AVHRR detections and remove false fires. | NOAA Hazard Mapping System | Many sensors in near real time | Analyst judgement | Agency states fire pixels are "a coarse indicator only" and gaps are not back-filled |
| Burned-area inter-calibration | Fit a statistical relation between MODIS and VIIRS burned area over the overlap. | Giglio et al. 2025 (recommendation, not yet done) | Overlap years; higher-resolution reference | Products differ systematically | Simple concatenation "not advisable" at local scales |
| FRP and burned-area fusion | Interpolate FRP in time and extrapolate energy across a mapped burn scar. | One 40-day Australian grassland fire (Boschetti and Roy 2009, abstract only) | Burned-area maps | Burn scar bounds the fire | Totals sensitive to which satellites sampled the fire |

## How each would map onto MODIS and VIIRS hotspots

**Combining the three VIIRS satellites for one month.** The published rules are to process each satellite alike and then average or fall back (GFED5NRT and GBBEPx, in r02), or to treat each satellite as its own time-stamped sample (Li et al. 2022, which states the two VIIRS are "merged based on observation times" but does not say what happens when both see one fire). A third published choice is to use one VIIRS satellite only for homogeneity: FEDS (Chen et al. 2022) and a 2026 global event dataset (Su and Yu 2026, a preprint) use Suomi NPP alone. The NOAA-21 readme reports "high level of consistency" among the three VIIRS products and an FRP bias of up to about 10% for NOAA-21, with no count ratio. Remy and Kaiser (2014) give the principle that the output must not depend on how many satellites flew. Steps with the team's data: scale NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 to Suomi NPP (team factors 1.011 and 1.034), average across satellites that have data each day, sum over the month. This fixes the doubling and tripling that summing causes (1.99 and 2.95 times) and the roughly 30% low bias of a plain monthly mean in outage months. It does not reduce the ±15–20% single-month scatter between two identical instruments. The team has the data, but the rule needs a per-day availability flag; their evidence shows the published outage list is insufficient, and a satellite with no hotspots on a day cannot be told apart from one that saw no fire unless the flag is derived from a larger area. Effort: hours.

**Single-satellite to constellation regression across the sensor change.** The count analogue of Remy and Kaiser (2014) is to fit, over years when both families flew, the ratio of Terra-plus-Aqua counts to the VIIRS value, and apply it to MODIS-only months (and a Terra-to-Terra-plus-Aqua relation for 2000 to 2002). The team's measured ratio is 0.242, or 0.34 by day and 0.18 at night. The paper fitted only regions with at least 400 positive cells; the equivalent here is to fit on the whole box, not on a user's area, because below about 50 km MODIS records nothing in a third or more of the months where VIIRS saw fire, and no ratio restores a zero. It also does not address the 2022–2026 drift; fitting must exclude drift years, as GFED5 does. Not tested on counts in any source. Effort: hours to a day.

**Diurnal-cycle correction for the Terra drift.** The adjustment factor of Zhang et al. (2020) is the published form of "correct for an overpass that moved off the fire peak", which is the team's Terra problem (about 11:15 to 09:20, daytime counts at about half of expected in 2026). It needs a fitted daily cycle for Interior BC from geostationary data (GOES-West covers BC), which the team does not hold. Inferring a cycle from the drift itself is untested and not in any paper. Effort: days or more.

**Duplicate removal before counting.** Zheng et al. (2026) remove same-pass duplicates with a time and footprint-overlap test. The team found 21–23% of MODIS hotspots have a same-pass neighbour within half a pixel, and has scan and track pixel sizes. If the FIRMS time field is only hours and minutes (not confirmed from a source), the 8-second test cannot be applied and duplicates would have to be found by overlap alone. The team's 2 km active cell-day unit removes such duplicates differently. Effort: hours.

## Not applicable, and why

- Priority switch: after 2012 VIIRS is always present, so it reduces to "use VIIRS", and before 2012 there is no VIIRS to prefer; it does not bridge the change.
- Kalman filter with persistence: needs observed area and non-detections, which FIRMS rows lack, and gap filling is moot at monthly scale.
- Geostationary-to-polar FRP calibration: needs a geostationary FRP stream, and the team's evidence shows FRP is a worse bridge than counts when night passes are included.
- Object-based tracking and GOFER: produce perimeters and progression, not monthly counts.
- Analyst-reviewed merge: not reproducible.
- Burned-area fusion and inter-calibration, Landsat and Sentinel-2: need burned-area maps or high-resolution scenes not in the team's data; Landsat revisits about every 8 days, so it suits validation only.

## Unconfirmed

- Scaduto, Chen and Jin (2020, IEEE Journal of Selected Topics in Applied Earth Observations and Remote Sensing), reported to combine MODIS and VIIRS for daily fire progression: only a search summary was seen; the file the draft saved under that name is in fact Liu et al. 2024.
- Freeborn et al. (2009, Remote Sensing of Environment 113:1700–1711, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2009.03.013), a geostationary and polar "virtual active fire product": publisher page blocked; method not read.
- Xu, Wooster, He and Zhang (2020, Remote Sensing of Environment 248:111947, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2020.111947), Sentinel-3 SLSTR: title and authors confirmed through Crossref; only the Wooster et al. 2021 review's summary (more night-time pixels than Terra MODIS at similar overpass times, mainly low-FRP fires) was read.
- A planned harmonized MODIS–VIIRS burned-area record and a GFAS Kalman smoother: search summaries only.
- That FIRMS time stamps carry only hours and minutes: from general knowledge, not a source opened.
- Read as abstract only: Boschetti and Roy 2009; Andela et al. 2015 (body searched, not read in full); Zheng et al. 2021 (abstract and header).
