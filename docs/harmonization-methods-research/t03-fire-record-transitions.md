# How fire records have been carried across sensor changes

## Summary

This topic covers how the fire community itself kept a long record going when the satellite or sensor underneath it changed. It excludes the MODIS (Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer) to VIIRS (Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite) active-fire comparisons already covered in r01 and r02. Fire records have used five approaches. Some keep comparisons inside one "reference" sensor and accept a break in the record. Some fit a regional multiplicative factor over overlap months. Some predict the old record at the new sensor's level and then match its distribution. Some compare anomalies, which are departures from each sensor's own seasonal mean, instead of levels. Some flag years affected by orbit drift instead of correcting them. All of these have been used for fire, but almost always for burned area or for counts summed over large regions. No source found gives a MODIS-to-VIIRS hotspot count factor, and every source that joined two sensors states that the join cannot restore fires the weaker sensor never detected. That limit is the team's measured small-area problem: below about 50 km radius, MODIS records nothing in a third or more of the months in which VIIRS saw fire.

## Methods

| Method | How it works | Where used | Data needed | Main assumption | Known failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single reference satellite | Every comparison across time uses the detections of one designated satellite, and other satellites are shown but never compared. When the reference changes, the break is accepted. | INPE (Brazil's National Institute for Space Research) Programa Queimadas: NOAA-12 AVHRR (Advanced Very High Resolution Radiometer) from June 1998 to July 2002, then Aqua MODIS ([INPE FAQ](https://terrabrasilis.dpi.inpe.br/queimadas/portal/pages/secao_informacoes/faq/); [INFOQUEIMA Sept 2025](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Infoqueima/2025/2025_09_infoqueima.pdf)) | Hotspots from one sensor over the whole baseline | The reference sensor and its orbit are stable | INPE says the 2002 switch "caused the discontinuity" in counts. The plan for after Aqua (move to Suomi NPP VIIRS, "adjust carefully") gives no method, and Suomi NPP has since ended. Days the reference misses are left empty. |
| Regional multiplicative factor on overlap, merged by precedence | Regress the reference sensor's monthly total on the older sensor's total over the overlap period for each region, giving a slope γ. Multiply the older record by γ. Where two older sensors exist, one takes precedence. | GFED3 (Global Fire Emissions Database version 3): ATSR (Along-Track Scanning Radiometer) and TRMM VIRS (Tropical Rainfall Measuring Mission Visible and Infrared Scanner) scaled to MODIS. γ = 1.35 for Boreal North America, r = 0.94 ([Giglio et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-7-1171-2010)) | Monthly regional totals from both sensors in overlap years | The ratio is constant in time within a region | It "does not restore burned area to those VIRS and ATSR zero-fire-count grid cells", and only scales cells that already had fire. |
| Same algorithm, then regional statistical inter-calibration | Run the old algorithm on the new sensor on the same grid. Measure differences by region. Recommend calibrating rather than concatenating at local scale. | MCD64A1 (MODIS burned area) to VNP64A1 (VIIRS burned area). Boreal North America VIIRS is +10.0% higher, median slope 1.07 ([Giglio et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2025.115006)) | Ability to rerun the detection algorithm, plus overlap years | Remaining differences are statistical and regional | The authors write that "simple concatenation ... at local scales is not advisable for long-term trend analyses". Inter-calibration is proposed, not yet done. |
| Back-cast to the new sensor plus quantile mapping | Train models on the overlap with the new sensor as target, predict the old years at the new sensor's level, then apply quantile mapping, which reshapes values so their distribution matches the target's. | MRBA60: MODIS FireCCI51 back-cast to Sentinel-3 FireCCIS311 using a Random Forest for each biome and season, trained on 2019–2024 ([Torres-Vázquez et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07959-w)) | Overlap years, plus predictors (active fires, fire radiative power, climate, vegetation) | The relationship learned in the overlap holds in earlier years | Inherits the target's "systematic biases". FireCCI51 showed "29% lower global BA" than the target in the overlap. |
| Anomaly comparison and geometric expected ratio | Remove each sensor's mean seasonal cycle (phase averaging), then compare residuals and their trends, judging continuity by correlation instead of level. Predict the count ratio from swath width × overpass frequency. | ATSR World Fire Atlas, ALGO3 night counts against MODIS Terra 2001–2012: correlation above 90% in most regions, expected ratio about 9 at the equator ([Ramoino et al. 2013](https://ftp.space.dtu.dk/pub/Ioana/papers/s252_3ramoi.pdf)) | Monthly counts per sensor and an overlap period | Both sensors track the same seasonal and inter-annual signal | The geometric ratio "may depend on fire characteristics". Correlation says nothing about absolute level. |
| Flag drifted years, do not correct | Test whether the record moves with sensor geometry. Here the test was a rank correlation of at least 0.5 between annual burned area and mid-year AVHRR solar zenith angle. Exclude or flag years that fail. | Critique of FireCCILT11, the AVHRR long-term burned area product ([Giglio and Roy 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-46168-0)) | Overpass time or geometry per year, plus an independent reference | Drift effects cannot be reliably modelled out | Fixes "often merely shifted the problems to different regions and spatial scales". |
| Monthly aggregation against sampling bias | Sum to calendar months so that a drifting or sparse overpass time samples the daily fire cycle more evenly. | TRMM VIRS in GFED3 (Giglio et al. 2010) | Daily detections | Sampling bias averages out over a month | Does not fix a systematic shift in overpass time, such as Terra moving from about 11:15 to 09:20. |
| Algorithm lineage across geostationary generations | Port the same detection algorithm to the new imager and treat it as a new, separately validated product. | WF_ABBA (Wildfire Automated Biomass Burning Algorithm) on the GOES Imager to FDC (Fire Detection and Characterization) on GOES-16 ABI (Advanced Baseline Imager) ([Csiszar, Schroeder, Schmidt 2017](https://gofcgold.umd.edu/sites/default/files/fireit_meetings/GOFC_Fire_IT_2017/day1/Ivan_Wilfrid.pdf); [Hall et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2019.101928)) | Radiances and the algorithm | Shared logic gives comparable output | No count factor between generations was found. The early ABI product was "minimally validated", with 450 of 514 sampled fire pixels false. |

## How each would map onto MODIS and VIIRS hotspots

**Single reference satellite.** Steps: choose one satellite, build each area's monthly baseline from that satellite alone, and compare the current month only with that baseline. Within the VIIRS era this is well supported, because NOAA-20 runs at 0.99 of Suomi NPP and NOAA-21 at 0.97. It needs no cross-sensor factor, and the team's evidence shows each sensor ranks months against its own history almost identically (rank correlation 0.97). It does not fix the pre-2012 years, which exist only from MODIS. A current VIIRS month therefore still needs a bridge to compare with 2000–2011, and INPE has published no method for that bridge. INPE's own reference, Aqua, is drifting: its daytime overpass moved from about 13:00 to 14:51 local time by 2026. The team has the data. Effort: hours.

**Regional factor on overlap, with precedence.** Steps: over stable-orbit overlap months (2012–2021), fit the ratio of MODIS to VIIRS monthly counts and apply it to one record. Where sensors overlap, let one take precedence. This is the GFED3 design applied to counts instead of burned area. It addresses the level difference (Terra-plus-Aqua to Suomi NPP is 0.242, or 0.34 by day and 0.18 at night). It does not fix single-month scatter of 15–30%. It cannot restore months in which MODIS saw nothing: below a 50 km radius those are 33–50% of VIIRS fire months, which is exactly the zero-cell limit GFED3 states. A factor fitted on a user's small area would rest on very few non-zero months, so the regional factor has to come from a large region such as the team's box. The team has the data. Effort: hours to a day.

**Same algorithm plus regional inter-calibration.** The team cannot rerun MODIS or VIIRS fire detection, so it cannot use the first half of this method. The transferable part is the authors' stated finding that the two records agree regionally but should not be concatenated locally. That matches the team's measured area-size limit. Effort: not applicable as a build.

**Back-cast plus quantile mapping.** Only the quantile-mapping step is feasible. It maps the distribution of a region's MODIS monthly counts onto the distribution of VIIRS monthly counts over the overlap years. With about 10 overlap fire seasons, the upper quantiles rest on a handful of months. It also has no answer for zero months, which are frequent at small radii. The Random Forest step needs climate and vegetation predictors that the team does not have. Effort: about a day.

**Anomaly comparison and geometric ratio.** Steps: express each month as a departure from that sensor's own monthly mean or median, and compare departures across sensors. The geometric ratio offers a sanity check on any fitted factor, but MODIS and VIIRS both image the area about daily, so the team's ratio is driven by pixel size and not by swath. The team's evidence limits this method: the two sensors give the same five-class label in only 71% of fire-season months and 44% of near-normal months, and the MODIS median is zero below about 100 km. The team has the data. Effort: hours.

**Flag drifted years.** Steps: mark Terra's daytime counts from 2023 onward, which fell to 0.46 of expected in 2026, and leave them out of fits or show them as flagged. This method has no fix for the record, only a rule on how to use it. The team has overpass times in the data. Effort: hours.

**Monthly aggregation.** The calendar is already monthly. Months should be keyed by local date; keying by UTC moved 455 Terra hotspots across a month boundary in 2021.

## Not applicable, and why

- Fixed-threshold detection on a stable band (ATSR ALGO3): this needs radiances, and the team has only detected points.
- The Random Forest back-cast: it needs predictors the team does not have, and days to build.
- Rerunning one algorithm on both sensors (VNP64A1, WF_ABBA to FDC): this needs swath data and code.
- CWFIS (Canadian Wildland Fire Information System) Fire M3: its 2019 metadata says only that AVHRR, MODIS and VIIRS hotspots are combined, with no stated scaling rule.
- The CEOS LPV (Committee on Earth Observation Satellites Land Product Validation) framework for comparing products that do not observe at the same time: it is planned, with a protocol draft expected at the end of 2025, and no published method was found.

## Unconfirmed

- Arino, Casadio and Serpe (2012), *Remote Sensing of Environment* 116:226–238, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2011.05.025. The title and authors were confirmed through Crossref, but the text was not read. A reported finding of "negligible offsets" between ATSR-2 and AATSR (Advanced ATSR) is unconfirmed.
- INPE FAQ question 38 reportedly gives "first half of 2012" as the end of NOAA-12 as the reference, which conflicts with question 7. The verification fetch did not return question 38.
- The claim that ABI detects about 6% more fire pixels than the GOES-13 Imager (Science of Remote Sensing, 2021) came from a search summary only. The page returned an HTTP 403 error.
- Chuvieco et al. (2019), the best review entry point found, was confirmed only through Crossref. Its text was not read.
- Lizundia-Loiola et al. (2022), *Remote Sensing of Environment* 282:113298, was read as an abstract only: FireCCIS310 mapped about 1 million km² more than FireCCI51 in 2019.
- Not found: any GOES-13 to GOES-R count factor, any CWFIS cross-sensor rule, any 2024–2026 MODIS-to-VIIRS active-fire count conversion, and any new INPE reference-satellite decision after the end of Suomi NPP. The web search budget ran out during verification, so these are "not found in the searches that were possible", not proven absences.
