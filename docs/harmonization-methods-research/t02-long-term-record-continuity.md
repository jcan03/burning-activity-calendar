# How long-term satellite records are continued across sensors

Verified 2026-11-14 against the sources listed below. The applicability statements are the reviewer's reading, checked against the team's measurements in `docs/harmonization-evidence.md`. They are not published findings, and they do not choose the team's method.

## Summary

Climate data records are long time series built from several satellites in turn. Records of vegetation, soil moisture, ocean colour and sea surface temperature (SST) are continued across sensors in much the same way. One reference sensor is chosen because it is stable or well calibrated. A correction is fitted where sensors overlap in time, or derived from a physical model where they do not. Changes in overpass time caused by orbit drift are handled as a separate step. Stability is shown as the trend of the difference against an independent reference. None of the sources opened applies these methods to fire hotspot counts. Every source works on continuous fields, which have a value at every pixel on every overpass. Hotspots are sparse threshold detections, and the team has no cloud mask and no record of passes that saw nothing. The ideas that carry over are therefore the simple ones: how to choose the reference, a ratio fitted over a clean overlap window, equal weighting of sensors, chaining through an intermediate sensor and a stability check. Distribution matching, error-weighted merging and diurnal-cycle correction depend on data the team lacks.

## Methods

| Method | How it works | Where used | Data needed | Main assumption | Known failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overlap regression ("translation equations") | Regress paired near-simultaneous, near-nadir values of an old and a new sensor with geometric mean regression, which allows for noise in both. Fit one global equation or one per land-cover class. | AVHRR (Advanced Very High Resolution Radiometer) and SPOT VEGETATION to Terra MODIS vegetation indices ([Tsend-Ayush and Miura 2013](https://measures.arizona.edu/documents/dataviewer/ContinuityScienceAlgorithm_Slides.pdf)). VIIRS (Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite) to MODIS NDVI for drought monitoring ([Benedict et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs13061210)) | Paired observations over an overlap period | The relationship is fixed over time and carries outside the overlap | Steps and drift remain in the record if the relationship changes over time ([Tian et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2015.03.031)) |
| Bandpass adjustment by simulation | Simulate both sensors' bands from the same hyperspectral spectra, then regress one on the other. No overlap in time is needed. | Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2, HLS ([Claverie et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2018.09.002)), and the earlier AVHRR instruments in the 2013 slides | Spectral response functions and a library of spectra | The spectral difference dominates the bias | The paper notes that its coefficients differ from those fitted on real image pairs |
| Common grid and view-angle normalisation | Resample both sensors to one grid. Normalise reflectance to a nadir view with fixed bidirectional reflectance (BRDF) coefficients. | HLS (Claverie et al. 2018). VIIRS–MODIS compatibility ([Miura et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1117/1.JRS.12.045004)) | View and sun angles for every pixel | The BRDF shape is constant | Valid only for view angles near nadir |
| Calibration against a better-calibrated sensor that overlaps only part of the record | Derive calibration parameters for every instrument from a stable sensor that overlaps only some of them, using Bayesian methods | GIMMS NDVI3g, AVHRR calibrated against SeaWiFS ([Pinzon and Tucker 2014](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs6086929)) | A trusted overlapping sensor | The reference itself is stable | Tian et al. 2015 report possible artefacts in NDVI3g in most humidity zones |
| Cumulative distribution function (CDF) matching to a reference | Rescale each sensor's percentiles linearly onto the percentiles of a reference "expected to have the most stable climatology" | ESA Climate Change Initiative (CCI) soil moisture ([Gruber et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-11-717-2019)) | Long, dense series of values for each location | The sensors share one climatology. Where there is no overlap, the method assumes there is no trend between periods. | It "can change" trend magnitude and "may introduce spurious error correlations" (Gruber et al. 2019, Sect. 10) |
| Merging weighted by triple collocation | Estimate each product's error variance by triple collocation, which compares three independent estimates of the same variable. Merge by weighted least squares. | CCI soil moisture v3 onward (Gruber et al. 2019) | Three independent, collocated estimates of one variable | Errors are independent of each other | The weights are averages over time, so they miss changes in a sensor's error |
| Per-cell ratio bias correction, band shifting and equal-weight merging | Divide each sensor by its ratio to a reference over 2003–2007, a window with no gaps. Give each sensor equal weight rather than weighting by observation count. Bring VIIRS in through MODIS-Aqua because VIIRS never overlapped SeaWiFS. | Ocean Colour CCI ([Sathyendranath et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/s19194285)) | Dense multi-year overlap in every cell | One correction per location stays valid outside the window | A single annual value carried a seasonal bias, so later versions used daily running averages |
| Recalibration plus a diurnal adjustment to a fixed local time | Recalibrate against the reference sensor in which the authors had most confidence (AATSR). Chain the AVHRR overlaps. Adjust every SST to 10:30 or 22:30 local time with a physical model, because aliasing the diurnal cycle "will produce spurious inter-annual trends" | SST CCI ([Merchant et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-019-0236-x)) | Matchups within 5 minutes, and a model of the diurnal cycle | The model represents the diurnal cycle correctly | Before 1995 in situ data serve as the reference, so the record is not independent of them |
| Orbit-drift correction: geometric modelling, diurnal-cycle reconstruction, or time series with the solar zenith angle as a proxy | Validate by simulating drifted sampling from 15-minute geostationary data, then score by bias and by the trend of the difference | AVHRR land surface temperature ([Julien and Sobrino 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/frsen.2022.851933)) | High-frequency reference data | The drift signal can be separated from real change | Such corrections "have been seldom validated". A rival method left a bias of 1.28 K. |
| Observation-based diurnal correction | Intercalibrate the instruments, then attribute the remaining differences between co-orbiting satellites at different local times to the diurnal cycle | Microwave Sounding Unit (MSU) and Advanced MSU temperatures ([Po-Chedley et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1175/JCLI-D-13-00767.1)) | Several satellites sampling different local times, with continuous coverage | Every remaining difference is caused by the diurnal cycle | Independent records disagreed on tropical trends by more than a factor of three, partly because of differences in drift correction |
| Drift diagnosis against a stable-geometry sensor | Map the spurious trend per pixel by comparing the drifting record with MODIS Aqua | AVHRR NDVI ([Nagol et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs6076680)) | An overlapping stable sensor | The reference does not drift | It diagnoses the problem but does not correct it |

## How each would map onto MODIS and VIIRS hotspots

**Choosing the reference by stability.** The CCI programmes pick the reference that is most stable or in whose calibration they have most confidence, not the newest sensor. In the team's data the VIIRS overpass times stayed at about 12:40 and 02:10 local solar time. Over the same years Terra's daytime pass moved from about 11:15 to 09:20 and Aqua's from about 13:00 to 14:51. This is an argument, not a computation. It supports whichever reference the team can show is stable. It does not by itself settle the target record, because Pinzon and Tucker used an older sensor as the reference. Effort: hours.

**A ratio fitted over a clean overlap window (Ocean Colour CCI style).** Fit Terra plus Aqua divided by Suomi NPP over February 2012 to December 2021 only, split by day and night (0.34 and 0.18). Apply it as a multiplication, as Ocean Colour CCI does, which never produces negative values. This handles the fourfold difference in level and excludes the drift years. It does not reduce the 15–30% single-month scatter or the two-satellite noise floor of ±15–20%. It cannot restore the months that MODIS missed below about a 50 km radius. Ocean Colour CCI fits one factor per grid cell, which is not possible for the team: at small radii the MODIS count is often zero and the ratio is undefined. The analogue is one regional factor, or one from an enclosing area. The team has the data. Effort: hours.

**Geometric mean regression (translation equations).** This is defensible if the team fits a line rather than a ratio of sums, because both monthly counts are noisy. The team's evidence shows slopes close to 1 and no gain from a curve, so it adds little to a ratio. The per-land-cover slopes on the 2013 slides differ by class: 1.25 for evergreen needleleaf forest against 1.10 for evergreen broadleaf. That supports stratifying by class in principle. The team found monthly factors weak, and a single box offers few classes. Effort: hours.

**Chaining through an intermediate sensor.** NOAA-21 never overlaps stable-orbit MODIS. Ocean Colour CCI faced the same gap with VIIRS and SeaWiFS and bridged it through MODIS-Aqua. The team can bridge NOAA-21 and NOAA-20 to Suomi NPP in the same way (factors 1.034 and 1.011), and then Suomi NPP to MODIS. The team has the data. Effort: hours.

**Equal weight for each sensor.** Ocean Colour CCI gave each available sensor equal weight rather than weighting by observation count. This is a published precedent for the team's rule of taking the daily mean of the VIIRS satellites that have data. Effort: already measured.

**Stability check as the trend of the difference.** Following SST CCI and Julien and Sobrino, report the trend per year of the logarithm of the MODIS-to-VIIRS ratio over 2012–2021, together with its interval. Report the drift years separately. The team's table of Terra's daytime count, which fell to 0.46 of its expected value in 2026, is already this diagnostic. The team has the data. BC fire records give an independent check of yearly totals only, and they have no 2026 data until April 2027. Effort: hours.

**CDF or percentile matching.** At the monthly level, ranking a month against its own sensor's history is close to percentile matching. The team has already measured it: a rank correlation of 0.97, but the same five-class label in only 71% of months. A full quantile mapping of counts is poorly defined when many months are zero. That is the case below about 100 km, where the MODIS median month is zero. Gruber et al. warn that it can change the size of a trend. The team has the data only for large areas. Effort: a day.

**Diurnal-cycle correction of Terra and Aqua.** In principle this would fix the 2026 fall in Terra's daytime count. It needs a fire diurnal cycle for Interior BC. The team cannot derive one: it has only three or four sampling times, no record of passes that saw nothing, and no cloud mask. It would therefore need outside geostationary data. Effort: days or more. It is context only.

## Not applicable, and why

- **Bandpass adjustment and band shifting.** Hotspot counts have no spectral bands. The difference that matters is the detection threshold and the pixel size (1 km against 375 m).
- **Simulation from hyperspectral spectra.** It would need a simulator of fire scenes.
- **View-angle normalisation with BRDF coefficients.** It assumes a continuous reflectance field. The team found that scan-angle filters did not reduce the monthly spread.
- **Merging by triple collocation.** The three VIIRS satellites carry the same instrument and algorithm, so their errors are not independent. There is also no third independent hotspot product.
- **Validating the correction by simulating drift.** It needs geostationary fire data the team does not have.

## Unconfirmed

- Benedict et al. 2021, Miura et al. 2018, Nagol et al. 2014 and Pinzon and Tucker 2014 were read as abstracts only. Tian et al. 2015 was read only as a summary of the abstract on the university repository page.
- Po-Chedley et al. 2015 was read through the reproduction in the author's 2016 dissertation (sections 2.1–2.3) and its Crossref abstract.
- It is not confirmed whether later Ocean Colour CCI versions use MERIS rather than SeaWiFS as the reference. The product guides would not open.
- Pinzon, Brown and Tucker 2005, an orbit-drift correction by empirical mode decomposition, is known only from the reference list of Julien and Sobrino 2022.
- Dorigo et al. 2017 was not opened.
- The reviewer found no source that applies any of these methods to fire hotspot counts. The search budget ran out before a dedicated search for fire applications and for a general review paper. Yang et al. 2016 ([doi:10.3390/rs8040331](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs8040331)), a review of satellite climate data records, was seen only as an abstract.
