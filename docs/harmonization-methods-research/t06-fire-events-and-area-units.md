# Fire-event, cluster and area-based units that do not depend on pixel size

Verified 2026-11-14. Terms: a **hotspot** is one row of the NASA Fire Information for Resource Management System (FIRMS), meaning one satellite pixel flagged as burning. **MODIS** (Moderate Resolution Imaging Spectroradiometer, 1 km pixels) flies on Terra and Aqua. **VIIRS** (Visible Infrared Imaging Radiometer Suite, 375 m pixels) flies on Suomi NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21. **MCD64A1** is the MODIS 500 m monthly burned-area product, which gives a day of burning for each burned pixel. **DBSCAN** (Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise) groups points that lie within a radius (eps) of each other when at least MinPts points are present. **ST-DBSCAN** adds a time window. **Ratio** means MODIS count divided by VIIRS count, as in the team's evidence file.

## Summary

This family replaces "one row per pixel" with a unit that is meant to be closer to the fire itself: a fire event (detections linked in space and time), a footprint area (each detection given an area and overlaps merged), or a gridded unit (grid cells with fire on a day). It is widely used for fire. The well-known event products (Global Fire Atlas, FIRED, GlobFire) cluster MCD64A1 burned-area pixels, not hotspots. Hotspot-based versions exist (FEDS, Su and Yu 2026, Dastour et al. 2024, Deng et al. 2026, the FINN emissions inventory). No study found tests whether monthly event counts or footprint areas from MODIS and VIIRS agree over a long overlap. The two closest results cut both ways: MODIS and VIIRS find nearly the same large fires in Alberta, but in Yunnan, adding NOAA-20 raised the annual event count markedly, mostly through small events. So events and areas reduce the effect of pixel size and duplicate detections. They do not remove the effect of detection sensitivity or of the number of satellite passes, and none of them is a "number of hotspots".

## Methods

| Method | How it works | Where used (source) | Data needed | Main assumption | Known failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Global Fire Atlas | Ignition points are taken as local minima of burn date. Fires grow into neighbouring 500 m cells burned within a "fire persistence" threshold: 4 days where a cell burned more than three times in 2003–2016, and 6, 8 or 10 days where it burned three times, twice or once. | Global, 2003 onward; Andela et al. 2019, ESSD ([doi](https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-11-529-2019)) | MCD64A1 | Burn date is accurate to a few days | The coarse grid sometimes fragments large fires. Burn-date uncertainty is "typically at least 1 d". Perimeter length is underestimated. |
| FIRED | A 3-D moving window: burned pixels within 5 pixels and 11 days join one event, and events that touch merge. These values were tuned over all 225 combinations of 1–15 pixels and 1–15 days against 13,741 perimeters from MTBS (Monitoring Trends in Burn Severity). | Contiguous US; Balch et al. 2020, Remote Sensing ([doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12213498)) | MCD64A1 | One space-time window fits the whole region | Event numbers change substantially with the thresholds, and the best values are expected to differ by region. Area fit to MTBS has R² of 0.92 overall but is weaker for small fires. |
| GlobFire | Touching burned patches form one fire unless more than 5 days apart. A fire closes after 16 days with no new burned area. The spatial step is DBSCAN at 0.01°. | Global; Artés et al. 2019, Scientific Data ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-019-0312-2)) | MCD64A1 | Fixed global thresholds | The authors say the thresholds "could depend on the location of the fire". |
| FEDS (Fire Event Data Suite) | Every 12 hours, new VIIRS pixels are grouped by proximity using a ball-tree search. A group joins an existing fire if it falls within that fire's perimeter plus a 5 km buffer (forest) or 1 km buffer (shrub, crop, urban). A fire stays active while it has detections in the past 5 days. Fires that touch merge. The perimeter is an alpha shape (α = 1 km) plus a 187.5 m buffer. | California, VIIRS; Chen et al. 2022, Scientific Data ([doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-022-01343-0)) | VIIRS points, land cover | Fire spread is continuous between overpasses | About 9% of the area inside the perimeters may be unburned islands. Position is uncertain by about ±500 m. Fast fires are missed between passes. |
| Su and Yu global events (preprint) | Keep `type` 0 and mask 0.005° cells with more than 90 detections a year. Grid to 0.005° keeping the earliest date per cell, then cluster within each month. Merge across months when patches overlap by more than 2 cells or 5% and the time gap is 5 days or less. Drop clusters under 3 cells. | Global, Suomi NPP 2012–2025; Su and Yu 2026, ESSD Discussions ([link](https://essd.copernicus.org/preprints/essd-2026-387/)) | FIRMS VIIRS CSVs | One satellite and a fixed grid | Precision against Canadian records is about 0.8 for fires over 10 km² but near 0.1 when small fires are included. Small agricultural and industrial noise is "practically challenging" to exclude. Cloud breaks tracking. |
| Hotspot DBSCAN, both sensors | Spatial DBSCAN on each year's hotspots, with eps tested from 750 to 1500 m (and up to 3000 m for MODIS) and MinPts of 3 or 4. | Northern Alberta, southern NWT and edges of BC; Dastour et al. 2024, Fire ([doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/fire7100355)) | FIRMS near-real-time (NRT) points | Spatial proximity alone separates fires | Matching falls sharply for small fires and is worst for MODIS. No multi-day gap rule is stated. |
| Multi-level ST-DBSCAN | Each satellite is clustered first, then the VIIRS satellites together, then all sensors. The settings are eps 1450 m for MODIS and 550 m for VIIRS, 7 days, and MinPts 2. | Yunnan, 2012–2024; Deng et al. 2026, Remote Sensing ([doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18091359)) | All five satellites | The settings are equivalent across sensors | The event count rose markedly after NOAA-20 was added in 2018, while total fire radiative power did not. |
| Concave-hull perimeters | Perimeters built around clustered hotspots, with an alpha set separately for each sensor (0.2 for VIIRS, 0.3 for MODIS) to keep commission error under 25% and 30%. | Same region; Bhuian et al. 2024, Fire ([doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/fire7070226)) | Points plus reference perimeters | Alpha can be tuned per sensor | Raising alpha raises both the area matched and the commission error. |
| FINN v2.5 footprint union (Fire INventory from NCAR) | Each detection gets a nominal square of 0.14 km² (VIIRS) or 1 km² (MODIS). Detections whose scan × track rectangles, enlarged to 110%, overlap belong to one fire. Pairwise convex hulls are drawn between them and unioned per day. Where tree cover is below 50%, only squares that actually overlap are merged. | Global emissions; Wiedinmyer et al. 2023, GMD ([doi](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-16-3873-2023)) | Points, scan, track, tree cover | "The identity of the sensor is not relevant ... as long as the pixel size ... is correctly represented" | Burning on earlier days in the same place is not accounted for. Adding VIIRS still raises global emissions by about 25%. |
| Older MODIS hotspot clustering | 2.5 km and 4 days (Loboda and Csiszar 2007); 1875 m and 4 days (Lizundia-Loiola et al. 2020); 11 km and 1 day with more than 2 hotspots (Loepfe et al. 2014). | Listed in FEDS Table 1 (Chen et al. 2022); the originals were not opened | MODIS points | — | Not assessed (second-hand) |
| Active cell-days | Count the grid cells with at least one hotspot on each local date. | The team's own analysis (m02); no published cross-sensor test found | Points | Grid size absorbs pixel size | The night ratio swings eightfold with activity. The count steps up with each added satellite. |

## How each would map onto MODIS and VIIRS hotspots

**Footprint union area (FINN-style).** Steps: for each satellite and local date, place a square of the nominal area on each hotspot, merge squares whose 110% scan × track rectangles overlap, union the hulls, and sum the area per month. Using the scan and track columns as the footprint itself, instead of FINN's nominal square, is the team's own variant and is not what FINN does. What it would fix: MODIS scan-overlap duplicates (21–23% of MODIS hotspots have a neighbour from the same pass within half a pixel) are absorbed, and pixel size enters explicitly. What it would not fix: a rough calculation (inference, untested) puts summed MODIS footprints at about 0.242 × (1 / 0.14) ≈ 1.7 times VIIRS before merging, so nominal areas over-correct. Fires MODIS never sees stay missing, and the 2026 Terra daytime loss remains. Data: everything is in FIRMS rows, but tree cover would need an outside raster or a fixed "forest" assumption. Effort: hours.

**Spatio-temporal events (FEDS-lite, Su and Yu-lite, ST-DBSCAN).** Steps: within each satellite, link hotspots closer than R and within T days using union-find or ST-DBSCAN. Published settings are R of 550 m (VIIRS) or 1450 m (MODIS), 1–1.5 km, or a 5 km forest buffer, and T of 4–7 days. Drop clusters under 3 cells, then count events active in, or started in, each month. What it would fix: duplicates and pixel-size crowding. Gap rules of 4–7 days are longer than the Terra overpass shift, so event continuity may be less affected by drift (inference). What it would not fix: event counts depend on detection sensitivity and on the number of passes. Deng et al. show a jump when NOAA-20 was added. The team's evidence shows 2 to 16 detecting passes per day depending on which satellites fly, and a 1.24–1.37 times step for the union of active cells across satellites. Pooling satellites would therefore create steps, and events would have to be built per satellite and then combined. Below a 50 km radius, MODIS records no hotspot in a third or more of the months in which VIIRS saw fire, and events cannot restore them. The evidence file's 921-row Kamloops static source averages about 31 Suomi NPP rows a year, below Su and Yu's 90-a-year mask, so it would survive as recurring small events unless removed separately. Thresholds are untuned for BC. Effort: hours for the code, and validating the thresholds would take a day or more.

**Events with a size floor.** Dastour et al. matched 96% of 173 Canadian National Fire Database fires of 100 ha or more with combined data, 94% with VIIRS and 88% with MODIS, but only 36–43% of fires of 10–25 ha. Counting only events above a floor set in hotspot terms might make counts steadier across sensors (inference, untested). It discards most small-area activity, which is the team's weakest case. Effort: hours on top of events.

**Active cell-days.** The team has measured this already: at 2 km, the ratio rises from 0.24 to 0.65, and grid size between 1 and 5 km makes little difference. The gain for single months is modest. This is the gridded member of the family and the only one with a measured MODIS-to-VIIRS factor for this box. Effort: done.

**Relation to the challenge wording.** None of these is "the number of hotspots detected". An event or footprint series would be a second unit shown with its own label, or a cross-check. The challenge leaves the method open if justified, according to the team's requirements notes.

## Not applicable, and why

- Global Fire Atlas, FIRED and GlobFire: they need MCD64A1 burned area, not FIRMS rows. The Earth Engine copy of GlobFire covers 2001 to 2020, and MCD64A1 ends with MODIS.
- Alpha-shape or concave-hull perimeters: they need tuned parameters per sensor and reference perimeters. That is days of work.
- Burned-area scaling fitted by region (GFED5NRT "effective fire area"): it needs a reference burned-area product. Already covered in r02.
- Methods that need non-detections or a cloud mask, such as corrections for observation opportunity: the team has no swath or cloud data.

## Unconfirmed

- Read as abstracts or search records only, with bibliographic details confirmed through Crossref: Oliva and Schroeder 2015, which aggregates VIIRS 375 m detections into burned area (accuracy better in forest, more errors in grassland and agriculture), and Fusco et al. 2019, which reports MODIS detection rates against agency fires. Their content was not verified.
- Sofan et al. 2020, a buffered VIIRS footprint study in peatland: the DOI and details could not be confirmed and the claims were removed.
- The original thresholds of Loboda and Csiszar 2007, Lizundia-Loiola et al. 2020 and Loepfe et al. 2014 are second-hand from FEDS Table 1.
- Andela et al. 2022 (adapted by Su and Yu) could not be opened. Su and Yu's within-month clustering parameters are therefore unknown.
- The claim that FIRED kept the 2013 Rim Fire as one event while the Global Fire Atlas split it into 14 is not in the preprint that was read, and was removed.
- No dedicated review of fire-event delineation was found. The web-search budget ran out during verification. FEDS Table 1 and FIRED Table 1 serve as partial reviews.
