# How operational fire systems bridge MODIS and VIIRS

## Answer

None of the systems checked here adds raw MODIS and VIIRS hotspot counts into one long series, and none publishes a factor for converting hotspot counts between the two sensors. The long-term emissions systems instead fit factors in fire radiative power (FRP, the heat output of a fire in megawatts) or emissions against a MODIS-era reference. Copernicus GFAS (Global Fire Assimilation System) used development factors of 1.23 by day and 0.46 by night for VIIRS. NASA QFED (Quick Fire Emissions Dataset) fits factors by biome and region on the year 2024. NOAA GBBEPx fits factors against QFED, by continent and later by ecoregion. GFED5NRT, the near-real-time extension of the Global Fire Emissions Database (GFED), is the closest precedent for a count-based app. It turns VIIRS 375 m detection counts into burned area with up to 224 regional "effective fire area" factors, fitted against the MODIS-based GFED5 over the 132 months of 2012–2022. It averages the Suomi NPP and NOAA-20 estimates. GFED5 itself stops in 2022 because MODIS orbital drift went past its quality thresholds. The fire-viewing services (CWFIS, EFFIS, GWIS, Global Forest Watch) do no cross-sensor harmonisation at all.

Families of approach found in these systems:

- **Regression factor against an anchor over an overlap period.** Used by GFAS, QFED and GBBEPx. Factors are split by day and night, biome, region or continent.
- **Convert detections to an area before combining.** Used by FINN, GFED5 and GFED5NRT.
- **Normalise by observation opportunity.** Used by the MODIS gridded product and by GFED5's cos(latitude) factor.
- **Switch date with a calibration overlap.** GFED5 ends in 2022 and GFED5NRT starts in 2023.
- **No harmonisation.** CWFIS, EFFIS, GWIS and Global Forest Watch.

## What this implies for the approach

- **Target record.** Every long-term system anchors to MODIS-era values (GFAS, GFED5NRT, GBBEPx, QFED 3.2). The exception is QFED 3.3, which moves its anchor to NOAA-20 because of the drift. Both directions have precedent. A MODIS-equivalent target matches most of them.
- **Unit.** No system publishes a factor for raw counts. Only factors for FRP, emissions and burned area exist. A count-based calendar would have to fit its own factor. Converting detections to an area per detection (FINN, GFED5NRT) is the documented way to make the pixel size drop out.
- **Scaling.** The evidence favours splitting factors by region and by day and night. The GFAS day factor (1.23) and night factor (0.46) differ by about 2.7 times. Global view-angle factors performed worse than regional ones. No system uses seasonal factors. All the fits are linear.
- **Combining VIIRS satellites.** GFED5NRT and GBBEPx process each satellite separately and then average, or use whichever one is available. QFED found that dropping Suomi NPP barely changed its emissions. This matters because Suomi NPP ended on 2 Nov 2026.
- **MODIS since 2022.** Several systems avoid fitting on the drift years. GFED5 stops in 2022. QFED chose the year 2024 to avoid a jump. GBBEPx v3 fitted on 2016–17. Terra's earlier crossing time biases it low.

## Limits

- The GFAS factors come from 2018 development slides. The factors used in GFAS 1.4.2 are not published.
- The QFED factors are shown only as figures, and the meaning of the 0.768 scaling is unclear.
- The CWFIS web pages would not render, so only the 2019 metadata file was read.
- None of these factors applies to hotspot counts or to Interior British Columbia without refitting.
- The MODIS gridded-product correction needs the total number of pixels observed in each cell. FIRMS hotspot rows do not include it.

## How it was computed

No analysis script was used. The cited PDFs were downloaded with curl into `scratchpad/verify_r02/dl/` and converted to text with pdftotext, and the text was searched for every number quoted in the draft. Web pages were read with WebFetch. Claims that could not be opened and read were removed.
