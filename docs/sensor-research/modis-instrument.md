# MODIS instrument specifications and scan geometry

Verified 2026-11-13. Each fact below was checked against the linked page or PDF text. Status tags: [confirmed] means the linked source supports the fact as stated; [corrected] means the researcher's version was wrong or incomplete and the fixed value is shown; [unverified] means no opened source supported it. Values marked "computed" are arithmetic from formulas in a cited source, not values the source prints.

Terms used: **TEB** is thermal emissive band (MODIS bands 20-25 and 27-36). **NEdT** is noise-equivalent temperature difference. **L1B** is the Level-1B calibrated radiance product (MOD021KM for Terra, MYD021KM for Aqua). **Scan angle** is the angle of the line of sight from nadir at the instrument. **View zenith angle (VZA)** is the angle at the ground, which is larger because the Earth is curved. **Along-scan** means across the ground track (roughly east-west). **Along-track** means along the ground track (roughly north-south). **C6/C6.1/C7** are MODIS processing collections (also called versions 6.0, 6.1 and 7.0).

### Nominal orbit and swath (design values; see "Current status" for drift)

- Design orbit: 705 km altitude, sun-synchronous, near-polar and circular. The descending node is at 10:30 a.m. for Terra and the ascending node at 1:30 p.m. for Aqua. The repeat cycle is 16 days. [LAADS](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/modis/) [confirmed]
- Swath: 2330 km cross-track by 10 km along-track at nadir per scan. [LAADS](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/modis/); [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) ("The MODIS instrument swath is 2,330 km") [confirmed]
- The researcher's claim of a "110 degrees" field of view was not found on the LAADS page. It is consistent with the ±55° scan range below (2 × 55° = 110°). [unverified as a quoted value; consistent with the ±55° scan range]
- FIRMS equator crossing times: Terra about 10:30 a.m. and 10:30 p.m. mean local time, Aqua about 1:30 p.m. and 1:30 a.m. These are nominal values and the FAQ does not mention drift. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- Until regular orbit maintenance ended, inclination-adjustment maneuvers held the mean local equator crossing time within a few minutes of 10:30 (Terra) and 13:30 (Aqua). Drag make-up maneuvers held a 16-day repeat ground track within ±20 km. [Twedt et al. 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001409/downloads/2023_SPIE_Twedt_MODIS_EV_orbit_drift_manuscript_v4.pdf) [confirmed; new item]

### Bands used by the active-fire algorithm (MOD14/MYD14)

The algorithm uses brightness temperatures from the 4 µm and 11 µm channels (bands 21, 22 and 31) to detect fires. It uses bands 1, 2, 7 and 32 to reject false alarms and mask clouds. Its inputs are the 1 km L1B file (MOD021KM/MYD021KM) and the geolocation file. [MOD14 SPA User's Guide v6.2.1, January 2017](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/users_guides_documents/MOD14_6.2.1_SPA_1.1.pdf) [confirmed]

### Thermal-band calibration (relevant to bands 21, 22, 31, 32)

- TEBs are calibrated scan by scan (scan period 1.4771 s) against an on-board V-groove blackbody, with a space view for background. Band 21 is the exception. [Xiong & Chen](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110015244/downloads/20110015244.pdf) [confirmed]
- Band 21's linear gain comes from look-up tables derived offline from periodic blackbody warm-up/cool-down data, in which the blackbody runs from about 272 K to 315 K. The offset and nonlinear terms are set to 0. This is the method for both Terra and Aqua band 21. [Xiong et al. 2008](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20080040164/downloads/20080040164.pdf); [Xiong & Chen](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110015244/downloads/20110015244.pdf); for C6.1, [Chang et al. 2021](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220000138/downloads/2021_Chang_SPIE_TEB_ScanCopy.pdf) [confirmed]
- Calibration accuracy requirements are 1% for most TEBs, 0.5% for bands 31 and 32, 0.75% for band 20, and 10% for band 21 when used for fire detection at Lmax. [Xiong et al. 2010, MODIS On-board Blackbody](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100020840/downloads/20100020840.pdf) [confirmed]
- Blackbody set point: Terra 290 K and Aqua 285 K for most of the mission. **Since April 2020 the Terra set point has been 285 K.** The MCST weekly report for 31 Oct-6 Nov 2026 still shows Terra at 285 K. [Xiong et al. 2010](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100020840/downloads/20100020840.pdf); [Chang et al. 2021](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220000138/downloads/2021_Chang_SPIE_TEB_ScanCopy.pdf); [MCST Terra weekly 2026/304-310](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/iot/am1weekly20263042026310) [corrected] The researcher gave 290 K for Terra without the 2020 change.
- Aqua was launched in the B-side electronics configuration, a choice based on lessons from Terra. [Xiong et al. 2010](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100020840/downloads/20100020840.pdf) [confirmed]. Terra now runs A-side, cross-strapped to Formatter B. Aqua runs B-side. [MCST Terra weekly](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/iot/am1weekly20263042026310); [MCST Aqua weekly](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/iot/pm1weekly20263042026310) [confirmed; new item]

### Scan mirror, detectors and granule layout

- MODIS is a cross-track scanning radiometer ("paddle broom" / whisk-broom). It has a continuously rotating double-sided scan mirror, and both mirror sides are used on alternate scans. TEBs are calibrated per detector and per mirror side. [Madhavan et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170003351/downloads/20170003351.pdf); [Pérez Díaz et al. 2021](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210013903/downloads/Terra%20and%20Aqua%20MODIS%20thermal%20emissive%20bands%20paper.pdf); [Chang et al. 2021](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220000138/downloads/2021_Chang_SPIE_TEB_ScanCopy.pdf) [confirmed]
- Earth-view data are taken over a scan range of ±55° about nadir. This corresponds to an angle of incidence on the mirror of 10.65° to 65.5°. [Pérez Díaz et al. 2021](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210013903/downloads/Terra%20and%20Aqua%20MODIS%20thermal%20emissive%20bands%20paper.pdf); ±55° also in [Xiong et al. 2010](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20100020840/downloads/20100020840.pdf) [confirmed]
- Detectors per band (aligned along-track): 10 for each 1 km band, 20 for each 500 m band, 40 for each 250 m band. One scan is about 10 km wide along-track at nadir. [Sayer et al. 2015](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/8/5277/2015/amt-8-5277-2015.pdf) [confirmed]
- A 5-minute L1B granule has 1354 pixels (frames) across-track and 203 scans along-track (2030 lines at 1 km). Each TEB detector records 1354 frames over the ±55° scan. [Sayer et al. 2015](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/8/5277/2015/amt-8-5277-2015.pdf); [Xiong et al. 2008](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20080040164/downloads/20080040164.pdf) [confirmed]. The fire product's sample index runs 0-1353. [Fire User's Guide](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- A Level-2 fire granule covers about 2340 km along-scan by 2030 km along-track. [Fire User's Guide Sec. 3.2](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]

### Instantaneous field of view (IFOV) and pixel geometry

- The fire product's sample index maps to scan angle by θ = s × (sample − 676.5) radians, with s = 0.0014184397. [Fire User's Guide Eq. 13-14](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]. Computed: the angular sample spacing is 1.418 mrad, which is 1.00 km at 705 km range. Because samples are 0-1353, θ runs from −54.98° (sample 0) to +54.98° (sample 1353). [corrected] The researcher gave −54.9° at "sample 1" and +55.1° at "sample 1354", which uses 1-based numbering; the index is 0-based. No opened primary source states the IFOV in µrad. The 1.418 mrad value is the sample spacing, not a measured detector IFOV.
- Pixel size formulas, from Ichoku & Kaufman (2005) as reproduced in the guide: ΔS = Re·s·(cosθ / √((Re/r)² − sin²θ) − 1) and ΔT = r·s·(cosθ − √((Re/r)² − sin²θ)). The constants are Re = 6378.137 km, r = Re + h and h = 705 km. Pixel area ≈ ΔS × ΔT. The guide says the approximation error is smaller than the error of treating the pixel as sharp-edged. [Fire User's Guide Sec. 8.4.6](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- Computed from these formulas (verified by recomputation). The VZA column uses sin(VZA) = (r/Re)·sin θ.

- NASA's 2025 transition document gives Aqua MODIS swath-edge pixels as "2.0 km (along-) x 4.8 km (cross-track)", which agrees with the computed edge values. [MODIS to VIIRS Transition for Atmospheric Science Products, 18 April 2025](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/learn/modis-to-viirs-transition/MODIS-VIIRS_Transition.pdf) [confirmed; new item]
- Pixel area grows by up to about a factor of 9 at the scan edge, where VZA is about 65°. For VZA > 30°, which is about 50% of the data, pixel area is enlarged by 50% or more. The main distortion is along-scan, with a smaller along-track elongation. [Sayer et al. 2015](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/8/5277/2015/amt-8-5277-2015.pdf) [confirmed]
- FIRMS defines "scan" as the east-west pixel dimension and "track" as the north-south pixel dimension. The pixel is 1 km only at nadir. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]. MCD14ML itself has no scan or track columns. It gives the sample number (0-1353), from which ΔS and ΔT can be computed. [Fire User's Guide Table 10](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [corrected] The researcher said "MCD14ML 'scan' and 'track' columns".

### Along-scan response and bow-tie overlap

- **Triangular along-scan response.** In the along-scan direction, the ground area that contributes to one sample is twice as long as the nominal sample size, and the sensor point spread function (PSF) is triangular. About 75% of the signal comes from the nominal pixel area and 25% from the adjacent nominal pixels. Along-track the PSF is approximately rectangular. The source is a peer-reviewed paper (Tan et al. 2006, Remote Sensing of Environment 105:98-114) citing Barnes et al. (1998) and the MODIS Level-1A Earth Location ATBD (Nishihama et al. 1997). [Tan et al. 2006](https://sites.bu.edu/cliveg/files/2013/09/tanbin03.pdf) [confirmed; fills a researcher open question]. In practice this means the effective along-scan footprint of a nominal 1 km sample is about 2 km wide (base of the triangle) at nadir.
- The pixel spatial response is not exactly square, with slight blurring between adjacent pixels. In pre-launch tests, Terra band 8 (nominal 1 km) had an effective size of about 1.08 km across-track and 1.01 km along-track. [Sayer et al. 2015](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/8/5277/2015/amt-8-5277-2015.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Bow-tie overlap between consecutive scans.** Consecutive scan lines overlap whenever VZA > 0°. The overlap grows with VZA to a maximum of almost 50% at the end of the scan line. [Tan et al. 2006](https://sites.bu.edu/cliveg/files/2013/09/tanbin03.pdf) [confirmed; new item]. This agrees with the computed ΔT = 2.0 km at the edge: a 10-detector scan covers about 20 km along-track while scans are spaced about 10 km apart (computed overlap fraction = 1 − 1/ΔT: about 6% at θ = 18°, 19% at θ = 34°, 50% at θ = 55°).
- Tan et al. also report the scan angle at which the overlap between consecutive scan lines exceeds one observation dimension: about 34° (VZA 39°) for 1 km data, 24° (VZA 27°) for 500 m and 17° (VZA 19°) for 250 m. [Tan et al. 2006](https://sites.bu.edu/cliveg/files/2013/09/tanbin03.pdf) [confirmed; new item]
- **Overlap between adjacent orbits (latitude dependence).** At 45° latitude, swaths from adjacent orbits begin to overlap at scan angles greater than 47° (VZA > 55°), with an overlapped along-scan length of about 360 km. Overlap between orbits increases toward the poles. [Tan et al. 2006](https://sites.bu.edu/cliveg/files/2013/09/tanbin03.pdf) [confirmed; new item]
- For 10 km aerosol retrieval pixels (10 × 10 blocks of 1 km pixels), successive scans at the swath edge are fully overlapped along-track, and pixels 10 retrievals in from the edge are still more than half overlapped. [Sayer et al. 2015](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/8/5277/2015/amt-8-5277-2015.pdf) [confirmed]

### Terra versus Aqua: detector health, crosstalk and calibration differences

- Terra before November 2000: some detectors were made dead or unusable to reduce unexpected crosstalk between many of the 500 m and 1 km bands. This caused striping, undetected small fires and undetected large fires. Severe miscalibration of band 21 in February-March 2000 in rare cases made whole scan lines appear as fire. Terra began acquiring data in February 2000, and problems persisted until early November 2000. The CMG and fire-location products (which includes MCD14ML) are not distributed for this period. [Fire User's Guide Sec. 7.2.1, 8.3.10](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]. FIRMS lists MODIS data from November 2000 (Terra) and July 2002 (Aqua). [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed; new item]
- Terra noisy-detector history (Table 6 of Xiong et al. 2008), launch to day 2006155. After more than 7 years, all 160 Terra TEB detectors were operable and 22 were noisy, including the 10 band-36 detectors that were noisy before launch. All noisy detectors are on the LWIR focal plane. Bands listed: B27 (1, 6), B28 (8, 9), B29 (6), B30 (3, 5, 8), B33 (1), B34 (6, 7, 8) and B36 (all). **No noisy detectors are listed for bands 20-25, 31 or 32.** Noisy-detector information is carried in L1B QA flags. [Xiong et al. 2008](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20080040164/downloads/20080040164.pdf) [confirmed]. The researcher marked this medium confidence; Table 6 was read directly.
- Terra MWIR crosstalk (bands 20-25, which include fire bands 21 and 22) is visible in lunar data. Examples: band 21 detector 1 receives signal from band 20 detector 10, and band 22 detector 8 and band 23 detector 10 show an extra contamination component. Wilson et al.'s summary: band 21 detectors 1 and 9 show striping over warm-water and desert scenes, with "limited improvement" from correction. Band 22 detector 8 shows striping and was successfully corrected. The authors call band 21 "typically only used as a backup to band 22" in fire products and "low-priority for correction". [Wilson et al. 2019](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190000662/downloads/20190000662.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Status of the MWIR crosstalk correction (resolves a researcher open question).** C6.1 L1B applies an electronic crosstalk correction only to Terra PV LWIR bands 27-30. C7 L1B adds corrections for selected Terra and Aqua MWIR detectors and Aqua PV LWIR bands. For Terra MWIR, only 4 detectors are corrected in C7: band 22 detector 8, band 23 detectors 1 and 10, and band 24 detector 1. Band 21 is not corrected. The electronic crosstalk in Terra rose substantially after a 2016 safe-mode event. [Chang et al. 2021, "MODIS TEB calibration improvements for Collection 7"](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220000138/downloads/2021_Chang_SPIE_TEB_ScanCopy.pdf) [confirmed; new item]
- Version 7.0 MODIS L1B (MOD/MYD021KM, 02HKM, 02QKM) and geolocation (MOD03/MYD03) were released by LAADS on 18 March 2026 (page updated 23 March 2026). Thermal changes in v7.0: crosstalk correction for Aqua PV LWIR bands 27-30 and selected MWIR detectors; deep-convective-cloud bias corrections for Terra bands 20 and 29 and Aqua 27-30; improved Aqua TEB mirror-side differences; adjusted nonlinear coefficients for band 30. Version 6.1 L1B was scheduled for decommissioning on 30 September 2026. [Earthdata alert: Version 7.0 MODIS calibrated radiances](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/version-7.0-modis-calibrated-radiances-geolocation-data-products-now-available) [confirmed; new item]
- Terra PV LWIR bands 27-30 show large downward gain drifts caused by electronic crosstalk, and their noise has increased since 2010. Terra PC LWIR bands (31-36, including 31 and 32) changed by at most about 2% in gain over the mission. Aqua's PV LWIR bands are stable. Aqua's band-averaged normalized NEdT is at least a factor of 2 below the specification line of 1.0. [Madhavan et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170003351/downloads/20170003351.pdf) [confirmed]
- Aqua's cold focal plane could not hold its 83 K set point from 2007 and reached close to 83.4 K by 2011-2012. Since 2010 it has oscillated within 0.4 K, and in about 2016 it was stable near 83.1 K ± 0.1 K seasonally. This causes seasonal oscillation in the response of Aqua's PC LWIR bands (31-36). The calibration impact is minimal because calibration is scan by scan. Terra's cold focal plane has been essentially constant apart from early mission. [Madhavan et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170003351/downloads/20170003351.pdf) [confirmed]
- Aqua TEB (paper published March 2009, covering a 6-year mission): apart from "a few noisy detectors", identified before or after launch, response changes were below 0.5% per year, and overall Aqua TEB performance was better than Terra's. The NTRS file opened is a one-page abstract; no detector list was available. [Xiong et al. 2009, IEEE TGRS 47(3)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110006430/downloads/20110006430.pdf) [confirmed]
- Aqua eliminated the photoconductive-band optical leak problem that affected Terra. [Xiong et al. 2008](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20080040164/downloads/20080040164.pdf) [confirmed; new item]
- Terra-to-Aqua consistency, C6.1, night-time warm ocean target, 2000-2020: the average bias is well within ±0.50 K for most bands, with the largest crosstalk-related biases in bands 27 and 30. Mean relative bias (Table III, in situ SST reference) for the fire bands is: band 21 −0.15 ± 0.53 K, band 22 +0.45 ± 0.37 K, band 31 −0.31 ± 0.37 K, band 32 +0.08 ± 0.11 K. Mission-long brightness-temperature change rates over the site (Table II, in situ reference) for Terra and Aqua respectively are: band 21 0.037 and 0.074 K/yr; band 22 0.038 and 0.039 K/yr; band 31 0.002 and 0.003 K/yr; band 32 0.015 and 0.029 K/yr. The paper describes Terra and Aqua as having operated for 20 and 18 years. [Pérez Díaz et al. 2021](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210013903/downloads/Terra%20and%20Aqua%20MODIS%20thermal%20emissive%20bands%20paper.pdf) [confirmed; per-band values are a new item]

### Interruptions and orbit drift

- The Aqua formatter-multiplexer unit failed on 16 August 2020, stopping regular science telemetry for about two weeks (16 August-2 September 2020). Products for this period were made from Direct Broadcast data only, so coverage is partial. [Fire User's Guide Sec. 7.2.4](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- Orbit-maintenance end dates: Terra's last inclination-adjustment maneuver was on 27 February 2020 and its last drag make-up maneuver on 28 July 2022. Constellation exit maneuvers on 12 and 19 October 2022 lowered Terra by about 5.5 km. Aqua's last inclination-adjustment maneuver was on 18 March 2021 and its last drag make-up maneuver on 1 December 2021. [Twedt et al. 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001409/downloads/2023_SPIE_Twedt_MODIS_EV_orbit_drift_manuscript_v4.pdf) [corrected] The researcher, following Giglio's May 2025 slides, said Terra's "final IAM + DMU + 6-km altitude decrease in 2022". The slides do say that, but the primary paper dates Terra's last IAM to February 2020 and gives about 5.5 km. The Terra home page says about 5 km.
- Effects of the drift on MODIS: the 1 km pixel had shrunk to about 970 m along-scan by spring 2025. Other effects are wider gaps between scans, changed bow-tie overlap, changed patterns of spatial sampling (from swath narrowing and faster spacecraft speed) and changed sampling of the diurnal cycle. The slides quote Lin et al. (2023): "maximal scan gaps at nadir widen from ~1/2 to ~1¼ [250-m] pixels (out of 40 [250-m] pixels per scan) in December 2026 for Terra and in July 2026" for Aqua. They also say spatial-sampling impacts "seem to have abruptly increased in magnitude in late 2024". [Giglio, LANCE UWG, May 2025](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/filePost/LANCE_UWG_Orbit_Drift_Giglio_202505.pdf) [confirmed]
- Drift direction: Terra is drifting to earlier morning crossings and Aqua to later afternoon crossings. [Twedt et al. 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001409/downloads/2023_SPIE_Twedt_MODIS_EV_orbit_drift_manuscript_v4.pdf); [LAADS transition PDF](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/learn/modis-to-viirs-transition/MODIS-VIIRS_Transition.pdf) [confirmed]. Values:
  - Terra home page (orbit chart "Updated November 2026"): mean local time 8:32 a.m. and altitude 693 km. The chart's alt text says 9:59 and 692 km, so the page contradicts itself. [terra.nasa.gov](https://terra.nasa.gov/) [unverified exact value; see Conflicts]
  - The MODIS Land news page (last updated 13 November 2025) says Aqua's mean local time "will continue to drift, reaching 3:50 PM around August 2026". [MODIS Land News](https://modis-land.gsfc.nasa.gov/news.html) [confirmed (as a projection)]

## Why these matter for combining the records

The fire algorithm depends on band 21/22 (4 µm) and band 31 (11 µm) brightness temperatures. Saturation limits, noise, the band 21 versus band 22 switch, and calibration offsets therefore set the floor and ceiling of what each sensor can detect and how FRP is computed. The Terra and Aqua units differ in measured saturation temperatures (Aqua is lower), in electronics side and blackbody set point, and in detector crosstalk history. Their fire-band brightness temperatures agree over warm ocean to within a few tenths of a kelvin (for example, band 22 +0.45 K, band 31 −0.31 K). These are small effects, but they are not zero.

Pixel area grows from 1 km² at nadir to about 9.7 km² at the swath edge. The along-scan response is triangular, so each sample also sees its neighbours, and scans overlap by up to about 50% near the swath edge. As a result, the detection threshold and the chance of counting one fire in two pixels depend strongly on scan angle. Adjacent-orbit overlap increases with latitude, so the number of looks per day also depends on latitude.

None of these geometric facts is constant over 2000-2026. Terra's early record (before November 2000) is degraded. Aqua has a gap in August-September 2020. Since 2020-2022 both orbits have been drifting: crossing times are moving and altitude is decreasing, which shrinks pixels to about 970 m and opens nadir scan gaps. The end-of-mission period (2026-2027) therefore has different sampling from the nominal record.

## Conflicts and unverified items

1. **Band 21 NEdT.** The LAADS band table lists 2.00 K ([LAADS](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/modis/), undated web page). Xiong & Chen Table 1 (about 2010, [link](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110015244/downloads/20110015244.pdf)) and Madhavan et al. Table 1 (about 2016-2017, [link](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170003351/downloads/20170003351.pdf)) both list 0.20 K at 335 K. The two peer-reviewed MCST calibration papers are more authoritative, and the LAADS value is probably a decimal-place typo. This was not confirmed with MCST.
2. **Band 21 saturation.** The design maximum is 500 K ([Xiong et al. 2008](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20080040164/downloads/20080040164.pdf)); the measured Terra on-orbit value is 478 K ([Xiong & Chen Table 1](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110015244/downloads/20110015244.pdf)). For band 22 the specification is 328 K and the measured Terra value 329 K. The measured value is the more relevant one for data. The Aqua band 21/22 saturation temperatures were not found.
3. **Band 31/32 saturation.** Terra: about 392 K and 387 K ([Xiong & Chen](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20110015244/downloads/20110015244.pdf)) versus "~380 K" ([Hall et al. AGU 2014](https://studio.m-anage.com/agu/fm14/webprogram/Paper6916.html)). Aqua: "~340 K" (AGU 2014 only). The AGU values are rounded abstract figures; Xiong & Chen is the more authoritative source for Terra.
4. **Swath width.** The instrument swath is 2330 km ([LAADS](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/modis/), [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq), [LAADS transition PDF 2025](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/learn/modis-to-viirs-transition/MODIS-VIIRS_Transition.pdf)). The Level-2 fire granule is about 2340 km along-scan ([Fire User's Guide, May 2021](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf)). Both are approximate; the difference is probably rounding or a different definition of extent. Neither source explains it.
5. **Terra maneuver dates and altitude drop.** Giglio's May 2025 slides say Terra's final IAM, DMU and 6 km altitude decrease were in 2022 ([slides](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/filePost/LANCE_UWG_Orbit_Drift_Giglio_202505.pdf)). The MCST paper gives last IAM 27 February 2020, last DMU 28 July 2022 and exit maneuvers in October 2022 lowering about 5.5 km ([Twedt et al. 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001409/downloads/2023_SPIE_Twedt_MODIS_EV_orbit_drift_manuscript_v4.pdf)). The Terra home page says about 5 km in October 2022 ([terra.nasa.gov](https://terra.nasa.gov/)). The dated MCST paper is the most precise source.
6. **End-of-mission dates.** (a) The LAADS transition PDF (18 April 2025) gives Aqua around August 2026 and Terra around January 2027. (b) The Earthdata alert (2 May 2025) says "late 2026/early 2027". (c) The MODIS Land News page (updated 13 November 2025) says Terra "until the end of the mission in December 2025" and Aqua "August 2026" ([link](https://modis-land.gsfc.nasa.gov/news.html)). (d) A 2023 MCST paper says "predicted end of the missions at the end of 2026" ([Twedt et al.](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20240001409/downloads/2023_SPIE_Twedt_MODIS_EV_orbit_drift_manuscript_v4.pdf)). (e) A search-engine summary claimed LAADS gives September 2027 for Aqua and February 2027 for Terra, but the LAADS transition page I opened shows no dates, so that claim is **unverified**. (f) A Copernicus CAMS article (not opened) reportedly gives Terra January 2027 and Aqua about September 2027 [unverified]. The observed fact is that both instruments were in science mode in the 31 October-6 November 2026 MCST reports, which contradicts (a) for Aqua and (c) for both.
7. **Terra current mean local time and altitude.** The terra.nasa.gov page (November 2026) shows 8:32 a.m. and 693 km in its text, but 9:59 and 692 km in the chart alt text ([terra.nasa.gov](https://terra.nasa.gov/)). A search summary also cited 9:26 a.m. "Updated April 2025". The current value is unverified.
8. **Pixel-area growth factor.** The formulas give 9.66× at the edge (computed). Sayer et al. say "up to a factor of ~9", and in another passage "almost a factor of 10" for 10 km retrieval pixels ([Sayer et al.](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/8/5277/2015/amt-8-5277-2015.pdf)). A GRASS wiki snippet saying a factor of 8 was not opened [unverified]. These are consistent within rounding.
9. **Field of view of 110°.** This is stated by the researcher as a LAADS value but was not found on the LAADS page when opened. It is consistent with ±55° [unverified as a quote].
10. **Scan rate, telescope, power and data rate.** These come only from Wikipedia, because modis.gsfc.nasa.gov/about/specifications.php reset the connection twice. They are internally consistent with the 1.4771 s scan period.
