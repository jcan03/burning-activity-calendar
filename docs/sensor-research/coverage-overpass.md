# Earth coverage, revisit and overpass timing

Verified 2026-11-13. Every row was checked against the linked page or PDF (PDFs were converted to text and searched). Status tags: [confirmed] = the source supports the claim as written; [corrected] = the researcher's value was wrong or incomplete and the corrected value is given; [unverified] = not found on an opened page (the reason is given).

Terms: **MLT** = mean local (solar) time of the equator crossing. **Ascending/descending node** = equator crossing going north/south. **Swath** = width of ground imaged across the track in one orbit. **CMG** = Climate Modeling Grid (gridded MODIS fire summaries). **IAM** = inclination adjust maneuver (holds the local crossing time). **DMU** = drag make-up maneuver (holds altitude and ground track).

### Global coverage and gaps

- A single MODIS has a gap of about **350 km** between consecutive orbits at the equator. The swaths of consecutive VIIRS orbits **overlap by about 350 km** at the equator ([FIRMS swath outlines blog, 25 Jun 2026](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/news/blog/new-firms-data-product-satellite-swath-outlines)). [confirmed; added]
- The MODIS daily-coverage gaps are "small, diamond-shaped gaps in daily coverage in tropical regions (between approximately 30 degrees North and South)" ([FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq)); the swath-outline blog likewise places Aqua MODIS gaps between about 30°N and 30°S ([FIRMS blog](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/news/blog/new-firms-data-product-satellite-swath-outlines)). So about **30° latitude** is the documented boundary poleward of which a single MODIS gives gap-free daily coverage. [confirmed; added. This answers a researcher open question.]
- Terra and Aqua MODIS each view the entire Earth every 1 to 2 days ([LAADS MODIS](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/modis/)). [confirmed]
- NASA SVS animation of Aqua MODIS: observations "cover the Earth during the third day except for small gaps at the equator, which are filled in during the fourth day" (the visualization accumulates days from a start date, so this describes a cumulative mosaic). The same page says MODIS measures a 2300 km swath and "almost the entire surface of the Earth every day" ([NASA SVS 3348](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/3348/)). [confirmed]
- MODIS C6/C6.1 user guide, FAQ 8.6.2: no daily CMG product is distributed because "at most latitudes a single MODIS instrument simply does not sample the Earth's surface adequately in time periods shorter than about 8 days" to average out sampling bias. CMG products are monthly (MOD14CMQ/MYD14CMQ) and 8-day (MOD14C8Q/MYD14C8Q) at 0.25° ([MODIS C6/C6.1 Fire UG v1.0, May 2021](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf)). [confirmed; note the guide is dated 5/2021 although the URL path says 2023-09]
- VIIRS: the 3060 km swath gives about 15% image overlap between consecutive orbits at the equator and "full global coverage every 12h" ([VIIRS UG v1.2, June 2025](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf)); Schroeder et al. 2014 say complete global coverage "every ≈12 h or less depending on the latitude" ([Schroeder et al. 2014](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf)). [confirmed]
- FIRMS FAQ: the wide swaths let MODIS and VIIRS "image almost the entire globe at least twice a day" (one day pass and one night pass) ([FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq)). [confirmed; added]

- Consecutive orbits about 100 minutes apart: a location can "potentially be imaged twice within 100 minutes on consecutive orbits" ([FIRMS blog](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/news/blog/new-firms-data-product-satellite-swath-outlines)); swaths from consecutive orbits "often overlap, especially at high latitudes" ([FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq)). The same persistent fire can therefore be counted more than once per day by one sensor. [confirmed]
- Giglio et al. 2006: because gridded fire counts from polar orbiters "are biased at high latitudes owing to nonuniform spatial and temporal sampling", the CMG product corrects raw counts "for multiple satellite overpasses and missing observations" by normalizing by the expected equatorial coverage in a complete calendar month with no missing observations (the overpass-corrected fire pixel count, layer CorrFirePix in C6) ([Giglio et al. 2006](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Publicacoes-Impacto/material3os/2006_Giglio_etal_GlobalDistribuiton_JGR_DE3os.pdf); [MODIS UG Table 9](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf)). [corrected: the researcher's URL had a typo ("GlobalDistribution"); the working URL spells it "GlobalDistribuiton". The paper's product names are Collection 4 MOD14CMH/MYD14CMH (0.5°); Collection 6 uses MOD14CMQ/MYD14CMQ (0.25°).]

- Orbit spacing: NOAA-21 flies "half an orbit, or 50 minutes, ahead of NOAA-20, with Suomi NPP between them" ([FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq)) [confirmed]. NOAA-20 rephasing to 180° separation from NOAA-21: maneuvers 19–20 March 2024 and final burn 4 April 2024, drift of about 14–16 days ([NOAA OSPO](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2024/04/MSG_20240404_1604.html)) [confirmed]. The 2022 plan was NOAA-21 a quarter orbit ahead of Suomi NPP and NOAA-20 a quarter orbit behind ([NASA SVS 5021](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/5021), page updated 6 Jan 2025) [confirmed]. NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 "fly in the same orbital plane as Suomi NPP, approximately 50 minutes apart" ([FIRMS transition blog, 27 Oct 2026](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/news/blog/transitioning-from-suomi-npp-viirs-omps-what-firms-users-need-know)) [confirmed; added].
- Researcher's note that OSPO said "some L2 products depending on orbital passing time were affected": OSPO lists VIIRS polar winds (failed after 8 days of drift), flood mapping and SST CoastWatch as affected; no fire product is named ([NOAA OSPO](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2024/04/MSG_20240404_1604.html)). [corrected: made specific]
- Terra MODIS is "the only morning overpass" among these sensors, about three hours before Aqua ([FIRMS transition blog](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/news/blog/transitioning-from-suomi-npp-viirs-omps-what-firms-users-need-know)); "No follow-on MODIS-like instrument with an orbit at the Terra morning equatorial-crossing MLT exists in the current or planned NASA-managed Earth observation missions" ([LAADS transition PDF](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/learn/modis-to-viirs-transition/MODIS-VIIRS_Transition.pdf)). [confirmed; added]
- Local time across the swath: no source was found. Derived estimate (my own geometry, not sourced): pixels in one scan are imaged at almost the same UTC instant, and local solar time changes by 4 min per degree of longitude. At the equator, the MODIS swath half-width of ~1165 km spans about ±10.5° of longitude, so about **±40 min** from nadir to swath edge; the VIIRS half-width of ~1530 km spans about ±13.7°, so about **±55 min**. The offset in minutes grows with latitude as the same distance spans more longitude. [unverified, derived]

### Fire diurnal cycle and morning versus afternoon detections

- Peak burning time: in 15 tropical and subtropical regions, using seven years of TRMM VIRS data, "the peak burning time varied between approximately 13:00 and 18:30, with fire activity peaking distinctly earlier for the heavily-forested regions"; the interquartile range of daily burning was 1.3 h (Central Africa) to 5.5 h (Eastern Australia); a single daily peak was usual, with a secondary morning peak in the Eastern Sahel and Northern Australia ([Giglio 2006 PhD dissertation, ch. 5, published as Giglio 2007 RSE 108:407–421](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Publicacoes-Impacto/material3os/2006_Giglio_GlobalDetection_UMaryland_DE3os.pdf)). [confirmed; added]
- About 90% of Terra and Aqua MODIS fire pixels in the tropics and subtropics were detected during daytime overpasses, so a total Terra versus Aqua comparison reflects the mid-morning versus early-afternoon difference ([Giglio et al. 2006, §4.4](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Publicacoes-Impacto/material3os/2006_Giglio_etal_GlobalDistribuiton_JGR_DE3os.pdf)). [confirmed]
- Aqua share of overpass-corrected Terra+Aqua fire pixels (July 2002 – June 2005, Fig. 5): (1) nearly 100% (negligible morning fire) in broadleaf forests of West Africa, Central Africa, Borneo and Brazil, and cropland in southeast Australia; (2) about 75% (predominantly early-afternoon fire) in southern and sub-Saharan Africa (except parts of Sudan and Ethiopia), Southeast Asia, Indonesia, southwest Australian cropland and a crescent in eastern Brazil; (3) about even in northern and western Australia, central Eurasia and boreal forests of Siberia and North America ([Giglio et al. 2006](https://dataserver-coids.inpe.br/queimadas/queimadas/Publicacoes-Impacto/material3os/2006_Giglio_etal_GlobalDistribuiton_JGR_DE3os.pdf)). Equivalent Terra:Aqua ratios are about 0:1, 1:3 and 1:1. [confirmed]
- At boreal latitudes the local-time windows sampled by each MODIS overlap, so the ratio "is in fact not a consistent measure" and "can potentially mute the apparent magnitude of the diurnal fire cycle"; it shows the minimum extent of regions with a pronounced diurnal cycle (same source). [confirmed]
- Terra versus Aqua monthly overpass-corrected cross-correlation, July 2002 – October 2005: high (0.9) in most areas; Table 1 gives Southeast USA 0.77, Eastern Argentina 0.87, Liberia 0.26, Central Africa 0.61, Eastern China 0.62, Southeast Australia 0.54, Pacific Northwest USA 0.99, Southeast Russia 0.99, Southern Africa 0.98, Northern Australia 0.96. In the tropical rain forests of Liberia, Borneo, Central Africa and the Amazon Basin, "Aqua yields fire seasons 5 to 10 times longer" than Terra (same source). [confirmed; extra table rows added]
- Schroeder et al. 2014: "larger and/or more intense biomass burning, typical of VIIRS early afternoon overpass times when dryer and warmer fuel and weather conditions prevail" favors separating fire pixels from background; "smaller and/or cooler night fires could partially offset" the better night-time contrast. The 375 m product gave a 3× (day) and 25× (night) increase in fire pixels over the 750 m baseline (abstract, 2013 data) ([Schroeder et al. 2014](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf)). [corrected: the researcher wrote that small, cool night fires "are detected better at night"; the paper says night fires tend to be smaller and cooler, which partly offsets the better night-time detectability. The 3×/25× figures are now confirmed in the abstract.]
- VIIRS UG v1.2: VNP14IMG detects more fire pixels than VNP14, "particularly pronounced during the nighttime part of the orbit"; for 2021 global C2 data, ~3× more daytime and ~17× more night-time fire pixels than VNP14 ([VIIRS UG](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf)). [confirmed; 2021 figures added. Note 17× (2021, C2) versus 25× (2013, Schroeder) for night.]
- Same guide: differences between VNP14IMG and 1 km Aqua MODIS fire products "are further magnified due to MODIS coverage gaps along the tropics" and MODIS pixel enlargement away from nadir. [confirmed; added]
- Sofiev and Kouznetsov 2026 (GMD 19:10123): Aqua "registers most fires around 13:00–14:00 local time, compared to 12:00–13:30 of Suomi-NPP"; the authors excluded Terra data after 2023 because of the constellation-exit maneuvers ([GMD 2026](https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/19/10123/2026/)). [confirmed; added]

## Why these matter for combining the records

The number of looks per day is not constant. It depends on the sensor (MODIS has equatorial gaps, VIIRS does not), on the number of satellites flying in each period, and strongly on latitude (from fewer than one to about 15 per instrument per day). Raw fire-pixel counts summed per month therefore carry a sampling signal that changes with latitude and with each satellite launch or loss: Terra alone until mid-2002, Terra plus Aqua from July 2002, Suomi NPP from 2012, NOAA-20 from late 2017, NOAA-21 from late 2022, and Suomi NPP gone after 2 November 2026. Because fire activity peaks in the afternoon and day passes provide about 90% of tropical detections, the local time of each day pass matters. Terra's morning pass and Aqua's afternoon pass detect very different numbers of fires in many regions. Both have drifted since 2020–2022 (Terra earlier, toward or past 9:00; Aqua later, toward about 15:30), so the "same" MODIS sensor samples a different part of the diurnal cycle late in its record. The three VIIRS share one 13:30 plane, so adding satellites adds looks at roughly the same local time rather than new times of day. Both MODIS instruments are still in science mode in November 2026, but their end dates are not settled.

## Conflicts and unverified items

1. **NOAA-20/NOAA-21 local crossing time.** FIRMS FAQ: "about 50 minutes ahead of Suomi NPP at approximately 12:40 p.m. and 12:40 a.m." ([FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq), current Nov 2026). NOAA: NOAA-20 held at 13:30 ± 10 min during the 2024 rephasing ([OSPO, 4 Apr 2024](https://www.ospo.noaa.gov/data/messages/2024/04/MSG_20240404_1604.html)); the whole series is in the "13:30 Local Time Ascending Node" orbit ([NESDIS flyout chart, July 2026](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/s3/2026-07/LEO-Flyout-Chart-2026_8_July.pdf)); and FIRMS's own blog says they share Suomi NPP's orbital plane ([FIRMS blog](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/news/blog/transitioning-from-suomi-npp-viirs-omps-what-firms-users-need-know)). NOAA, the operator, is more authoritative. Satellites in the same sun-synchronous plane cross the equator at the same local solar time; a 50 min phase lead changes the UTC time and longitude of the crossing, not its local solar time. The FIRMS 12:40 figure appears to mix up the phase offset with local time.
2. **VIIRS swath width.** ~3060 km (VIIRS UG v1.2, June 2025); 3,040 km (FIRMS FAQ; LAADS transition PDF, Apr 2025); ~3000 km / 6400 pixels (Schroeder 2014). The 375 m user guide is the product-specific source; the differences (~2%) probably reflect rounding or a different definition.
3. **MODIS swath width.** 2330 km (LAADS, FIRMS FAQ, LAADS transition PDF) versus 2300 km (NASA SVS 3348, a visualization page). LAADS is more authoritative. (Sofiev and Kouznetsov 2026 give "∼2063 km" for the scan width, which does not match either; not used.)
4. **Suomi NPP orbit.** NASA Earthdata: 824 km, inclination 97.1° (LAADS transition PDF also says 824 km for VIIRS). Wikipedia: perigee/apogee 833.7/834.3 km, 98.79°, 101.44 min. Note: a sun-synchronous orbit near 824–834 km needs an inclination near 98.7° (the value NASA gives for NOAA-20/21), so 97.1° looks like an error on the Earthdata page (my reasoning, not sourced). A NASA SPIE paper citing ~98.67° was seen in search results only [unverified].
5. **Suomi NPP end time.** NESDIS notice: 13:00 UTC, 2 Nov 2026 (planned start of impact; the page's update headlines also cite 15:40 UTC). LANCE blog and FIRMS FAQ: production ceased 15:40 UTC on 2 Nov 2026. 15:40 UTC is the actual end; 13:00 UTC was the planned time. The researcher's "earlier notice said 1 November" is found only in Wikipedia ([Suomi NPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suomi_NPP)), not on any NOAA or NASA page I opened [corrected to: 1 Nov unverified in primary sources].
6. **MODIS end of science collection.** Aqua: shutdown 26 Aug 2026 and passivation 4 Nov 2026 (Aqua status, Nov 2023); "around August 2026" (LAADS PDF, Apr 2025); around September 2027 (CAMS, Feb 2026, citing NASA); a search snippet attributed "Sept 2027 / Feb 2027" to LAADS but the live LAADS page shows no dates [unverified]. Terra: around January 2027 (LAADS PDF; CAMS); "until February 2027" and "last contact Q1 2026" (Wikipedia, self-contradictory). Late 2026/early 2027 for both (Earthdata alert, May 2025). The MCST weekly report for 31 Oct – 6 Nov 2026 is the most recent and shows both MODIS in science mode, so the Aug/Nov 2026 Aqua dates have passed without shutdown.
7. **Terra final maneuvers.** Twedt et al. 2023 (MCST): last IAM 27 Feb 2020, last DMU 28 Jul 2022. Giglio 2025 slides and LDOPE 2026: "final IAM + DMU ... in 2022". The Terra project page and Earthdata also give February 2020 for the last IAM, so Twedt is more authoritative; the slides compress the dates.
8. **Terra altitude.** 694 km after the October 2022 exit (Earthdata acquisition plan; Wikipedia) versus "nominal mean of 715 km to 702 km by late 2026" (Lin et al. AGU 2024 abstract) versus 705 km nominal (LAADS). The difference probably reflects different altitude definitions (mean semi-major-axis altitude versus nominal or equatorial altitude); not resolved.
9. **Terra projected MLT.** 9:00 around December 2025 (Earthdata acquisition plan, project scientist page) versus 8:30 LTDN by late 2026 (Lin et al. AGU 2024). These are compatible as points on one drift curve but neither is a measured value. The Terra project page still shows Fall 2022 as a future prediction despite "Last Updated: Nov 13, 2026".
10. **Aqua projected MLT.** ~15:30 in Aug 2026 (NASA Aqua status sheet, 2023) versus ~15:50 by Aug 2026 (attributed to NSIDC and to a NOAA FRP paper in search snippets) [unverified].
11. **Unverified researcher items:** Aqua mean motion 14.571 rev/day (not on the page opened); "Terra crossing time passed 10:15 by October 2022" as a measured fact (Wikipedia only).

## Open questions

- No primary-source table gives daytime and night-time overpass counts by latitude band for one MODIS, Terra+Aqua, one VIIRS, or two/three VIIRS. Only the anchor points above are sourced (gaps within 30°S–30°N for one MODIS; at least 4 per day at the equator for Terra+Aqua; 3–4 at mid-latitudes for one VIIRS; 6–8 per sensor at high latitudes; nearly 15 at the poles). Exact counts would have to be computed from orbit geometry or the FIRMS swath-outline product.
- Variation of local solar time across the swath has no published value; ±40 min (MODIS) and ±55 min (VIIRS) at the equator are my geometric estimates.
- Measured (not projected) Terra and Aqua MLT for 2023–2026 was not found; NASA charts on the mission pages have no readable values.
- Current measured LTAN of NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 (13:30 versus 13:25) and whether Suomi NPP's LTAN drifted before it ended.
- Firm end dates for Terra MODIS and Aqua MODIS data collection.
- No published quantification of how the orbit drift changes MODIS fire counts; NASA says it is under active investigation.
- No single documented global Terra:Aqua fire-count ratio was found; only the regional classes from Giglio et al. 2006.
- No documented comparison of fire counts between NOAA-20/NOAA-21 and Suomi NPP afternoon passes was found.
