# Instrument health, calibration stability and band differences over time

Verified 2026-11-13. Status tags: [confirmed] = the linked page was opened and supports the statement; [corrected] = the researcher's value or source was wrong and has been replaced (old value given); [unverified] = could not be confirmed on an opened page.

Terms used below:
- **TEB**: thermal emissive bands, the infrared bands calibrated against an onboard blackbody (BB).
- **NEdT**: noise-equivalent temperature difference, the noise expressed as a temperature in kelvin (K) at a stated reference scene temperature.
- **BT**: brightness temperature.
- **MWIR / LWIR**: mid-wave infrared (about 3.5 to 4.2 µm) / long-wave infrared (about 8 to 12.5 µm).
- **PV / PC**: photovoltaic / photoconductive detectors. On MODIS, bands 20 to 30 are PV and bands 31 to 36 are PC.
- **MMOG**: mid-mission outgassing, a procedure that heats the VIIRS cold focal plane dewar to remove ice.
- **WUCD**: blackbody warm-up / cool-down, done periodically to measure detector non-linearity.
- **L1B**: Level-1B calibrated radiance product, which feeds the fire algorithms.
- **C6.1 / C7 / C2**: MODIS Collection 6.1 and 7, and VIIRS Collection 2.

### MODIS thermal-band calibration stability (Terra and Aqua)

- **Calibration method.** MODIS TEB calibration uses a quadratic response, applied scan by scan against the onboard blackbody. Quarterly WUCD events (blackbody from about 270 K to 315 K) update the non-linear terms. Blackbody setpoint: 290 K for Terra and 285 K for Aqua. Terra changed to 285 K starting April 2020, and both instruments ran at 285 K in late 2026. Band 21 uses a linear calibration only, with a0 = a2 = 0, from blackbody cool-down data. [Chang et al. 2021, C7 TEB improvements](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220000138/downloads/2021_Chang_SPIE_TEB_ScanCopy.pdf), [MCST weekly](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/iot/am1weekly20263042026310) [confirmed]
- **Terra gain history after about 16 years (paper about 2017).** Band-averaged gain changes in the MWIR bands (20–25) are "generally within 2-3%", apart from changes caused by operating configuration and focal-plane temperature. PV LWIR bands 27–30 lost 10–20% of detector gain, mostly through changing electronic crosstalk. PC LWIR bands 31–36 are "very stable to within 1.5%". [Xiong et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170004617/downloads/20170004617.pdf) [confirmed; new]
- **Noisy and inoperable detectors.** Terra MODIS had 35 noisy detectors at launch and 53 after more than 16 years; 3 detectors (one in band 29, two in band 30) were flagged inoperable. Most noisy or inoperable detectors that appeared after launch are in the PV LWIR bands, and nearly half came from the 2016 safe hold. The source does not give per-detector status for bands 21, 22 or 31. [Xiong et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170004617/downloads/20170004617.pdf) [confirmed]
- **February 2016 Terra safe mode.** The spacecraft entered safe mode at 2016/049 14:33:17Z (18 Feb 2016) during an inclination adjustment manoeuvre, and MODIS followed. The spacecraft was recovered on 2016/053 (22 Feb). MODIS went to standby, outgassed for about 25.7 hours, and returned to science mode at 2016/055 19:26:34 (24 Feb 2016). PV LWIR bands 27–30 were most affected: gain changes up to 25% for some detectors (Xiong et al. 2017 say up to 18%), increased noise, and band 27 detectors 9 and 10 and band 30 detector 6 newly flagged noisy, with band 30 detector 8 made inoperable. MCST: "No observable impact in the on-board and the earth-view measurements was observed for the remaining TEB", a group that includes fire bands 21, 22 and 31. [MCST Safe Mode Recovery Summary](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/news/MCST_Terra_MODIS_Safe_Mode_Recovery.pdf), [Xiong et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170004617/downloads/20170004617.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Terra PV LWIR crosstalk after 2016.** After the safe mode, signal contamination in each PV LWIR band (27–30) "increased drastically". C6.1 corrects it with coefficients derived from monthly lunar observations, applied mission-wide. From January 2019, a0 and a2 are regenerated after every Terra lunar roll because the crosstalk changes rapidly. Terra band 30 shows the largest residual drift, about −0.08 K/yr over ocean. This affects bands 27–30, not the fire bands. [Pérez Díaz et al. (NTRS 20210013903)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20210013903/downloads/Terra%20and%20Aqua%20MODIS%20thermal%20emissive%20bands%20paper.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Aqua PV LWIR drift.** Aqua bands 27–30 also have crosstalk and long-term stability problems. Band 30 drifted most, mainly between 2012 and 2021. In C6.1 the rate of change reaches "up to 0.67K/year for these bands"; C7 largely reduces it. [Chang et al. 2021, Sec. 4.2.2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220000138/downloads/2021_Chang_SPIE_TEB_ScanCopy.pdf) [confirmed]

### MODIS collections: what changed in the fire inputs

- **C6 to C6.1.** No algorithm or product changes were made to the fire products in C6.1. "The thermal bands used by the fire detection algorithm were not impacted under Collection 6.1, thus the retrieved fire radiative power (FRP) remains unchanged". The only differences come from recalibrated bands 1 and 2, which the cloud mask uses. For January 2020 the global fire-pixel totals were 404,057 (C6) and 404,052 (C6.1), a 0.001% change, and 99.64% of granules were identical. C6 and C6.1 fire products "may be freely intermixed". [Fire User's Guide, Sec. 2.1](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed; new]
- **FIRMS uses C6.1.** The FIRMS FAQ describes NRT MODIS as Collection 6.1, "available since April 2021". [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed; new]
- **C7 TEB changes (Chang et al. 2021).** Terra MWIR crosstalk correction applies to only 4 detectors: band 22 detector 8, band 23 detectors 1 and 10, and band 24 detector 1. For Aqua, C7 applies crosstalk correction to detector 1 of every MWIR band except band 21, which includes band 22, a fire band. Band 21 stays linear with no a0 correction. Band 31 is the reference band for deep-convective-cloud analysis and "exhibits accurate calibration and excellent stability". Band 21's uncertainty is not shown in the C7 uncertainty chart. [Chang et al. 2021](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20220000138/downloads/2021_Chang_SPIE_TEB_ScanCopy.pdf) [confirmed; Aqua detail new]
- **C7 L1B release (2026).** LAADS released Version 7.0 MODIS L1B radiance and geolocation products: published 18 Mar 2026, issued 27 Mar 2026. TEB changes include crosstalk correction for Aqua PV LWIR bands 27–30 and select MWIR detectors, bias corrections for Terra bands 20 and 29 and Aqua bands 27–30, and adjusted band 30 non-linear coefficients. The notice does not mention bands 21, 22 or 31. Version 6.1 L1B was to be decommissioned on 30 Sep 2026. No C7 active-fire (MOD14/MYD14) product or reprocessing announcement was found. [Earthdata V7.0 L1B notice](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/version-7.0-modis-calibrated-radiances-geolocation-data-products-now-available) [confirmed; new; this replaces the researcher's statement that C7 "is not applied" as a future possibility]

### MODIS orbit drift late in life (affects sampling, not calibration)

- **Terra.** Last manoeuvre to hold 10:30 a.m. mean local time on 27 Feb 2020; expected to "reach and exceed 10:15 AM MLT by October 2022"; constellation exit "with a lowered orbit altitude of 694kms". The May 2025 user-working-group slides say: "Final inclination adjustment maneuver (IAM) + drag make-up (DMU) + 6-km altitude decrease in 2022". [MODIS Land news](https://modis-land.gsfc.nasa.gov/news.html), [Giglio, LANCE UWG May 2025](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/filePost/LANCE_UWG_Orbit_Drift_Giglio_202505.pdf) [corrected: the researcher's "lowered about 5 km" is 6 km in the slides; the MODIS Land page gives a 694 km exit altitude]
- **Aqua.** Final manoeuvres to hold 1:30 p.m. and 705 km on 18 Mar 2021; began drifting later in July 2021; exceeded 1:45 p.m. in February 2023; expected to reach "3:50 PM around August 2026". MODIS Land says products are "mostly expected to be science quality except for reduced grid size". [MODIS Land news](https://modis-land.gsfc.nasa.gov/news.html) [confirmed]
- **Drift effects on MODIS pixels.** Pixel size at 1 km nadir falls to about 970 m along-scan (spring 2025). Maximal nadir scan gaps widen from about ½ to about 1¼ 250 m pixels (out of 40 per scan) by December 2026 for Terra and July 2026 for Aqua (quoting Lin et al. 2023). Bowtie overlap and diurnal sampling also change. [Giglio, LANCE UWG May 2025](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/filePost/LANCE_UWG_Orbit_Drift_Giglio_202505.pdf) [confirmed; slide text extracted]

- **How often I4 saturates (S-NPP, early mission).** Nominal I4 saturation affected about 8% of daytime fire pixels and 1% of nighttime fire pixels. I5 saturation affected 0.02% of daytime pixels and was negligible at night. I4 digital-number folding affected 0.2% of fire pixels, with none on I5. Suspicious I4 values from mixing saturated and unsaturated native pixels affected 0.5% of daytime and 0.04% of nighttime fire pixels. [Schroeder et al. 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed; new]
- **Three I4 saturation scenarios (C2 user's guide v1.2, June 2025).** Asked how often I4 saturates, the guide answers "Quite often". The scenarios are: (1) nominal saturation at 367 K; (2) DN folding, which gives an abnormally low temperature that can be identified using I5; (3) on-board aggregation that mixes saturated and unsaturated native pixels, giving spurious Level-1 radiances with no quality flag. They have "small/negligible effect on the fire detection performance" but are "a major factor limiting the retrieval of sub-pixel fire characteristics (FRP) using the I4 channel". High-confidence class 9 pixels are those with day or night saturation, including DN folding. The daytime saturation condition is BT5 ≥ 325 K OR BT4 = 367 K OR ΔBT45 < 0. [375 m User's Guide v1.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- **FRP uses M13.** 375 m FRP is computed from co-located 750 m M13 data, "an alternative solution to the frequent saturation" of I4. All 375 m pixels inside one 750 m pixel share the same fractional FRP. If M13 saturates, the 375 m fire pixels can still be reported, but their FRP is set to zero. [375 m User's Guide v1.2, Sec. 3.1.3](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed; new]

### Spectral differences between the sensors' fire channels and their documented effects

- **I4 sits about 0.3 µm shorter than M13.** Schroeder et al. (2014) write that I4's "spectral placement … is approximately 0.3 μm shorter than the corresponding VIIRS M13 radiometer channel". This "introduces an approximately three-fold increase of the reflected solar component in the I4 channel, with potential reduction of the radiometric separation between fire-affected pixels and bright fire-free surfaces". The paper states the comparison against M13. That I4 is similarly offset from MODIS bands 21/22 (3.929–3.989 µm) follows from the band ranges and is not stated in the paper. [Schroeder et al. 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed]
- **M13 and the CO2 band.** Because M13 partly overlaps a CO2 absorption band near 4 µm, "atmospheric attenuation effects may double compared, for example, to the corresponding MODIS mid-infrared data (channels 21/22)". This "could lead to systematic underestimation of VIIRS FRP values compared to coincident MODIS data". Pixel size, aggregation and point-spread function create other, variable effects. [750 m ATBD v1.0, Sept 2017](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_750m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Band widths.** I5 spans 1.9 µm (10.5–12.4 µm), while MODIS band 31 spans 0.5 µm (10.78–11.28 µm). I4 spans 0.38 µm, while MODIS bands 21/22 span 0.06 µm. [Xiong et al. S-NPP/N-20](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190000314/downloads/20190000314.pdf), [MCST](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/calibration/information) [confirmed (arithmetic on confirmed ranges)]
- **NOAA-21 M13 is shifted.** The NOAA-21 M13 spectral response function is shifted toward shorter wavelengths compared with NOAA-20 and S-NPP. [Wang et al., AMS 2024 abstract](https://ams.confex.com/ams/104ANNUAL/webprogram/Paper429538.html), [Zhang et al. 2024](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65977) [confirmed]

### VIIRS calibration stability by unit

**S-NPP**
- After more than 6 years in orbit, the MWIR gain change was within 0.5%. LWIR changes were within 0.3%, "except for band I5 that has experienced approximately 1.8% gain degradation to date" (paper about 2018). TEB calibration is done scan by scan, so gain trends had negligible effect on SDR quality, and NEdT stayed nearly unchanged. [Xiong et al. S-NPP/N-20](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190000314/downloads/20190000314.pdf) [confirmed]
- M-band TEB long-term stability, Feb 2012 to Aug 2020, clear-sky ocean 60°S–60°N, observed minus radiative-transfer simulation: drifts "consistently less than 0.102 K/Decade" across all M TEB bands, and within 0.04 K/decade for M14 and M16. I4 and I5 were not evaluated. [Zhang et al. 2024, Remote Sensing 16(7):1271](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65977) [confirmed]
- Fire record start: S-NPP operations began 19 Jan 2012. The first hours were affected by cooling of the onboard calibration sources, so users should "adopt 20 January 2012 as the effective start date". C2 uses NASA L1B inputs that avoid the "bad scans" of the SDR inputs used for Collection 1. [375 m User's Guide v1.2, Sec. 2 and 4](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]

**NOAA-20**
- LWIR bands M15, M16 and I5 lost gain soon after launch because ice built up on the LWIR focal-plane dewar window. A mid-mission outgassing on 12 Mar 2018 restored the gains to within 0.3% of at-launch values. NEdT stayed nearly unchanged and met requirements. [Xiong et al. S-NPP/N-20](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190000314/downloads/20190000314.pdf) [confirmed]
- Since that outgassing, "all N-20 TEB responses have continued to be stable, showing nearly identical behavior to that of S-NPP TEB, including relatively large change in the I5 (0.3%/year)". [Xiong et al., "NOAA-20 VIIRS On-orbit Calibration Improvements" (NOAA IR 65873, about 2020)](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65873/noaa_65873_DS1.pdf) [corrected source: the researcher cited NTRS 20190000314, which does not contain the 0.3%/year statement]
- S-NPP vs NOAA-20 cross-calibration using GOES ABI as a transfer, for TEB M12 (3.7 µm), M14 (8.55 µm) and M15 (10.763 µm), daytime and nighttime: "very low absolute brightness temperature biases (<0.10 K) and a very small DDP (<0.03%)". The methods do not always track each other, and I4, I5 and M13 were not assessed. [Huang, Yan, Sun (NOAA IR 65872)](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65872/noaa_65872_DS1.pdf) [confirmed]

**NOAA-21**
- Event timeline: launch 10 Nov 2022; science mode 20 Nov 2022; Ka-band transmitter anomaly 16 Dec 2022, which caused a 48-day data gap; nadir door open 5 Dec 2022; cryoradiator door open 8 Feb 2023; MMOG #1 23 Feb 2023; cold focal plane set point changed from 82 K to 80 K on 3 Mar 2023; pitch manoeuvre 10 Mar 2023; MMOG #2 26 Feb 2024; spacecraft safe modes 22 Jun 2024 and 1 Oct 2025. The blackbody is nominally held at 292.5 K. [Angal et al. 2026](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260008550/downloads/SPIE26_N21TEB_Angal_revD_assubmitted.pdf), [Xiong et al. IGARSS 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230010145/downloads/2023_IGARSS_N21_VIIRS_Initial_Performance_MAN.pdf) [confirmed; Ka-band gap and BB setpoint new]
- TEB gain degraded as ice accumulated in the dewar and recovered after each MMOG. Gain is stable within each of the three periods (before MMOG #1, after #1, after #2), and NEdT for all TEB stays "well within their respective specifications" through April 2026. Non-linear coefficients from the annual WUCD for I4 and I5 (and M12–M16) agree with prelaunch values, meaning "no significant changes in detector nonlinearity". The early NOAA-21 blackbody sometimes exceeded its 30 mK uniformity requirement, especially in daytime orbits, unlike S-NPP and NOAA-20. [Angal et al. 2026](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260008550/downloads/SPIE26_N21TEB_Angal_revD_assubmitted.pdf) [confirmed; BB uniformity new]
- Early mission: TEB responses changed by less than 1.0% for all bands, mostly in the MWIR. NOAA-21 is "similar to N-20 and slightly better than S-NPP in terms of calibration stability". [Xiong et al. IGARSS 2023](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230010145/downloads/2023_IGARSS_N21_VIIRS_Initial_Performance_MAN.pdf) [confirmed]

**Between units (S-NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21)**
- Clear-sky ocean 60°S–60°N, 18 Mar to 30 Nov 2023, double differences: mean differences are within 0.08 K for all M TEBs except M13. [Zhang et al. 2024](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/65977) [confirmed (abstract)]

### Instrument effects visible in the fire products

- **Lunar calibration rolls.** These happen roughly monthly and can produce "linear patterns of large numbers of anomalous active fire detection data" in a MODIS or VIIRS swath. "NASA FIRMS makes every effort to filter and remove these anomalous detections from the data stream in real-time", though they can occasionally appear briefly. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- **Confidence schemes differ.** VIIRS 375 m low confidence means a daytime I4 anomaly below 15 K or sun-glint risk. Nominal means no daytime glint and an anomaly above 15 K, day or night. High means day or night saturated pixels. MODIS confidence is a 0–100% value mapped to three classes. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq), [375 m User's Guide v1.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]

## Why these matter for combining the records

- The MODIS fire record has hardware-driven breaks: before November 2000 (no distributed Terra CMG or location products), around the 15 June to 2 July 2001 Terra outage and the change to A-side electronics that followed (more striping in bands 21 and 22), and in the August–September 2020 Aqua telemetry outage. Short gaps such as the March 2026 Terra standby and the August 2026 S-NPP anomaly also lower monthly counts for reasons that have nothing to do with fire.
- The MODIS fire bands drifted by a few hundredths of a kelvin per year at ocean temperatures, which adds up to well under 1 K over the mission. Terra and Aqua agree to within about ±0.5 K at those temperatures. Most of the documented MODIS degradation is in bands 27–30, which the fire algorithm does not use. No source measures fire-band stability at fire temperatures.
- I4 saturates at 367 K, against about 500 K for MODIS band 21. Combined with the reflected-sunlight effect of I4's shorter wavelength, this means VIIRS 375 m detections, confidence classes and BT values are not physically equivalent to MODIS ones. The difference is largest in daytime and for intense fires.
- Within VIIRS, the three units agree closely in most bands. The documented exception is the NOAA-21 M13 offset of about 0.2 to 0.3 K, which comes from its shifted spectral response and feeds FRP. NOAA-21 also has step changes at its outgassing dates, and NOAA-20 I5 has a slow gain trend that calibration tracks.
- Both MODIS units are now near shutdown, projected for early to late 2027, in drifted orbits with smaller pixels and later or earlier overpass times. S-NPP data ended on 2 Nov 2026. The overlap years and the closing months of each record are therefore not equivalent to the middle of the record.
- MODIS L1B moved to C7 in March 2026 with TEB changes, while the fire products in FIRMS remain C6.1. Any future fire reprocessing would change the inputs to the fire algorithm.

## Conflicts and unverified items

1. **MODIS band 21 required NEdT.** MCST, the MODIS calibration team, gives 0.20 K at 335 K ([MCST calibration information](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/calibration/information)). LAADS gives 2.00 K at 335 K ([LAADS MODIS](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/modis/)). A search summary reports 0.20 K on the NASA MODIS specifications page as well, but that page reset the connection when opened. MCST is the more authoritative source because it owns the instrument calibration. Undated web tables.
2. **S-NPP end time on 2 Nov 2026.** 13:00 UTC was the planned time, in the NOAA notice of 3 Aug 2026 and the Earthdata alert updated 14 Oct 2026 ([NESDIS](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/news/cessation-of-suomi-national-polar-orbiting-partnership-s-npp-data-users-onafter-november-2-2026), [Earthdata](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/suomi-npp-observations-cease-november-2-2026)). 15:40 UTC is the actual end, per later NOAA updates relayed by the Earthdata reminder updated 10 Nov 2026 ([Earthdata reminder](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/reminder-new-suomi-npp-observations-cease-november-2-2026)) and by the FIRMS FAQ. The later, after-the-fact statements are authoritative. Earlier notices said 1 Nov 2026.
3. **Terra and Aqua end of mission.** MODIS Land (updated 13 Nov 2025) says Terra December 2025 and Aqua August 2026. NASA Science (29 Dec 2025, updated 20 Mar 2026) says end of science in February 2027 for Terra and September 2027 for Aqua. Earthdata (2 May 2025) says shutdown begins "late 2026/early 2027". The NASA Science editor's note is the newest, and both instruments were in science mode on 6 Nov 2026, so the MODIS Land dates are superseded.
4. **M13 saturation temperature.** About 659 K in the 750 m ATBD v1.0 (Sept 2017) vs 634 K in Schroeder et al. (2014). The ATBD is the later and product-specific document. Neither source explains the difference.
5. **VIIRS 11 µm saturation.** About 340 K for the planned channel (Justice et al. 2002, pre-launch) vs 380 K for the flown I5 (Schroeder et al. 2014). The 2014 value describes the actual instrument.
6. **Terra B-side switch date.** 30 Oct 2000 in the MCST event table ([Xiong et al. 2017](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20170004617/downloads/20170004617.pdf)) vs "early November 2000 (Julian day 305)", which is 31 Oct 2000, in Justice et al. (2002). This is a one-day difference; MCST's engineering log is more precise.
7. **Terra 2016 maximum gain change in PV LWIR detectors.** "Up to 25%" (MCST recovery summary, March 2016) vs "up to 18%" (Xiong et al. 2017). The two may describe different detectors or metrics.
8. **Terra altitude loss.** The researcher's "about 5 km" is not supported. The May 2025 slides say a 6 km decrease in 2022, and MODIS Land gives a 694 km exit altitude, 11 km below the 705 km design. The two statements may use different reference altitudes.
9. **NOAA-20/21 orbit altitude.** 833 km in eoPortal vs 824 km in search summaries of other secondary sites. No primary page with the altitude was opened. [unverified]
10. **S-NPP design life of 5 years.** Supported only by search summaries; the NOAA page could not be resolved. One secondary source says a 5-year mission on a 7-year design. [unverified]
11. **I5 stability across units.** S-NPP I5 lost about 1.8% gain by about 2018, and NOAA-20 I5 changes about 0.3%/yr. NOAA-21 I5 is reported stable within periods. These cover different instruments and periods, so they are not a true contradiction.
12. **Aqua band 21 drift against SST.** 0.074 K/yr, twice Terra's 0.037 K/yr, while band 22 is 0.039 vs 0.038 K/yr. Against band 31 the Aqua band 21 rate is only 0.014 K/yr. The two 3.96 µm channels on Aqua do not drift alike against the in situ reference.

## Open questions

- No source gives I4 or I5 brightness-temperature differences between S-NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21. Only M-band results (the M13 offset) and M12/M14/M15 ABI double differences were found.
- No source measures MODIS band 21/22 or VIIRS I4/M13 calibration stability at fire temperatures (above about 340 K). All stability studies found use scenes near 200–310 K. Band 21's saturation level was described as unknown in 2002, and no later MCST value was found.
- No source quantifies how instrument ageing has affected fire counts or FRP in MOD14/MYD14 or VNP14IMG. Only Level-1B stability is documented.
- No per-detector NEdT history for MODIS bands 21, 22 and 31 was found, only whole-instrument noisy-detector counts.
- Will MOD14/MYD14 be regenerated from C7 L1B, and how is forward processing of the C6.1 fire product fed now that V6.1 L1B was scheduled for decommissioning on 30 Sep 2026? No announcement was found.
- Late-life NOAA-20 TEB stability (2023–2026) and the cause of the 5–7 Nov 2026 NOAA-20/NOAA-21 data gaps are not documented in what was found.
- When exactly the Terra and Aqua MODIS instruments will stop collecting data in 2027 is not yet scheduled publicly beyond the month-level projections.
