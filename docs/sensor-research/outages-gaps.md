# Data outages, gaps and anomalies by sensor, 2000 to 2026

Verified on 2026-11-13. All dates are UTC unless stated. "DOY" means day of year (for example 2016/049 = 18 February 2016). "NRT" means near-real-time processing (LANCE/FIRMS fast stream). "Standard" means the science-quality archive stream (LAADS, LP DAAC). "L0" means Level 0 raw instrument data. Status tags: [confirmed] = the cited page supports the statement; [corrected] = the researcher's value was changed, old value given; [unverified] = not found on an opened page.

- Terra MODIS problems before November 2000: some detectors were made dead or unusable to reduce crosstalk between the 500 m and 1 km bands. This causes striping and missed small and large fires. In rare cases a severe band-21 miscalibration in February and March 2000 flags entire scan lines as fire. [User Guide sec. 7.2.1](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- The NOAA-21 product readme says the NOAA operational algorithm and output formats are the same for S-NPP, NOAA-20 and NOAA-21. The one exception is the FRP (fire radiative power) coefficient, which was changed because the NOAA-21 M13 spectral response shifted. Even so, NOAA-21 FRP shows a positive bias of up to about 10% relative to S-NPP and NOAA-20. This applies to NOAA's NDE product, not NASA's VNP14IMG/VJ114IMG/VJ214IMG. [NOAA-21 Readme](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/JPSS/documents/AMM/N21/NOAA-21_Active_Fires_Provisional_Readme.pdf) [confirmed]

### Terra and Aqua orbit drift and end of mission (status at 2026-11-13)

- Terra's last inclination adjustment was in February 2020 (LANCE says the "last mission maneuver was Feb 27, 2020"). The October 2022 constellation exit lowered Terra to 694 km. LANCE expected Terra to drift past 10:15 local time by October 2022. [LANCE Known Issues](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/knownissue) [confirmed]
- The Terra project page predicts the equator crossing time will pass 09:00 MLT (mean local time) in winter 2025/2026, followed by perigee-lowering burns of about 20 km more. It says "there has not been an official passivation date set". It does not confirm that the 09:00 milestone has passed. [Terra: A Message from the Project Scientist](https://terra.nasa.gov/about/terras-orbit-changes/terra-orbital-drift-information/a-message-from-the-project-scientist) [added, confirmed]
- The FIRMS FAQ still describes Terra crossing at about 10:30 a.m./p.m. and Aqua at about 1:30 p.m./a.m., which are the nominal values, not the current drifted ones. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/faq/firms-faq) [added, confirmed]
- Aqua's final inclination adjustment and drag make-up maneuver was in 2021. Per the May 2025 LANCE User Working Group slides, orbit drift affects diurnal sampling, pixel size (1 km to about 970 m along-scan by spring 2025), scan gaps and bowtie overlap. The slides quote Lin et al. (2023): maximal nadir scan gaps widen from about 1/2 to about 1 1/4 250 m pixels in December 2026 for Terra and July 2026 for Aqua. The slides also say effects on spatial sampling "seem to have abruptly increased in magnitude in late 2024". [Giglio, LANCE UWG May 2025](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/filePost/LANCE_UWG_Orbit_Drift_Giglio_202505.pdf) [added, confirmed]
- LANCE issue HC_MOD_26008 (opened 8 Jan 2026) says Terra and Aqua drift affects pixel size, scan gaps and diurnal sampling, with larger effects seen in late 2024. The issue is under investigation. [LANCE Known Issues](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/knownissue) [added, confirmed]
- NASA says the Terra and Aqua platforms "will begin shutting down" in late 2026 or early 2027, MODIS data collection will then stop, and a final MODIS reprocessing will follow. Issued 2 May 2025 and not updated since. No exact date was found. [Earthdata: Transition from MODIS to VIIRS](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/transition-from-modis-viirs) [added, confirmed]
- A NASA blog post dated 27 Oct 2026 says Terra currently provides the only morning overpass, about 3 hours before Aqua, and that NASA is "investigating alternatives" for the morning orbit. It says there will be no loss of afternoon-orbit capability when Aqua ends, because NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 cover it. No end dates for Terra or Aqua are given. [Earthdata blog: Transitioning from Suomi NPP VIIRS/OMPS](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/news/blog/transitioning-from-suomi-npp-viirs-omps-what-firms-users-need-know) [confirmed]

### Coverage and product-level caveats

- Consecutive MODIS swaths do not fully overlap at the equator. This leaves small diamond-shaped gaps in daily coverage between about 30° N and 30° S, which are usually filled by the next pass or another satellite. At higher latitudes swaths overlap more and revisits are more frequent. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/faq/firms-faq) [confirmed]
- The 3060 km VIIRS swath gives about 15% overlap between consecutive orbits at the equator, which provides full global coverage every 12 hours. Mid-latitudes get 3 to 4 looks a day. The VIIRS guide notes MODIS "coverage gaps along the tropics". [VIIRS C2 375 m UG v1.2, sec. 7](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [added, confirmed]
- The VIIRS user guide warns that the NRT stream can have coverage gaps from processing interruptions. It recommends the standard (non-NRT) VNP14IMG data from LP DAAC and LAADS for scientific studies. [VIIRS C2 375 m UG v1.2, sec. 4.3](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [added, confirmed]
- A bug in the Collection 2 monthly fire-location files (Vxx14IMGML) set the "Type" field inconsistently in versions 1 and 2, so static land sources were often labeled type 0 (presumed vegetation fire). The bug was fixed in May 2025 and all files were reprocessed to version 3. [VIIRS C2 375 m UG v1.2, sec. 6.1](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [added, confirmed]

## Why these matter for combining the records

Each sensor's monthly series has holes of very different sizes. Some are whole weeks with no data (Terra in June 2001 and March 2002, Aqua in August 2020 and April 2022, S-NPP in July and August 2022), and a month with a week missing will undercount fires unless the missing observation time is accounted for. Several events are listed as no-data days in the FIRMS FAQ, but others are not (Terra December 2003, Terra February 2016), so a missing-observation mask cannot be built from the FAQ list alone. Some events change calibration or sampling rather than removing data: Terra's October 2022 orbit lowering, the continuing drift of both MODIS orbits (Terra toward earlier morning crossing times; the direction for Aqua was not sourced here), the post-anomaly LUT changes on S-NPP in 2026, and the NOAA-21 M13 FRP bias of up to about 10%. These can shift detection counts or FRP without leaving a visible gap. The start dates differ between NOAA's operational products and NASA's FIRMS archive (NOAA-21: February 2023 versus 17 January 2024), so the version used fixes the overlap periods available. Late-2026 months are still being filled, and S-NPP ends on 2 November 2026, so the most recent months are provisional and partial.

## Conflicts and unverified items

1. **S-NPP end time on 2 Nov 2026:** 13:00 UTC in the [NASA blog dated 27 Oct 2026, written as a future plan](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/news/blog/transitioning-from-suomi-npp-viirs-omps-what-firms-users-need-know) versus 15:40 UTC in the [FIRMS FAQ, written in past tense ("ceased")](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/faq/firms-faq). The FAQ is more authoritative for what actually happened because it was written after the event. A 1 Nov 2026 date seen only in a search snippet was not verified.
2. **S-NPP start:** the FIRMS FAQ gives 20 Jan 2012 in its availability section and "routine production started January 19, 2012" in its VIIRS section. The [VIIRS user guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) explains the difference: first data on 19 Jan, recommended record start 20 Jan.
3. **NOAA-21 start:** NOAA's product was made from 9 Feb 2023, was reliable from 23 Feb 2023, and reached Provisional maturity effective 30 Mar 2023 ([readme](https://www.star.nesdis.noaa.gov/JPSS/documents/AMM/N21/NOAA-21_Active_Fires_Provisional_Readme.pdf)). FIRMS data start 17 Jan 2024 ([FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/faq/firms-faq)). These are different archives, and both are correct for their own archive.
4. **S-NPP July 2026 outage start:** 14:50 UTC on 10 Jul (non-nominal state, notice issued 13 Jul) versus 11:48 UTC (outage start, 27 Jul update) on the [same page](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/suomi-npp-viirs-data-outage-anomaly-july-10-2026). The later update is more likely to be the final value.
5. **S-NPP June 2026:** data were approved at 12:00 UTC on 10 Jun and the new LUTs were in NRT from 16:00 UTC on 10 Jun, but the 12 Jun update says "from June 12 onward" ([page](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/suomi-npp-viirs-data-outage-anomaly-june-1-2026)). The page does not explain the difference.
6. **S-NPP August 2026:** the excluded window is 3 Aug 02:00 to 6 Aug 16:24, yet NRT resumed with a granule from 13:54 on 4 Aug ([page](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/suomi-npp-viirs-data-outage-anomaly-august-2-2026)). This suggests NRT and standard processing excluded different periods. The anomaly began 2 Aug 02:05, but the exclusion starts 3 Aug.
7. **Terra December 2022 alert:** resumed 16 Dec 2022, but the metadata end date is 25 Dec 2022 ([LAADS 200027](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/alerts-and-issues/200027)). The update text (16 Dec) is the better guide.
8. **Terra altitude change in October 2022:** "about 5 km lower" ([LAADS 200027](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/alerts-and-issues/200027)); "6-km altitude decrease" ([Giglio 2025 slides](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/data/filePost/LANCE_UWG_Orbit_Drift_Giglio_202505.pdf)); "from 705 km to 694 km", 11 km, in the [LP DAAC/Earthdata alert](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/terra-constellation-exit-data-outage) and on the [Terra project page](https://terra.nasa.gov/about/terras-orbit-changes/terra-orbital-drift-information/a-message-from-the-project-scientist). 705 km is the nominal design altitude. By 2022 Terra was probably already below it, which would explain why the drop measured from the actual orbit is 5 to 6 km. This explanation is my inference and is not stated on any page.
9. **Terra October 2022 outage window:** 10 to 19 Oct (LP DAAC alert); return to science configuration on 21 Oct (DOY 294) and operational processing on 23 Oct (MCST/LANCE); NRT on 28 Oct (LANCE). These describe different stages, not a disagreement.
10. **Aqua return to science in April 2022:** "April 13-15" outgas to science ([MCST event page](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/news/aqua-safe-mode-event)); 14 Apr, DOY 104 ([MCST LWIR page](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/news/aqua-lwir-c61-post-safe-mode-update)); 15 Apr ([Earthdata alert](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/aqua-safe-mode-alert)). All agree that usable data start 17 Apr.
11. **Terra December 2003:** MODIS commanded to science at 22:33:57 on 22 Dec, but MCST says science operations resumed 24 Dec, when the nadir door was opened ([MCST weekly](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/iot/am1weekly2003-3542003-360)). Earth-view data need the nadir door open, so 24 Dec is the better end date. This event is not in the FIRMS FAQ no-data list.
12. **Terra March 2002 end:** FIRMS gives 28 Mar as the last no-data day. MCST shows a short return to science on 27 Mar that ended about 45 minutes later with a formatter reset. A search snippet citing a SpaceRef report said the formatter was loaded at 00:34 GMT on 28 Mar; this was not opened. [unverified]
13. **Terra February 2016 data-loss window (DOY 049 to 054/055):** inferred from the recovery timeline. No page stating the lost dates was opened. [unverified]
14. **Terra 15 Apr 2001 no-data day:** the cause was not found. [unverified]
15. **Aqua August 2020 start time:** a search snippet citing a LAADS alert gave an outage start of 09:26:40 on 16 Aug 2020 from an FMU/solid-state recorder error. That page was not opened. [unverified]
16. **S-NPP August 2026 dataset count:** 19 (researcher) versus 20 product entries on the page as fetched. [corrected]

## Open questions

- Neither the FIRMS missing-data table (linked from the FAQ, contents not rendered) nor a FIRMS-specific outage list was retrieved. It is not known whether FIRMS counts the 2003, 2016, 2020, 2022 and 2026 events as missing days.
- The data-loss windows were not found for Terra's 22 Aug 2006 door closure or Aqua's 29 Jul and 12 Sep 2002 safe holds.
- Exact Terra and Aqua shutdown dates: only "late 2026/early 2027" (May 2025 alert) was found. Whether Terra has passed 09:00 MLT, and whether Terra's perigee-lowering burns have happened, is not confirmed.
- The current Aqua equator crossing time was not found on a primary page.
- No NASA science-ready date was found for NOAA-20 375 m (VJ114IMG), nor an explanation of why FIRMS NOAA-20 starts on 1 Apr 2018.
- Whether the gaps of 27 Oct and 5 to 7 Nov 2026 (VIIRS) will be filled is still open.
- Whether the regenerated S-NPP standard data (1 May 2026 onward) in the LP DAAC/LAADS archive match what FIRMS holds for May to October 2026 is unknown.
- Any 2013 to 2021 S-NPP or 2018 to 2023 NOAA-20 outages beyond those listed were not searched systematically. The NASA alert archive and NOAA OSPO message archive are the places to check.
