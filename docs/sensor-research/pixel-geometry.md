# Pixel footprint, detection counts and geolocation: MODIS versus VIIRS

Verification date: 2026-11-13. Every source below was opened during verification; PDF text was extracted and searched directly rather than relying on the researcher's quotes. Status tags: [confirmed] = the page supports the claim as stated; [corrected] = the claim needed a change (old and new value given); [unverified] = could not be confirmed on an opened page.

Terms used:
- **Nadir**: the point directly below the satellite. **Scan angle**: the angle of the line of sight away from nadir.
- **Along-scan** (FIRMS "scan" column): the cross-track direction, roughly east-west. **Along-track** (FIRMS "track" column): the direction of flight, roughly north-south.
- **Bow-tie effect**: because pixels grow along-track towards the swath edge, pixels from consecutive scans overlap there, so the same ground is seen twice.
- **Fire pixel / hotspot / detection**: one pixel that the algorithm flags as containing at least one fire or other thermal anomaly.

## Key facts

### What a detection means

- A FIRMS hotspot is the centre point of a pixel flagged as containing one or more fires or other thermal anomalies. The pixel is about 1 km for MODIS and about 375 m for VIIRS, and the location is "the center point of the pixel (not necessarily the coordinates of the actual fire)". [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- FIRMS attribute table: MODIS latitude/longitude is the "Center of 1 km fire pixel, but not necessarily the actual location of the fire"; VIIRS latitude/longitude is the "Center of nominal 375 m fire pixel". [FIRMS attributes](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/active-fire-data-attributes-modis-viirs) [confirmed]
- A detection says nothing direct about fire size. The FIRMS FAQ (wildfire perimeter entry) says a small intense fire filling part of the pixel and a broad cooler fire filling most of it are both reported as one fire pixel at the data's resolution (375 m VIIRS, 1 km MODIS), so cumulative detections typically overestimate wildfire size and do not align with perimeters. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- MODIS C6/C6.1 User's Guide (v1.0, May 2021), section 8.3.2: MODIS routinely detects flaming and smouldering fires of about 1000 m²; under very good conditions (near nadir, little or no smoke, homogeneous surface) flaming fires one tenth that size (about 100 m²); under pristine, extremely rare conditions flaming fires of about 50 m². There is no upper limit to the largest or hottest fire that can be detected. Section 8.3.3: a fire may be "too small or too cool to be detected in the 1 km2 MODIS footprint". [MODIS C6/C6.1 User's Guide](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- The VIIRS C2 375 m guide (v1.2, June 2025) says that, because of its higher spatial resolution, the 375 m product (VNP14IMG) "provides much higher sensitivity to fires of relatively small size, as well as improved mapping of large fire perimeters", whereas the VIIRS 750 m product (VNP14) performs more like the 1 km Aqua MODIS product (MYD14). [VIIRS C2 375 m User's Guide v1.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- The same guide says none of the 375 m channels were designed for fire detection, and the I4 mid-infrared channel saturates "quite often"; a saturated pixel is assigned the nominal saturation temperature of 367 K. [VIIRS C2 guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]

### Pixel footprint across the scan

MODIS pixel-size formulas (User's Guide section 8.4.6, reproduced from Ichoku and Kaufman 2005): along-scan ΔS = Re·s·(cos θ / √((Re/r)² − sin² θ) − 1) and along-track ΔT = r·s·(cos θ − √((Re/r)² − sin² θ)), with Re = 6378.137 km, r = Re + h, h = 705 km, s = 0.0014184397 rad, and θ = s × (sample − 676.5). Pixel area = ΔS × ΔT. The guide says the area "is nominally 1 km2 but grows away from nadir". The MCD14ML sample column runs 0 to 1353 (section 8.7.5, Table 10). [MODIS guide](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]

VIIRS I-band (375 m) footprint (Schroeder et al. 2014): native resolution before onboard aggregation about 125 × 375 m at nadir. Aggregation: 3 native samples per output sample from 0° to ±31.72°, 2 from ±31.72° to ±44.86°, 1 from ±44.86° to ±56.28°; the three sections hold 1184, 736 and 1280 samples (6400 per scan line). Effective footprint ranges from 383 × 360 m at nadir to 795 × 784 m at the maximum scan angle of 56.28°. [Schroeder et al. 2014](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed]

- Sayer et al. (2015), an independent primary paper on MODIS geometry: edge pixels are elongated both along-track and across-track, "resulting in an increase of pixel area by up to a factor of ∼9" and in overlap of pixels from consecutive scans. The swath is about 2330 km "despite the fact that there are only 1354 across-track" pixels; each scan is about 10 km wide at nadir, with 10 detectors for the nominal 1 km bands. [Sayer et al. 2015](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/8/5277/2015/amt-8-5277-2015.pdf) [confirmed]
- Sayer et al. (2015) also note that MODIS pixel spatial responses are not exactly square and blur slightly into adjacent pixels; Terra band 8 (412 nm, nominally 1 km, not a fire band) had an effective size of about 1.08 km across-track by 1.01 km along-track before launch. [Sayer et al. 2015](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/8/5277/2015/amt-8-5277-2015.pdf) [confirmed]
- Wolfe et al. (2013): the VIIRS 3×1, 2×1 and 1×1 aggregation limits pixel growth in the scan direction to about the same rate as in the track direction, "a maximum size of about 2 times nadir at the end of the scan". Nominal resolution at nadir is 375 m (I-bands) and 750 m (M-bands). [Wolfe et al. 2013](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140010285/downloads/20140010285.pdf) [confirmed]
- FIRMS FAQ: VIIRS 375 m data are a nominal resolution after onboard aggregation in three regions: nadir to 31.59° (three native pixels per sample), 31.59° to 44.68° (two), and 44.68° to 56.06° at the swath edge (one). [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed; boundaries differ slightly from Schroeder 2014, see Conflicts]
- FIRMS FAQ: "While MODIS pixels expand significantly at swath edges, VIIRS maintains a much more consistent pixel size across its entire 3,040 km field of view." It also describes a MODIS pixel as 1 km × 1 km at nadir that stretches to cover a larger area at the swath edge. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]

### Scan and track columns in FIRMS

- FIRMS FAQ: "The scan value represents the spatial-resolution in the East-West direction of the scan and the track value represents the North-South spatial resolution of the scan." Pixels at the eastern and western scan edges are bigger than 1 km; "It is 1 km only along the nadir"; scan and track "represent the actual spatial resolution of the scanned pixel". [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- FIRMS attributes page: MODIS "pixels get bigger toward the edge of scan. Scan and track reflect actual pixel size"; VIIRS "The algorithm produces approximately 375 m pixels at nadir. Scan and track reflect actual pixel size". Neither page gives a numeric range. [FIRMS attributes](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/active-fire-data-attributes-modis-viirs) [confirmed]
- Expected range (derived, not printed): MODIS scan about 1.0 to 4.8 km and track about 1.0 to 2.0 km; VIIRS 375 m scan and track about 0.36-0.38 km at nadir to about 0.78-0.80 km at the edge. The units in FIRMS are km. Actual FIRMS CSV values were not inspected because downloading data was outside this task. [MODIS guide](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf), [Schroeder 2014](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [unverified against real files]
- The MODIS MCD14ML file itself has no scan/track columns; it has a `sample` column (0-1353) from which scan angle and pixel size follow by the guide's formulas (section 8.7.5). FIRMS adds scan and track. [MODIS guide Table 10](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- The VIIRS monthly CSV (VNP14IMGML / VJ114IMGML / VJ214IMGML, collection 02, product version 03) carries `Line`, `Sample` (0-6399), `VZA` (view zenith angle, degrees), `PixArea` (I-band fire pixel area, km²), `Type` (0 presumed vegetation fire, 1 active volcano, 2 other static land source, 3 offshore including all detections over water), `DNFlag` and `Confidence` (L/N/H). The swath Level 2 file (VNP14IMG) holds sparse arrays such as `FP_line` (0 to 32·N − 1, N scans per granule) and `FP_sample` (0-6399). [VIIRS C2 guide sections 3.1.2, 3.2.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [corrected: the researcher placed PixArea, VZA, Type, DNFlag and Confidence in "Table 3 sparse arrays"; they are fields of the monthly ML CSV (section 3.2.2), while Table 3 holds FP_line, FP_sample and the other FP_ arrays]
- Known issue (C2 guide section 6.1): a bug in the Collection 2 VxxIMGML software set the `Type` field inconsistently in product versions 1 and 2, often labelling obvious type-2 (static land source) hotspots as type-0 (vegetation fire). It was fixed in May 2025 and all VxxIMGML files were reprocessed to version 3. [VIIRS C2 guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed; added]
- Confidence attribute: MODIS is a number from 0 to 100% based on intermediate algorithm quantities. VIIRS is a class: low (daytime pixels typically in sun glint with an I4 temperature anomaly below 15 K; at night low confidence occurs only in a defined South Atlantic region), nominal (glint-free by day, anomaly above 15 K by day or night), high (day or night saturated pixels). [FIRMS attributes](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/active-fire-data-attributes-modis-viirs), [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]

### One fire, several pixels, and duplicates

- MODIS triangular along-scan response: the MODIS fire ATBD (v2.3, October 2006) states that "Due to the triangular along-scan response of the MODIS instrument (Kaufman et al., 1998b), the two along-scan pixels adjacent to the potential fire pixel are deemed unreliable and excluded from the background characterization." [MOD14 ATBD v2.3](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/87/MOD14_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed; added, was an open question]
- Ellison and Ichoku (accessory publication, GSFC) describe "the MODIS triangular response function that reaches halfway into the neighboring pixels along-scan", so a fire's signal spreads into the adjacent along-scan pixels. This explains why one fire can produce detections in two neighbouring MODIS pixels, but neither document quantifies how often that produces duplicate fire pixels. [Ellison and Ichoku accessory publication](https://www.firescience.gov/ords/prd/jf_jfsp/file/getfile/PRODUCTS/32970) [confirmed for the response shape; the duplicate rate is unverified]
- The same ATBD says that during background characterization "an optimized nearest-neighbor search is used to correct for the ``bowtie'' effect, or overlap between MODIS scans". This handles overlap when building the background window; it does not remove duplicate fire pixels from the output. [MOD14 ATBD v2.3](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/87/MOD14_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed; added]
- Csiszar et al. (2014): a "prominent bow tie effect is embedded in the MODIS fire pixel statistics artificially inflating the number of off-nadir fire pixels due to oversampling by adjacent scan lines. The onboard processing of VIIRS bow-tie pixels produces fewer fire pixel duplicates compared to MODIS." The VIIRS count curve versus scan angle has discontinuities at the aggregation switchover angles because pixel size changes there. [Csiszar et al. 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Csiszar_et_al_2014_JGR.pdf) [confirmed; switchover discontinuity added]
- Sayer et al. (2015): consecutive MODIS scans overlap near the swath edges because of the along-track growth. [Sayer 2015](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/8/5277/2015/amt-8-5277-2015.pdf) [confirmed]. No MODIS onboard bow-tie deletion is described in any of the MODIS documents opened. [unverified as a negative statement, from the absence of any mention]
- VIIRS bow-tie deletion (Schroeder et al. 2014): for the I-bands, the four outermost sample rows of each scan (two at each end) are replaced with fill values in the second (2:1) section, and the eight outermost rows (four at each end) in the third (1:1) section. This gives "approximately 50% reduction in data redundancy" in the SDR and a striped fill pattern. Wolfe et al. (2013) Figure 3 gives the same totals (four I-band detectors in the 2-sample zone, eight in the no-aggregation zone). [Schroeder 2014](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf), [Wolfe 2013](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140010285/downloads/20140010285.pdf) [confirmed]
- VIIRS C2 guide section 3.1.2: fire-mask class 1 marks bow-tie-deleted pixels; "residual bowtie pixels are still present in the regular input files". Fire pixels found in residual bow-tie data are output and flagged in algorithm QA bit 22, which "should be used when emissions estimates based on FRP data are calculated". [VIIRS C2 guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [corrected: the researcher cited the C2 guide as confirming the row counts and the 50% figure; the C2 guide confirms class 1 and QA bit 22 only, and the row counts and 50% come from Schroeder 2014]
- Note: the QA bit 22 flag is in the Level 2 swath file. The FIRMS/ML CSV field list does not include it, so residual bow-tie duplicates cannot be removed from the CSV by that flag. (Inference from the field lists in the C2 guide.) [VIIRS C2 guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed field list; the consequence is an inference]
- FIRMS FAQ on ML files: FIRMS uses the monthly science-quality "ML" ASCII files (for example VNP14IMGML, VJ114IMGML for NOAA-20, VJ214IMGML for NOAA-21, and MCD14ML for MODIS), available "with a 2-3 month lag". It says "the standard Level 2G products are better suited to quantitative analysis" and "account for overlapping satellite orbits to avoid double-counting fire detections". The ML files are not described as removing duplicates. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- Schroeder et al. (2014): one 750 m VIIRS pixel overlaps four (2 × 2) 375 m pixels. Over global S-NPP data from 1 to 30 August 2013, the 375 m algorithm gave a three-fold increase in daytime fire pixels and a 25-fold increase in nighttime fire pixels compared with the operational 750 m Active Fires ARP (IDPS). Coincident detections were 43% (375 m) and 83% (750 m) of pixels by day, rising to 90% of 750 m pixels when 375 m low-confidence pixels were included, and 10% and 98% at night. At night, multiple contiguous 375 m fire pixels overlapped smaller 750 m clusters that often contained fewer than two pixels. [Schroeder 2014](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [corrected: the researcher called the data "Collection 1-era"; it is a pre-release prototype run on August 2013 SDR data, before any NASA Collection 1 product. Daytime coincidence figures added.]
- VIIRS C2 guide (one year, 2021, global, Collection 2): VNP14IMG detected about 3× more daytime and about 17× more nighttime fire pixels than VNP14. By day, 53% of VNP14IMG pixels had no VNP14 match and 11% of VNP14 pixels had no VNP14IMG match (some in sun-glint areas, because the 375 m glint tests are more conservative). At night, 85% of VNP14IMG pixels had no VNP14 match, and 0.8% of VNP14 pixels had no VNP14IMG match. The guide says these figures are "very broadly representative" and vary with sample size and area. [VIIRS C2 guide section 7](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]

### Why VIIRS reports more fires, and night performance

- FIRMS FAQ: the 375 m data complement MODIS, "show good agreement in hotspot detection", respond more strongly to fires of relatively small area, map large fire perimeters better, and have "improved nighttime performance". On 375 m versus 750 m: "Because of its improved spatial resolution, the 375 m algorithm will tend to detect more fire pixels", a difference "particularly pronounced during the nighttime part of the orbit when the occurrence of smaller/cooler fires will favor the 375 m product". [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- The VIIRS C2 guide words the night explanation differently: the difference is pronounced at night "when the enhanced ability to detect fires in general (compared to daytime detection) will favor the VNP14IMG product". [VIIRS C2 guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed; added]
- VIIRS C2 guide: differences between the 375 m VNP14IMG and 1 km Aqua MODIS products are "further magnified due to MODIS coverage gaps along the tropics and the sampling characteristics (specifically, pixel enlargement away from nadir) of the MODIS sensor". Figure 4 (Lower Mekong, 1 March 2016) lists daytime counts of 606 (Terra MODIS, 04:05 UTC), 9,643 (S-NPP VIIRS, 06:20 UTC) and 361 (Aqua MODIS, 07:10 UTC), and nighttime counts of 58 (Terra, 14:55 UTC), 4,608 (S-NPP, 18:55 UTC) and 22 (Aqua, 19:20 UTC). This is one day in one region. [VIIRS C2 guide Figure 4](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- Csiszar et al. (2014), comparing the VIIRS **750 m** product with Aqua MODIS over global data from 15 February to 15 June 2013 (nearly one million VIIRS and over half a million MODIS fire pixels): VIIRS gave about 26% more detections in the 3-pixel aggregation zone (about ±31°) and 70% more in the 2- and 1-pixel zones, "due to the more limited increase of VIIRS pixel size with scan angle compared to MODIS". MODIS counts form a bell-shaped curve against scan angle because pixel size increases and detection rates fall towards high scan angles. In the first month of data (January-February 2012) near-nadir counts were anomalously similar because of an aggregation bug, fixed in IDPS Mx5.3 on 3 April 2012. [Csiszar 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Csiszar_et_al_2014_JGR.pdf) [confirmed; bug-fix date added]

(MCST, 18 Nov 2019) conclusion: MODIS band B1 mean errors near 0 and uncertainties about 50 m at nadir, "Mostly within 20% band B1 HSI (250 m) = 50 m @ nadir (75 m for VIIRS I1)"; within 10% for 500 m bands and 5% for 1 km bands. Scan-to-scan underlaps exist around nadir near the equator, up to about 150 m for Aqua MODIS band 2 near 15° N, and "Lowering orbit by 4 km or more will increase underlaps after A-Train exit". [Lin et al. 2019](https://mcst.gsfc.nasa.gov/sites/default/files/meetings_files/06_MODIS_GEO_Lin.pdf) [confirmed; underlap points added]
- Relative to pixel size, both sensors' geolocation errors (about 50 m MODIS, 55-75 m VIIRS) are small: about 5% of a 1 km MODIS fire pixel and about 15-20% of a 375 m VIIRS pixel at nadir. (Arithmetic.) [derived, confirmed]
- VIIRS terrain correction (Wolfe 2013): I- and M-band geolocation is provided both on the ellipsoid and terrain-corrected; the Day/Night Band (DNB) only on the ellipsoid. Without terrain correction the scan-direction error is elevation times a factor that reaches 2.75 at the end of scan; Mount Everest (8.85 km) viewed at the end of scan gives a 24.3 km difference (the terrain-corrected location is closer to nadir). The geoid can differ from the ellipsoid by up to 107 m, which could give about 300 m error at the end of scan. Schroeder et al. (2014) state the 375 m fire algorithm uses the terrain-corrected pixel-level geolocation file. [Wolfe 2013](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140010285/downloads/20140010285.pdf), [Schroeder 2014](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed]
- NASA VIIRS Level 1 geolocation products (VJ103IMG, VJ203MOD) use "an Earth ellipsoid, geoid, and a digital terrain model" and are titled "Terrain-Corrected Geolocation". [LAADS VJ103IMG](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/VJ103IMG), [LAADS VJ203MOD](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/VJ203MOD) [confirmed; added]
- MODIS MOD03/MYD03 geolocation fields are computed from spacecraft attitude and orbit, instrument telemetry and a digital elevation model, and the file contains ground elevation. The LAADS page gives no accuracy number. MOD14/MYD14 take MOD03/MYD03 as geolocation input (MODIS guide). [LAADS MOD03](https://ladsweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/missions-and-measurements/products/MOD03/), [MODIS guide](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- FIRMS FAQ, NRT versus standard: most of the time the additional error in reported NRT fire location for MODIS and VIIRS is under 100 m. After spacecraft manoeuvres or severe space weather, errors can reach several kilometres; the degraded period is typically under 2 hours for MODIS/Terra, VIIRS/NOAA-20 and VIIRS/NOAA-21, but up to 12 hours for MODIS/Aqua. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]. The researcher's added statement that standard processing "removes data degraded by maneuvers" was not re-found in the passages read. [unverified]
- Plume parallax (VIIRS C2 guide FAQ): at night, for very large, explosively growing fires with tall superheated plumes, viewed at high scan angle, the plume detection is displaced laterally when projected to the surface; "Displaced pixels will be located on the fire perimeter's side further away from the image center and closer to the swath's edge." The algorithm cannot separate these from surface fire pixels; users should look for observations closer to nadir. The FIRMS FAQ repeats this. [VIIRS C2 guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf), [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]

### Status in 2025-2026 that changes geometry or counts

- **Suomi NPP ended.** FIRMS FAQ: production and delivery of new Suomi NPP data "ceased at 15:40 Universal Time (UTC) on November 2, 2026"; VIIRS S-NPP data in FIRMS run from 20 January 2012 to 2 November 2026. Banner: "Suomi NPP is no longer delivering new data." [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]. Earlier notices gave 13:00 UTC; see Conflicts.
- **FIRMS availability start dates:** MODIS Terra from 1 November 2000; MODIS Aqua from July 2002; VIIRS NOAA-20 from 1 April 2018; VIIRS NOAA-21 from 17 January 2024. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- **MODIS orbit drift alters pixel geometry.** NASA LDOPE issue "MODIS - Terra/Aqua Drift" (opened 8 January 2026): Terra completed its final inclination adjustment and drag make-up manoeuvres in 2022 and Aqua in 2021; "orbit drift has impacted MODIS data, affecting pixel size, spacing, scan gaps, bowtie overlap, and diurnal cycle sampling", with "widened scan gaps and altered spatial sampling patterns, with significant impacts observed in late 2024". "The effects on MODIS fire products and broader datasets are under active investigation." [LDOPE issue 1254](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/displayissue?id=1254) [confirmed; added]. Note: the h = 705 km in the MODIS guide's pixel-size formula is the nominal altitude; with a lower, drifting orbit the formula's footprints are approximations. (Inference.)
- **Terra and Aqua end of operations.** Earthdata notice (2 May 2025): Terra and Aqua are expected to begin shutting down "In late 2026/early 2027", ending MODIS data collection, followed by a final MODIS reprocessing; "Both platforms are currently drifting out of their designed orbits, impacting crossing times and possibly impacting data." [Earthdata MODIS-to-VIIRS transition notice](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/transition-from-modis-viirs) [confirmed; added]. Search results summarised other pages as giving February 2027 (Terra) and September 2027 (Aqua) end-of-science dates; those pages were not opened. [unverified]

## Why these matter for combining the records

A FIRMS hotspot count is a count of flagged pixels, not of fires or burned area. A MODIS pixel covers about 7 times the ground area of a VIIRS 375 m pixel at nadir and about 15 times at the swath edge, so one fire front that MODIS reports as one or two pixels can appear as many contiguous VIIRS pixels. Because the MODIS footprint grows about 9.7-fold from nadir to edge while the VIIRS footprint grows about 4.5-fold, the ratio between the two sensors' counts depends on where in the swath a fire was seen, and the scan and track columns (or VIIRS PixArea and MODIS sample) carry the information needed to describe that. MODIS counts off nadir are inflated by bow-tie oversampling from adjacent scans and can include a neighbour pixel because of the triangular along-scan response, while VIIRS deletes most bow-tie overlap on board but still keeps residual bow-tie pixels; the monthly ML/FIRMS files remove neither. Day and night counts behave differently, especially for VIIRS, whose night gain over coarser products is much larger than its day gain. Geolocation errors are small compared with either pixel, so position mismatch matters far less than footprint size. The MODIS record's geometry has itself changed since about 2022-2024 because of orbit drift, and the S-NPP record stopped on 2 November 2026, so the satellite mix contributing to any month changes over time.

## Conflicts and unverified items

1. **VIIRS swath width.** 3,040 km (FIRMS FAQ, current page) versus about 3060 km (VIIRS C2 375 m guide v1.2, June 2025) versus about 3000 km (Schroeder et al. 2014). All are rounded descriptions of the same 6400-sample swath; the C2 guide is the most specific product document. Sources: [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq), [C2 guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf), [Schroeder 2014](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf).
2. **VIIRS aggregation boundaries and maximum scan angle.** FIRMS FAQ 31.59° / 44.68° / 56.06°; Schroeder et al. 2014 31.72° / 44.86° / 56.28°; Csiszar et al. 2014 31.58° / 44.6° (abstract "approximately ±31°"). None states the definition used (scan angle at the instrument versus view zenith angle at the ground could explain about 0.1-0.2°; that is an inference). Schroeder 2014 is the peer-reviewed product-defining paper. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq), [Schroeder 2014](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf), [Csiszar 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Csiszar_et_al_2014_JGR.pdf).
3. **Night gain of 375 m over 750 m VIIRS.** 25-fold (Schroeder 2014, global August 2013, prototype algorithm versus IDPS 750 m ARP) versus about 17-fold (C2 guide, global 2021, Collection 2 VNP14IMG versus VNP14). Day is about 3-fold in both. Different algorithm versions and years; the C2 figure is current for the archive. Neither compares with MODIS.
4. **Aqua MODIS geolocation.** Wolfe 2013 Table 2 (column "MODIS Aqua (C6)") 46 m track / 51 m scan; Lin 2019 46 / 54 m (C6) and 46 / 53 m (C6.1). The difference is in the scan direction only (3 m) and comes from different data periods (3,539 versus 6,310 data days). Lin 2019 is newer and longer. The researcher's note that the Wolfe column header was ambiguous is resolved: it is clearly labelled.
5. **Suomi NPP cessation time.** NESDIS notice issued 3 August 2026 (URL slug "november-01-2026", body now "November 2, 2026 at 1300 UTC") and the Earthdata alert issued 7 August 2026: 13:00 UTC on 2 November 2026. A search snippet attributed 13:00 UTC on 1 November 2026 to the original 3 August notice; the page as opened now says 2 November. Later NESDIS sidebar entries and the FIRMS FAQ: 15:40 UTC on 2 November 2026. The 15:40 UTC value is later and appears to be the actual cut-off; the 13:00 values were planned times "subject to change". [NESDIS Nov-01 page](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/news/cessation-of-suomi-national-polar-orbiting-partnership-s-npp-data-users-onafter-november-01-2026), [NESDIS Nov-2 page](https://www.nesdis.noaa.gov/news/cessation-of-suomi-national-polar-orbiting-partnership-s-npp-data-users-onafter-november-2-2026), [Earthdata alert](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/suomi-npp-observations-cease-november-2-2026), [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq).
6. **S-NPP start date.** FIRMS FAQ gives 20 January 2012 (availability list) and says routine production "started January 19, 2012" in another entry. Wolfe 2013 reprocessed geolocation from 19 January 2012. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq), [Wolfe 2013](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140010285/downloads/20140010285.pdf).
7. **MODIS edge area growth.** "Up to a factor of ∼9" (Sayer 2015, general MODIS geometry) versus 9.66 km² from the fire guide formula at sample 0/1353. Consistent within rounding.
8. **Final inclination manoeuvres.** LDOPE (January 2026) says Terra's final IAM and drag make-up were in 2022; a search-result summary said Terra stopped inclination adjustments in 2020. Only the LDOPE page was opened. [LDOPE issue 1254](https://landweb.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/displayissue?id=1254).
9. **Unverified:** VIIRS footprint at 31.7° and 44.9° (not printed in opened sources); real scan/track ranges in FIRMS CSVs; the rate at which the MODIS triangular response produces duplicate fire pixels; the statement that standard processing removes manoeuvre-degraded data; Terra and Aqua end-of-science dates in 2027.
