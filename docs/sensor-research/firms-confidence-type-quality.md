# Confidence, type flags, false alarms and quality filtering in FIRMS data

Verified 2026-11-13. Abbreviations: **FIRMS** = NASA Fire Information for Resource Management System. **NRT** = near-real-time data (LANCE, roughly 3 h latency). **Standard / ML** = the science-quality archive and its monthly fire-location text files: MCD14ML for MODIS and Vxx14IMGML for VIIRS 375 m, where xx = NP (S-NPP), J1 (NOAA-20) or J2 (NOAA-21). **I4** = VIIRS 3.74 µm mid-infrared imagery band (375 m). **BT4, BT5** = I4 and I5 brightness temperatures. **ΔBT45** = BT4 − BT5. **SAMA** = South Atlantic Magnetic Anomaly. **SZA** = solar zenith angle.

Sources used most often:
- MODIS guide: Giglio, Schroeder, Hall and Justice, *MODIS Collection 6 and Collection 6.1 Active Fire Product User's Guide*, version 1.0, May 2021.
- VIIRS guide: Schroeder, Giglio and Hall, *Collection 2 VIIRS 375-m Active Fire Product User's Guide*, version 1.2, June 2025.

I read the full text of both PDFs.

| Nominal | 8 / "N" | "free of potential Sun glint contamination during the day, and marked by strong (>15K) temperature anomaly in either day or nighttime data" | [confirmed] [source](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) |
| High | 9 / "H" | "associated with day or nighttime saturated pixels, including nominal saturation and digital number (DN) folding" | [confirmed] [source](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) |

- The FIRMS attribute page and the Earthdata VIIRS 375 m data page give the same three definitions, but they say only "sun glint" for the low class and leave out "or water pixels". Neither page gives numeric MODIS class cut-offs. The MODIS entry says only that values range "between 0 and 100% and are assigned one of the three fire classes". Sources: [FIRMS attributes](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/active-fire-data-attributes-modis-viirs), [VIIRS 375 m data page](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/data/instruments/viirs/viirs-i-band-375-m-active-fire-data) [confirmed]
- **FIRMS FAQ caution (corrected).** The FAQ says: "The confidence field should be used with caution as it is likely that it will vary in terms of meaning in different parts of the world." The researcher wrote that the meaning varies "between sensors/products". The FAQ actually speaks of variation **between regions of the world**. That MODIS and VIIRS confidence sit on different scales follows from the definitions above, not from this sentence. The same FAQ answer says: "For VIIRS, the confidence values are set to low, nominal, and high" and "based on a collection of intermediate algorithm quantities used in the detection process." Source: [FIRMS FAQ, "What is the detection confidence?"](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [corrected]
- **VIIRS algorithm QA bits 16 and 17 (daytime only).** Bit 16, pixel saturation: BT5 ≥ 325 K, OR BT4 = 367 K, OR ΔBT45 < 0. Bit 17, glint: ΔBT45 ≤ 30 K, OR glint angle θg < 15°. Source: [VIIRS guide Table 2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Accuracy of the original 375 m algorithm** (S-NPP, 2014 paper, the earlier algorithm version, not Collection 2):
  - Commission error for nominal-confidence pixels was "small, although variable (<1.2%)".
  - In eastern China, daytime false alarms reached 1.2%, mostly on reflective industrial rooftops.
  - Globally, daytime urban false alarms for nominal confidence were estimated at 0.03%.
  - On average, 6% of low-confidence daytime pixels were linked to urban areas and bright rooftops.
  - Nighttime commission errors outside the SAMA region were "considered negligible".
  - Source: [Schroeder et al. 2014, RSE 143:85–96](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed] (added)

### South Atlantic Magnetic Anomaly (VIIRS I4)

- **What the anomaly does.** The SAMA produces artificially high nighttime I4 brightness temperatures that are 15–30 K above the background. These pixels carry nominal Level 1 quality flags, so they cannot be removed with the quality flags. The I5 band is not affected. The area most affected spans about 110°W to 11°E and 7°N to 55°S. In August 2013 data, low-confidence SAMA pixels were about 5% of all nighttime fire pixels detected globally. Source: [Schroeder et al. 2014 §2.1, §4](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed] (added)
- **Collection 2 handling.** Low-confidence pixels over water or linked to the SAMA "are marked with unique quality flags in the 'algorithm QA' SDS (Table 2, bits 18-21)". In Table 2, bit 18 = potential SAMA pixel, bit 19 = fire pixel over water, and bits 20–21 = persistence tests. The guide says: "Those occurrences are predominantly linked to spurious detections although some verifiable fires may be mixed in." Those pixels "are assigned a corresponding land or water class in the 'fire mask' SDS and removed from the sparse array SDSs". Source: [VIIRS guide §3.1.2, Table 2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- **FIRMS FAQ ("Are those few isolated fire pixels in the middle of the South Atlantic Ocean real?").** These are "typically associated with spurious fire detections due to the South Atlantic Magnetic Anomaly". The 375 m algorithm "contains a specific filter to flag those occurrences as low confidence detections". **Added by the verifier:** "in some cases (average 2-3 pixels every night) the spurious signal … is confused for a regular fire pixel and therefore assigned a nominal confidence flag". Verified true positives occur over South Atlantic waters off southeast Brazil and west Africa, where oil rigs operate. Source: [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- **FIRMS attribute page.** Low-confidence nighttime pixels occur only in the SAMA region. "These have been removed from the NRT data distributed by FIRMS." Source: [FIRMS attributes](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/active-fire-data-attributes-modis-viirs) [confirmed]
- **Detections the filter wrongly removes.** The SAMA nighttime filter caused confirmed omissions of small, low-intensity fires, for example known inland gas flares. Source: [Schroeder et al. 2014](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed] (added)

### Documented sources of false alarms and non-fire detections

- **What a hotspot can be.** The FIRMS FAQ answers "Are all active fire hotspots vegetation fires?" with "No." It continues: "In most cases hotspots/fires are vegetation fires, but sometimes a hotspot/fire is a volcanic eruption or the flare from a gas well." Elsewhere the FAQ lists static thermal anomalies that "can include petrochemical and cement production, gas flares and volcanoes". Source: [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- **MODIS Collection 6 rejection tests.** The guide says: "Additional specialized tests are used to eliminate false detections caused by sun glint, desert boundaries, errors in the water mask, and small forest clearings." Collection 6 changes included "Reduced incidence of false alarms caused by small forest clearings" and "Expanded sun-glint rejection". Source: [MODIS guide §8.3.1, §2.2](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Collection 5 forest-clearing false alarms.** Schroeder et al. (2008) found persistent false alarms in small Amazon rainforest clearings in Collection 5 Terra data. The example is dated 23 May 2002. Collection 6 added a rejection test for this. Source: [MODIS guide §7.3.1](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Collection 5 calibration-maneuver arcs.** A Level 1B bug during lunar roll maneuvers produced "spurious stripes of fire pixels across the entire swath in up to ∼20 scans". Some of these reached the Collection 5 CMG and MCD14ML products; an example is Aqua on 8 December 2008 at 22:35 UTC. The bug was fixed in late 2009, and the corrected code is used for Collection 6. Source: [MODIS guide §7.3.2](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Offshore detections from Collection 6 onward.** Collection 6 processes oceans and large water bodies "to detect offshore gas flaring", so cloud, unknown and fire classes can now occur over water. To separate land from water, use bits 0–1 of the algorithm QA layer. Before Collection 6 the fire mask could serve as a rough land/water mask. Source: [MODIS guide §5.1.2, §2.2](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Terra before November 2000.** Dead detectors caused striping, undetected small fires and undetected large fires. Severe band-21 miscalibration in February–March 2000 can make whole scan lines appear as fire. NASA does not distribute the CMG and fire-location (MCD14ML) products for the period before November 2000. Source: [MODIS guide §7.2.1, §8.3.10](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **VIIRS sun glint.** The FIRMS FAQ says areas of strong solar reflectance from sun glint, "such as metallic rooftops or highly reflective desert, could see a few 750 m fire pixels without a corresponding 375 m fire detection". It attributes this to "the relatively shorter wavelength of the 375 m mid-infrared channel … which will experience greater influence of the solar component". The VIIRS guide separately says the extra 750 m-only daytime pixels are partly "concentrated in sun glint areas due to the more conservative glint rejection tests used in the VNP14IMG algorithm". The researcher's wording ("VIIRS and MODIS algorithms use slightly more conservative sun-glint tests") was not found on either page. Sources: [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq); [VIIRS guide §7](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [corrected]
- **VIIRS Collection 1 to Collection 2.** Collection 2 uses NASA Level-1B inputs that "do not suffer from the frequent occurrence of spuriously calibrated 'bad scans' that plagued" the Collection 1 SDR inputs. New Level-1B quality flags "virtually eliminate the spurious detections that were common for Collection 1". Source: [VIIRS guide §2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed] (added)
- **VIIRS residual bowtie pixels.** Redundant pixels near the swath edge can be output as fire pixels and are flagged by QA bit 22. NASA says to use this flag in FRP emissions estimates to avoid double counting. Source: [VIIRS guide §3.1.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed] (added)
- **VIIRS nighttime plume parallax.** Over very large, explosively growing wildfires with tall plumes, nighttime detections at high scan angle can be displaced outside the fire perimeter. The algorithm "is not currently able to distinguish nighttime surface fire pixels from the isolated plume detections". Source: [VIIRS guide §7](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- **VIIRS I4 saturation ("quite often").** There are three scenarios:
  1. Nominal saturation, where the pixel is set to 367 K.
  2. DN folding, which gives an abnormally low temperature that can be identified using I5. The 2014 paper reports 208 K, the bottom of the I4 range.
  3. Mixing of saturated and unsaturated native pixels during onboard aggregation, with no Level 1 quality flag.

  These scenarios are believed to have a "small/negligible effect on the fire detection performance" but are "a major factor limiting the retrieval of sub-pixel fire characteristics (FRP) using the I4 channel". Sources: [VIIRS guide §7](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf); [Schroeder 2014 §2.1](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/imported/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed]

### Cloud and smoke obscuration, and "no detection is not no fire"

- **FIRMS FAQ.** "Cloud cover, heavy smoke, or tree canopy may completely obscure a fire", and the hotspot data "do not provide any information on cloud cover or missing data". Source: [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- **MODIS guide on fire-location files.** Known fires missing from the fire-location files "are not necessarily missed by the detection algorithm. Cloud obscuration, a lack of coverage, or a misclassification in the land/sea mask may instead be responsible, but with only the information provided in the fire location files this will be impossible to determine." The guide also says: "We urge caution in using fire pixel locations in lieu of the 1-km gridded MODIS fire products". Source: [MODIS guide §7.1.1](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Time binning.** "Severe temporal and spatial biases may arise in any MODIS fire time series analysis employing time intervals shorter than about eight days." A gridded product built from MCD14ML "carelessly" risks "severe temporal and spatial biases". MCD14ML files typically contain 200,000–500,000 lines per month. Source: [MODIS guide §7.1.1, §8.7.1–8.7.2](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Why MODIS misses fires.** The fire may start and end between overpasses. It may be too small or too cool for the 1 km² footprint. It may be hidden by cloud, heavy smoke or tree canopy. The instrument may be inoperable, for example Terra MODIS in September 2000. Understory burns: "unknown but probably very low". Unexposed coal seam fires: "In general, no." Source: [MODIS guide §8.3.3–8.3.5](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **MODIS minimum fire size.** About 1,000 m² is detected routinely, flaming or smoldering. Under very good conditions (near nadir, little smoke, homogeneous surface), flaming fires one tenth that size are detectable. About 50 m² is detectable under "pristine (and extremely rare)" conditions. There is no upper limit. Source: [MODIS guide §8.3.2](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Aqua outage.** A formatter-multiplexer unit (FMU) failure on 16 August 2020 stopped regular science telemetry for about two weeks (16 August to 2 September 2020). Only limited Direct Broadcast coverage exists for that period. Source: [MODIS guide §7.2.4, Fig. 11](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **VIIRS NRT gaps.** The VIIRS guide warns NRT users "about possible coverage gaps resulting from temporary interruptions in the NRT data processing chain". It says "Latency-insensitive applications demanding higher consistency (e.g., scientific studies) should favor the non-NRT VNP14IMG and VNP14 data". Source: [VIIRS guide §4.3](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed] (added)

### Hotspot versus fire, and counts across sensors and products

- **What a hotspot is.** The FIRMS FAQ says "Each hotspot/active fire detection represents the center of a pixel flagged as containing one or more fires". The location "is the center point of the pixel (not necessarily the coordinates of the actual fire)", and "at least one fire is located within the flagged pixel". It adds: "The fire is often less than the size of the pixel"; "Sometimes you will see several active fires in a line. This generally represents a fire front"; and cumulative detection data can lead to "overestimating the size of a wildfire". **No FAQ answer gives the number of hotspots per fire.** Source: [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]
- **Burned area from fire pixels.** The MODIS guide says estimating burned area from fire pixels to acceptable accuracy "is generally not possible due to nontrivial spatial and temporal sampling issues", and that the effective area burned per pixel "is not simply a constant". Source: [MODIS guide §8.3.9](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2023-09/MODIS_C6_C6.1_Fire_User_Guide_1.0.pdf) [confirmed]
- **VIIRS 375 m vs 750 m, Collection 2, global, year 2021.** NASA calls these figures "very broadly representative" and says they "will vary with the sample size and geographic area". Source: [VIIRS guide §7](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]

- **The same comparison in 2014 (earlier algorithm, Collection 1-era S-NPP data, compared with the IDPS 750 m product).** The 375 m product had about 3× more pixels by day and about **25×** more by night. ## Why these matter for combining the records

- **Confidence is not on a common scale.** MODIS confidence is a 0–100% value that can be re-thresholded. VIIRS confidence is a three-level class driven by sun glint, anomaly size and saturation. A filter on "low" removes different kinds of pixels from each record. MODIS confidence semantics also changed between Collections 4, 5 and 6, but C6 and C6.1 fire products are consistent with each other.
- **The type field depends on the data stream.** It exists only in the Standard monthly files, not in NRT. For VIIRS it was unreliable in ML versions 1–2 until the May 2025 reprocessing to version 3. Static-source screening therefore depends on which stream and file version a record came from.
- **Some filtering is regional or time-limited.** VIIRS removes most low-confidence SAMA and over-water pixels from its fire-pixel arrays, and FIRMS also removes them from NRT. MODIS has no equivalent SAMA filter, so the South Atlantic sector is filtered differently. Offshore MODIS detections exist only from Collection 6 onward.
- **Absent detections are not zeros.** Point files carry no cloud or missing-data information. Documented gaps include Terra before November 2000 and in September 2000, and Aqua from 16 August to 2 September 2020. These appear as low counts rather than as missing data.
- **Day and night are defined differently.** VIIRS uses different detection thresholds by day and night, and its advantage over 750 m and MODIS is largest at night. FIRMS NRT and Standard data define day and night differently (orbit direction versus SZA 85°), so day/night splits can differ near the terminator and at high latitudes.
- **A hotspot is a pixel, not a fire.** NASA gives no hotspots-per-fire factor. Published count ratios depend on product, year, region and day/night: about 3× by day and 17× by night for 375 m vs 750 m in 2021, versus 25× at night in 2014.
- **The record is changing in 2026.** S-NPP production ended on 2 November 2026, and Terra and Aqua are drifting and scheduled to end in late 2026 to early 2027. The satellite mix behind any monthly total is changing during this period.

## Conflicts and unverified items

1. **Scope of the Type field.** The FIRMS attributes page lists Type under MODIS only, "available only for MCD14ML" ([source](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/active-fire-data-attributes-modis-viirs), undated web page). The VIIRS guide v1.2 (June 2025) documents a Type field with the same 0–3 values in Vxx14IMGML ([source](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf)). The guide is more authoritative about the product contents because it is written by the product's science team. The FIRMS page most likely describes what FIRMS itself serves. Both agree that NRT lacks Type.
2. **Removal of SAMA pixels.**
   - FIRMS attributes page: low-confidence nighttime SAMA pixels "have been removed from the NRT data distributed by FIRMS".
   - VIIRS guide: low-confidence SAMA and water pixels are reclassified as land or water and removed from the sparse arrays of the Collection 2 product itself.
   - FIRMS FAQ: they are "flagged as low confidence", but on average 2–3 spurious pixels a night are still assigned nominal confidence and so remain.

   These statements are compatible but scoped differently. Taken together: most SAMA artifacts are removed in both Standard and NRT data, but some nominal-confidence residuals remain.
3. **Definition of VIIRS low confidence.** The VIIRS guide says "Sun glint or water pixels". The FIRMS pages say "sun glint" only. The guide is the primary source.
4. **VIIRS swath.** The FIRMS FAQ gives 3,040 km. The VIIRS guide gives about 3,060 km. Schroeder et al. 2014 gives about 3,000 km (6,400 pixels). All three are approximate. The guide's 6400 × 32-row granule description is the most specific.
5. **375 m vs 750 m nighttime ratio.** It is about 17× in the VIIRS guide (Collection 2, global 2021 data) and about 25× in Schroeder et al. 2014 (initial algorithm vs the IDPS 750 m product, data from about 2013). The 2021 Collection 2 value is the current one. The ratio varies with algorithm version, input data and sample.
6. **S-NPP cessation time.** The FIRMS blog of 27 October 2026 gave the planned time as 13:00 UTC on 2 November 2026. The FIRMS FAQ, after the event, gives 15:40 UTC on 2 November 2026. The later FAQ value is the actual time. The Earthdata VIIRS 375 m page still says end of life was anticipated "on or before October 2026", which is out of date.
7. **FIRMS FAQ "confidence caution" (corrected).** The researcher said the meaning varies between sensors. The FAQ says it varies "in different parts of the world".
8. **Glint wording (corrected).** The researcher attributed "slightly more conservative sun-glint tests" to the FIRMS FAQ for both VIIRS and MODIS. The FAQ attributes the 750 m-only glint detections to the shorter wavelength of the 375 m mid-infrared channel. The VIIRS guide attributes them to "more conservative glint rejection tests used in the VNP14IMG algorithm".
9. **Section number (minor correction).** The VIIRS confidence and SAMA text is in §3.1.2 "Data layers", not §3.1.1.
10. **Unverified wording.** I could not read word for word, through the summarizing fetch tool, the list of FRP comparison factors in the FIRMS FAQ (researcher: "sensor spatial resolution, saturation temperature of thermal bands and satellite view zenith angle"). The FAQ text I could see mentions pixel growth with view zenith angle, band saturation and sub-pixel fire position. Mark this as low confidence until it is checked on the page.

## Open questions

- **How VIIRS ML types 1 and 2 are assigned.** I found no documentation of the rules for type 1 (active volcano) and type 2 (other static land source) in the Vxx14IMGML files (persistence window, volcano database, urban mask). For MODIS the rule is 16 or more days in a calendar year plus MCD12Q1 urban areas.
- **MODIS day vs night.** The MODIS user guide does not quantify differences in detection rate or thresholds between day and night. The Collection 6 algorithm paper (Giglio et al. 2016, RSE 178:31–41) was not opened.
- **Coverage of NASA guidance.** The FIRMS FAQ gives no hotspots-per-fire factor and no NASA guidance on combining MODIS and VIIRS counts into one series. I found nothing to the contrary on the pages I read.
- **MODIS active fire end-of-mission notice.** I found no FIRMS-specific notice (as of 2026-11-13) on when Terra and Aqua MODIS active fire NRT and MCD14ML production will end, or on the effect of orbit drift on overpass times and detection.
- **VIIRS 750 m confidence.** It is not clear whether the VIIRS 750 m (VNP14) confidence uses MODIS-style 0–100% values. The 750 m user guide (Giglio et al. 2024) was not opened.
- **Effect of the VIIRS ML version 3 reprocessing.** The change in type-0 counts from the May 2025 version 3 reprocessing of Vxx14IMGML is not quantified in the guide.
