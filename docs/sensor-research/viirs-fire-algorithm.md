# VIIRS active fire algorithms and products (375 m and 750 m)

Verified 2026-11-13. Each item ends with its source link and a status tag. [confirmed] means the opened page supports the claim as written. [corrected] means the researcher's value or attribution was wrong or incomplete and is fixed here. [unverified] means the claim could not be confirmed on an opened page.

Terms used below:
- **I-bands** are the five VIIRS channels at 375 m nominal resolution (I1 to I5). **M-bands** are the 16 channels at 750 m (M1 to M16).
- **BT4** and **BT5** are the brightness temperatures of I4 and I5. **ΔBT45** = BT4 − BT5. **ρi** is the top-of-atmosphere reflectance in channel Ii. **QF4** and **QF5** are the Level-1 quality flags of I4 and I5.
- Subscript **B** means a statistic of the valid background pixels around the candidate pixel (mean), and **δ** means mean absolute deviation.
- **VNP** = Suomi NPP (S-NPP), **VJ1** = NOAA-20 (formerly JPSS-1), **VJ2** = NOAA-21 (formerly JPSS-2).
- **SAA / SAMA** = South Atlantic (Magnetic) Anomaly.
- **FRP** = fire radiative power, in megawatts (MW).

Main sources: the Collection 2 375 m User's Guide v1.2 (June 2025, "C2 guide"), the 375 m ATBD v1.0 (December 2016), Schroeder et al. 2014 (Remote Sensing of Environment 143:85-96), the 375 m and 750 m User's Guide v1.4 (June 2018, "C1 guide"), the 750 m ATBD v1.0 (September 2017), and NASA FIRMS, LANCE, LP DAAC and NOAA pages.

### Inputs and outline of the 375 m detection algorithm

| Channel | Resolution | Band (µm) | Use in the 375 m algorithm |
|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | 375 m | 0.60–0.68 | Cloud and water classification |
| I2 | 375 m | 0.846–0.885 | Cloud and water classification |
| I3 | 375 m | 1.58–1.64 | Water classification |
| I4 | 375 m | 3.55–3.93 | Fire detection |
| I5 | 375 m | 10.5–12.4 | Fire detection and cloud classification |
| M13 (dual gain) | 750 m | 3.973–4.128 | FRP retrieval, fire detection over water and across the SAA region (both aggregated 750×750 m and un-aggregated 250×750 m data are used) |

Source: [375 m ATBD Table 1](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]

- The algorithm combines fixed-threshold and contextual tests, mainly on I4 and I5, and runs on both day and night data. Night is defined as solar zenith angle ≥ 90°. [375 m ATBD §2.3](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Cloud mask (deliberately liberal, to avoid missing fires under thin cloud).** Day: BT5 < 265 K, OR (ρ1+ρ2 > 0.9 AND BT5 < 295 K), OR (ρ1+ρ2 > 0.7 AND BT5 < 285 K). Night: BT5 < 265 K AND BT4 < 295 K. Cloud pixels are skipped and excluded from the background. [ATBD §2.3.1](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Water.** Daytime internal water test ρ1 > ρ2 > ρ3, combined with a static land-water mask built on the MODIS 250 m water product. All water pixels are still processed so that gas flares and other thermal anomalies over water can be detected. [ATBD §2.3.1](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Fixed-threshold ("high confidence") tests.** Unsaturated night: BT4 > 320 K AND QF4 = 0. Saturated day or night: BT4 = 367 K AND QF4 = 9 AND QF5 = 0, plus BT5 > 290 K AND ρ1+ρ2 < 0.7 by day. I4 folding: ΔBT45 < 0 AND BT5 > 325 K AND QF5 = 0 (day); ΔBT45 < 0 AND BT5 > 310 K AND QF5 = 0 (night); or BT4 = 208 K AND BT5 > 335 K (night). [ATBD §2.3.2](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Potential background fire pixels** (masked out of background statistics): BT4 > 335 K AND ΔBT45 > 30 K (day); BT4 > 300 K AND ΔBT45 > 10 K (night); plus pixels with folded I4. [ATBD §2.3.3](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed; added]
- **Candidate fire pixels.** Day: BT4 > BT4S AND ΔBT45 > 25 K. Night: BT4 > 295 K AND ΔBT45 > 10 K (fixed). BT4S = MIN[330, MAX[325, M+25]] K, where M is the median BT4 in a 501×501 pixel window (excluding cloud, water, background fires and non-zero quality flags). BT4S is set to 330 K if the window has fewer than 10 valid pixels. The day candidate threshold therefore ranges from 325 K to 330 K. The stated intent is better sensitivity in colder high-latitude regions and fewer false alarms over warmer low-latitude backgrounds. [ATBD §2.3.5](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]. The QA bit 10 description in both the ATBD and the C2 guide prints the day threshold simply as "BT4 > 325 K". [C2 guide Table 2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Contextual tests ("nominal confidence").** The background window grows from 11×11 to 31×31 pixels until ≥ 25% or ≥ 10 valid pixels are found; land candidates use land background and water candidates use water background. Pixels without enough background become "unclassified" (class 6). Day: ΔBT45 > ΔBT45B + 2·δ45B AND ΔBT45 > ΔBT45B + 10 K AND BT4 > BT4B + 3.5·δ4B AND (BT5 > BT5B + δ5B − 4 K OR δ′4B > 5 K). Night: ΔBT45 > ΔBT45B + 3·δ45B AND ΔBT45 > ΔBT45B + 9 K AND BT4 > BT4B + 3·δ4B. [ATBD §2.3.6](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]. The product field FP_WinSize has a stated range of 10–35 in both the C1 and C2 guides. [C2 guide Table 3](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Extra day test in fire-dense windows.** Day candidates with ≥ 4 background fire pixels (or background fires > 10% of valid background) are rejected if ρ2 > 0.15 AND BT′4B < 345 K AND δ′4B < 3 K AND BT4 < BT′4B + 6·δ′4B. [ATBD §2.3.6](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed; added]
- **Secondary tests ("low confidence").** (i) Pixels with BT5 ≥ 325 K OR BT4 = 355 K OR ΔBT45 < 0 K that have at least one adjacent nominal or high pixel become low confidence. (ii) Nominal pixels with ΔBT45 ≤ 30 K OR glint angle < 15° become low confidence if ≥ 2 adjacent sun-glint pixels exist, or if no adjacent high pixel exists and BT4 is less than 15 K above the adjacent pixels. [ATBD §2.3.7](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]. Note that the ATBD text uses BT4 = 355 K here, while QA bit 16 in the ATBD and C2 guide uses BT4 = 367 K (see Conflicts).

Sources: [C2 guide Table 1](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf); [C1 guide Table 4](https://LPDAAC.USGS.GOV/documents/427/VNP14_User_Guide_V1.pdf) [confirmed]

- In the 375 m sparse arrays FP_confidence is 7, 8 or 9; in the monthly CSV it is "L", "N" or "H". [C2 guide Table 3 and §3.2.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]. In the 750 m VNP14 sparse arrays FP_confidence is 0–100 (as in MODIS), and the mask classes 7–9 follow a heuristic confidence measure. [C1 guide §2.3](https://LPDAAC.USGS.GOV/documents/427/VNP14_User_Guide_V1.pdf) [confirmed]
- **What the 375 m classes mean (user guide).** Low (7): daytime pixels typically linked to sun glint or water, with a relative I4 anomaly < 15 K. Nominal (8): free of potential daytime sun glint and with a strong (> 15 K) anomaly, day or night. High (9): saturated pixels, day or night, including nominal saturation and DN folding. [C2 guide §3.1.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]. In the algorithm, high comes from the fixed tests, nominal from the contextual tests, and low from the secondary downgrades. [ATBD §2.3.2, 2.3.6, 2.3.7](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- **SAA and water low-confidence pixels in Collection 2.** Low-confidence pixels over water or linked to the SAA are flagged in algorithm QA bits 18–21, reassigned to the land or water class in the fire mask, and removed from the sparse arrays. [C2 guide §3.1.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- **FIRMS definition.** FIRMS defines low, nominal and high in the same way. It states that low-confidence nighttime pixels occur only from 11°E to 110°W and 7°N to 55°S and "have been removed from the NRT data distributed by FIRMS". [FIRMS attributes page](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/active-fire-data-attributes-modis-viirs) [corrected: the researcher said this FIRMS page also notes the Collection 2 SAA handling. It does not; the page mentions only VIIRS Collection 1. The Collection 2 handling is stated in the C2 guide.]

### I4 saturation and digital-number folding

- I4 is a single-gain channel with a nominal saturation brightness temperature of 367 K. I5 is single-gain with saturation at 380 K. [Schroeder et al. 2014 §2](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed; I5 value added]
- Three saturation scenarios: (1) nominal saturation, assigned 367 K; (2) DN folding, where a fire far beyond the I4 range wraps the digital number and gives an abnormally low BT4 at or near the 208 K low end, identified with I5; (3) native saturated and unsaturated pixels mixed during onboard aggregation, giving spurious Level-1 radiances with nominal quality flags. The guide says these have "small/negligible" effect on detection but are "a major factor limiting" sub-pixel (FRP) retrieval with I4. [C2 guide §7 FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf); [ATBD §2.2](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- Measured on S-NPP, 1–30 August 2013: nominal I4 saturation in ≈ 8% of daytime fire pixels (0.02% on I5); night I4 saturation ≈ 1% (negligible on I5); I4 folding in 0.2% of fire pixels (none on I5); suspected saturated/unsaturated mixing in 0.5% of day and 0.04% of night fire pixels. Because of this, the 2014 paper deferred FRP, size and temperature retrieval pending use of M13. [Schroeder et al. 2014 §4](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed]
- The ATBD gives an effective I4 saturation of ≈ 358 K and an overall discernible fire-pixel saturation rate of ≈ 9% across all three scenarios. [ATBD §2.2](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]

### Fire radiative power (FRP)

- FRP is computed from co-located 750 m M13 radiances, not from I4. The method follows Wooster et al. (2003): FRP = A·σ·(L13 − L13B)/a, assuming unit atmospheric transmittance and surface emissivity. A is the pixel area (varies with scan angle), σ = 5.67×10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴, a = 2.88×10⁻⁹ W m⁻² sr⁻¹ µm⁻¹ K⁻⁴ for M13, L13 and L13B are the fire-pixel and mean background M13 radiances. [ATBD §2.3.10](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- One 750 m FRP value is split equally among the coincident 375 m fire pixels, so those pixels carry the same value (for two pixels, FRPi = FRP ÷ 2). FRP is set to zero if M13 saturates, and can be null when background data are insufficient; both are described as infrequent. [C2 guide §3.1.3](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- Units: MW. Level-2 field FP_power (typical range > 0 to 1500 MW); monthly CSV field FRP. M13 radiance fields are FP_Rad13 and FP_MeanRad13 in W m⁻² sr⁻¹ µm⁻¹ (the ATBD calls them FP_M13 and FP_MeanM13). [C2 guide Table 3 and §3.2.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf); [ATBD §3.1.2](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- M13 saturation temperature: ≈ 659 K in the 375 m ATBD (2016) and the 750 m ATBD (2017; compared with 500 K for MODIS channel 21); 634 K in Schroeder et al. 2014. [ATBD §2.2](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf); [750 m ATBD §2.2](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_750m_ATBD.pdf); [Schroeder et al. 2014](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [corrected: the researcher gave "634-659 K" without attribution; the two values come from different documents.]
- FIRMS says that "starting in 2015 the algorithm incorporated additional VIIRS channel M13 ... 750 m data" for FRP. No opened page names the product version or exact date. [FIRMS attributes page](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/active-fire-data-attributes-modis-viirs); [FIRMS VIIRS 375 m page](https://earthdata.nasa.gov/data/instruments/viirs/viirs-i-band-375-m-active-fire-data) [confirmed]
- The 375 m (M13-based) FRP agrees with the 750 m product FRP at R² = 0.99, with the 750 m values ≈ 1% lower. Because M13 partly overlaps a 4 µm CO₂ absorption band, atmospheric attenuation "may double" relative to MODIS channels 21/22, which "could lead to systematic underestimation of VIIRS FRP values compared to coincident MODIS data". Pixel size and geometry, aggregation and the point spread function also affect FRP. [ATBD §4.3](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]

### Detection limits

- Theoretical 50% probability-of-detection curves were simulated for fire areas of 2–250 m² and temperatures of 400–1200 K (2 m² and 10 K steps), inserted into real backgrounds from 10 day and 10 night S-NPP granules from August 2013, using 10 near-nadir pixels per image. Night performance is better because night backgrounds are more homogeneous. The numeric curve values appear only in a figure (ATBD Figure 5; paper Figure 4). [ATBD §4.1](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- A test bonfire of 1.25 m radius at ≈ 1000 K near Rio de Janeiro on 8 July 2013 (VIIRS overpass 1:23 am local time) was detected at night at a 32.7° scan angle, giving a +10 K increase over the I4 background. [Schroeder et al. 2014 §4](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed; date and place added]
- The ATBD notes that intense fires (> 1000 K) covering as little as 10⁻⁴ of a pixel may be detected by the 4 µm channels over cool (≤ 300 K) backgrounds. [ATBD §2.1](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed; added]
- The FIRMS FAQ gives minimum sizes only for MODIS: flaming and smoldering fires of 1000 m² routinely; flaming fires one-tenth that size under very good conditions; 50 m² under pristine, extremely rare conditions. It gives no minimum size for VIIRS and says only that the 375 m product "provides a greater response over fires of relatively small areas". [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) [confirmed]

### Known artifacts

- **South Atlantic Anomaly.** This noise produces spurious, mostly nighttime, high I4 brightness temperatures over roughly 110°W–11°E and 7°N–55°S; daytime cases are sparse. Affected I4 pixels typically depart from the background by 15–30 K and carry nominal quality flags. No discernible effect on night I5 was found. Mitigation: night fire pixels are checked against a max-aggregated M13 array, and the coincident M13 pixel must be ≥ 2 K warmer than all adjacent M13 pixels. Pixels that fail are reset to land or water and flagged (QA bit 18). [ATBD §2.2 and §2.3.8](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]. In the August 2013 sample, SAA low-confidence pixels were ≈ 5% of all nighttime fire pixels globally. The filter caused occasional omission of small, low-intensity fires and of some known inland gas flares, and about 3 suspicious nominal nighttime pixels per day remained. [Schroeder et al. 2014 §4](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Persistence test over water.** Low, nominal and high pixels over water must show an M13 signal at least 2.5 K above adjacent M13 pixels, or have ≥ 3 co-located detections in the previous 30 days. Otherwise they are downgraded to fire-free and flagged (QA bits 19–21). [ATBD §2.3.9](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]. In the monthly CSV, Type 3 = "offshore detection (includes all detections over water)". [C2 guide §3.2.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Sun glint and bright surfaces (day).** Glint angle θg is computed from the view and solar zenith angles and the relative azimuth. A pixel is classed as sun glint (class 2, not a candidate) if θg < 15° AND ρ1+ρ2 > 0.35, OR θg < 25° AND ρ1+ρ2 > 0.4. Bright-surface rejection: ρ3 > 0.3 AND ρ3 > ρ2 AND ρ2 > 0.25 AND BT4 ≤ 335 K. Named false-alarm sources are "large metallic rooftops in industrial parks", small water bodies and other bright urban surfaces. [ATBD §2.3.4](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Commission error (August 2013, S-NPP).** Nominal-confidence daytime detections had a 1.2% commission rate over eastern China, the worst of 12 sampled 10°×10° regions. Urban-driven commission for nominal daytime pixels was ≈ 0.03% globally. Night commission outside the SAA region was negligible. Low-confidence daytime pixels were ≈ 11% of daytime detections; on average 6% of them were linked to urban settings and bright rooftops, and confirmed false alarms among them peaked near 40% over eastern China. [Schroeder et al. 2014 §4](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf) [confirmed]. The ATBD summarises this as < 1.2% for nominal/high pixels and low-confidence pixels as ≈ 10% of "all global fire pixels". [ATBD §4.3](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Bow-tie.** Class 1 marks redundant samples near the swath edge that are deleted onboard. Residual bow-tie pixels are still present in the input and processed. Fire pixels at those positions appear in the fire mask (classes 7–9) and sparse arrays and are flagged in QA bit 22, which the guide says to use to avoid double-counting FRP. [C2 guide §3.1.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Pixel aggregation zones.** Three native pixels are aggregated along scan from nadir to 31.59° scan angle, two from 31.59° to 44.68°, and one from 44.68° to the swath edge at 56.06°. I-bands are aggregated onboard; M13 is aggregated on the ground. The same zones apply to M-bands. [ATBD §2.2](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf); [750 m ATBD §2.2](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_750m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]. The monthly CSV gives each fire pixel's area in km² (PixArea). [C2 guide §3.2.2](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]
- **Plume detections.** At night and at high scan angle, very large wildfires with tall, hot plumes can produce 375 m detections displaced outside the fire perimeter by parallax, towards the swath edge. The algorithm cannot tell these apart from surface fire pixels. [C2 guide §7 FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]

### Coverage and sampling

- The VIIRS swath is ≈ 3060 km wide, giving ≈ 15% overlap between consecutive orbits at the equator and full global coverage every 12 h. S-NPP's nominal equator-crossing times are 1:30 pm and 1:30 am, and mid-latitudes get 3–4 looks a day. [C2 guide §7 FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf) [confirmed]. The ATBD says "every 12 h or less depending on the latitude" and gives S-NPP's altitude as 829 km, sun-synchronous. [ATBD §1](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf) [confirmed]
- NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 fly in the same orbital plane as S-NPP, about 50 minutes apart. ### Collections, versions and file formats

- **Collection 1 to Collection 2 (375 m).** Three changes: (1) inputs are the Collection 2 NASA Level-1B radiance and geolocation, replacing the SDR inputs that had frequent spuriously calibrated "bad scans"; (2) new Collection 2 L1B quality flags are used to "virtually eliminate the spurious detections that were common for Collection 1"; (3) files are much smaller thanks to better NetCDF compression. ## Why these matter for combining the records

- The VIIRS 375 m record in FIRMS comes from three separate processing streams (S-NPP to 2 November 2026, NOAA-20 from 2018, NOAA-21 from 2024). They start on different dates in the archive and in FIRMS, so the overlap between satellites, and the months with only one or two VIIRS sensors, depend on the dates in the table above.
- The 375 m product detects about 3 times more fire pixels by day and 17 to 25 times more by night than the 750 m VIIRS product, which itself performs about like Aqua MODIS. Pairing MODIS 1 km with VIIRS 375 m therefore mixes very different sensitivities, especially at night.
- VIIRS 375 m confidence has three classes, and "high" means saturated pixels, not "most certain". MODIS and VIIRS 750 m confidence is 0 to 100. The two scales do not map one-to-one.
- 375 m FRP is a 750 m M13 value split among 375 m pixels, using a band that sees stronger CO₂ absorption than MODIS bands 21/22. FRP sums are therefore not equivalent to MODIS FRP sums pixel by pixel, and residual bow-tie duplicates (QA bit 22) can double-count FRP.
- Day candidate thresholds depend on background temperature (325 to 330 K), and the SAA filter and its omissions apply only between about 7°N and 55°S and 110°W and 11°E. Detection rates therefore vary with latitude, season and region.
- Defects differ by collection and version: Collection 1 had bad-scan artifacts, ML versions 1 and 2 mislabelled static sources, and NRT and standard data use different processing. Mixing them changes counts.
- The day/night split is at solar zenith 90° for the 375 m product and 85° for the 750 m product (MODIS-style), which affects any day/night stratification.

The guide range is likely a loose "typical range", but this was not confirmed.
9. **Night 375 m / 750 m ratio.** 25× (2014, against the IDPS 750 m product), up to 20× (C1, 90 days), ≈ 17× (C2, 2021). These differ by period and by version of the 750 m product, not as errors.
10. **Low-confidence share.** ≈ 11% of daytime detections ([paper](https://directreadout.sci.gsfc.nasa.gov/publications_documents/Schroeder_et_al_2014b_RSE.pdf)) versus ≈ 10% of "all global fire pixels" ([ATBD](https://viirsland.gsfc.nasa.gov/PDF/VIIRS_activefire_375m_ATBD.pdf)). The paper is the primary measurement.
11. **Sub-pixel characterization.** The [LANCE NRT page](https://modaps.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/services/about/products/viirs-land-c2-nrt/vj214imgtdl_nrt.html) says "detection information only with no sub-pixel fire characterization" and also defines FRP in MW. The guides and ATBD document M13-based FRP, so the "no sub-pixel" sentence appears to be outdated text from before 2015.
12. **Channels named in the FIRMS FAQ.** One passage says the VIIRS fire product uses I3 and I4 plus a 750 m band, with M11 co-located with the 375 m pixels. Another says the algorithm uses M13 and M15. [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq). The 375 m ATBD lists I1–I5 and M13. The 750 m ATBD lists M5, M7, M11, M13, M15 and M16. The FAQ passages appear to mix the two products, and the ATBDs are authoritative.
13. **FIRMS version codes.** The [FIRMS attributes page](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/active-fire-data-attributes-modis-viirs) lists only Collection 1 codes for VIIRS ("1.0NRT", "1.0"). The [LANCE page](https://modaps.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov/services/about/products/viirs-land-c2-nrt/vj214imgtdl_nrt.html), under its "viirs-land-c2-nrt" path, adds "2.0URT" and "2.0RT". Whether current FIRMS NRT records carry "2.0NRT" or "1.0NRT" was not confirmed on an opened page.
14. **Monthly ML filename.** The convention is written "Vxx14IMGML.YYYMM.CC.VV.csv", with YYYMM a typo for YYYYMM and CC = "02". The example session in the same guide fetches files named "VNP14IMGML.201801.C2.03.csv.gz", that is with "C2" and gzip compression. [C2 guide §3.2.1 and §4.2.1](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-06/VIIRS_C2_AF-375m_User_Guide_1.2.pdf)
15. **Commission statement.** The researcher wrote "under 1.2% overall". The paper reports 1.2% as the eastern China nominal-daytime rate, the highest regional value. The ATBD paraphrases this as "lower than 1.2% for nominal/high confidence pixels for all sites".
16. **FIRMS latency.** The researcher gave URT as about 1 minute. The [FIRMS FAQ](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/tools/firms/faq) says within 5 minutes.
17. **Unverified:** the geolocation product names VNP03MODLL and VJ103MODLL (only VJ203MODLL was seen); the FIRMS blog about Terra being the only morning overpass; the NOAA-21 recovery at 08:04 UTC on 18 September 2025 and the end of the 1 October 2025 safe-mode outage; the [GOFC 2024 presentation](https://gofcgold.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/Giglio_GOFC_2024_NASA_Fire.pdf), shown in search results as saying a Collection 3 maintenance release combining S-NPP and NOAA-20 is planned (the PDF failed with a certificate error).

## Open questions

- Which product version or date first added M13-based FRP to the 375 m product? FIRMS says only "starting in 2015", and the ATBD v1.0 is dated December 2016. Do early S-NPP records (2012 to about 2015) carry FRP in the reprocessed Collection 2 archive?
- Are the NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 Collection 2 processing code and thresholds identical to S-NPP's? The C2 guide promises platform caveats "as necessary" but lists none.
- What are the numeric 50% detection areas (m²) by temperature, day and night? They appear only in ATBD Figure 5 and paper Figure 4.
- The Collection 2 750 m User's Guide (Giglio et al. 2024) was not found online in this pass. The 750 m Collection 2 changes are not sourced here beyond the 2017 ATBD and 2018 C1 guide.
- Does FIRMS serve standard (SP) Collection 2 data for NOAA-20 and NOAA-21, from what dates, and when do standard records replace NRT? The FIRMS FAQ only says NRT replaces URT, and standard data lag by 2–3 months.
- Is a Collection 3 VIIRS fire release planned, and when? The GOFC 2024 slides could not be opened.
- How does NOAA's own operational VIIRS I-band active fire product (NOAA OSPO/NDE "efire") relate to NASA's VNP14IMG? The NOAA page timed out (HTTP 504). FIRMS uses the NASA LANCE product.
- When did the NOAA-21 safe-mode outage that began on 1 October 2025 end, and how much fire data was lost? Will the November 5–7, 2026 NOAA-20/NOAA-21 gaps be filled?
- Any satellite-specific known issues for VJ114IMG or VJ214IMG fire detection: the LP DAAC pages point to the MODIS/VIIRS Land Quality Assessment site, which was not opened.
